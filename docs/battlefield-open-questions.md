# Battlefields: open questions

These are places where the period books' research disagrees with a battlefield's data, and where
the rule for settling them did not settle them. The rule, set on 24 October 2026: **an official
history wins, unless it is a single report; where there is not enough data, note it.** Each item
here is either a dispute between sources with no official history to decide it, or a point that
rests on a single report. The data stands as it is until better evidence turns up. Each book's
battle history gives the sources under *Where the accounts disagree*.

## Black-powder wars
- **Lützen.** Army sizes: Roberts gives 18,996 Swedes and 16,770 Imperialists; the field has about 15,700 and 14,100. The windmill battery: 7 guns here, 14 by Niderost. No official history.
- **Cowpens.** The militia line: 900 here; Bearss (NPS) about 300 in Pickens' line, the NPS elsewhere almost 500, Babits about 1,000. No source gives the 07:45 about-face or "within musket range in under three minutes".
- **Fetterman.** Which nation held which side of the trap (Wikipedia has the Cheyenne and Arapaho west, the field has them east). Whether the cavalry or the infantry was caught first (Ambrose against Smith, the fort's history and Vaughn).
- **Wagon Box.** The start: 09:15 by Powell's report (kept); about 07:00 by Gibson, 07:30 by Wikipedia.
- **The Great Redan, 8 September.** The distance crossed (200 yards here; nearly 300, or 400 m). The abatis: one eyewitness says the bombardment had torn it up; the map still draws it.

## The World Wars
- **Mons.** Which Fusilier company held the Nimy road bridge (B Company here; Ashburner's by visitmons; two platoons under Forster by Kendall).
- **Snipe.** Capitano Preve's attack (late morning by Miskimon, 13:00 here); whether Gruppe Stiffelmayer was German or mixed; the withdrawal (21:00 by the New Zealand official history, which the field does not show).
- **Cisterna.** The 715th Motorized Infantry Division found the Rangers in the night (Laurie, CMH), but no source gives its strength or where it stood, so it is not on the map.
- **Cassino, February.** Paratroops or Baade's 90th Panzer Grenadier Division on Point 593: Blumenson has the 90th in charge of the sector; who held the hill itself is not settled.
- **Cassino, May.** The 08:40 "forced off 593" phase rests on one source (warhist.pl); it stays a marked marker phase, against the agreed 19:30 end.

## Korea and Vietnam
- **Fox Hill.** 220 or 240 men; a company, two battalions or a regiment attacking; the 81 mm mortars attached from Weapons Company (no number of tubes given).
- **Chipyong-ni.** The times: Mossman (G Company's hill lost about 03:00, a counter-attack at 04:00) against Gugeler (via Wikipedia, 06:15), both Army histories. The 155 mm guns of B Battery, 503rd, left between the lines (Mossman): the field keeps them as off-map support.
- **Kapyong.** The US 4.2-inch mortar company that left before midnight; the New Zealand regiment's withdrawal at about 02:00-03:00. Both from the Anzac Portal alone.
- **Pork Chop Hill.** The shell counts (Marshall via Marino) and whether the field should run on through 18 April; Hermes does not narrate the April battle.
- **Hue.** Which company took the Treasury (F, H, or taken and given up on 3 February).

## The late wars
- **Goose Green.** The 13:00 Argentine counter-attack (Adkin via Wikipedia: about 07:30; the Argentine Army: about 06:00); A Company 90 or 114; Freedman's official history not read.
- **73 Easting.** Where the first Iraqi position was, the Iraqi numbers and the minefields: McMaster, the IDA reconstruction and Wikipedia differ; the CMH history was not read on these.
- **Mogadishu.** Somali losses (500 to 1,500 by the CMH, kept; 200 to 364 by others).
- **Grozny.** The breakout (18:00 on 1 January here; about midnight by Russian accounts), the start of the fight (16:30 or 19:00), and both sides' strengths. No official history.
- **Wanat.** When the Apaches (about 05:20 or half an hour in) and the medevac (05:52 or 06:15) came: the CSI study's battle chapter was not read.
- **Keating.** The Afghan soldiers (20 here, 42 by Wikipedia) and the Latvians; where the attackers broke in.

## The medieval wars
- **Crécy, Agincourt, Dornach, Towton, Visby, Aljubarrota:** the open points in each history's *Where the accounts disagree*; no official histories exist.
- **Sidon.** "Mamluk" (the study, after *Eracles*) or "Ayyubid" (the ruler of Damascus in 1253).
