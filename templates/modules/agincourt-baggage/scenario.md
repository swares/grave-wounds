# The baggage {#baggage}

*For the GM.*

**Where and when.** Maisoncelles, a village south of the field of Agincourt, on 25 October 1415. The English
army spent the wet night before the battle there, and in the morning the horses and the baggage were left at the rear. While the
battle was fought, men of the Hesdin country under Ysembart d'Azincourt, Robinet de Bournonville and Rifflart
de Clamasse, a few men-at-arms and about six hundred peasants, fell on the baggage, with the guard at the
battle (Monstrelet). They took horses, money and jewels, the king's crown, his sword and the seals of the
chancery (Barker, *Agincourt*, from the royal accounts). The king's chaplain who wrote the *Gesta* sat on a
horse among the baggage, and has the attack come as the fighting began. Some chroniclers blamed the raid for
Henry's order to kill the prisoners. That much is history. Our four men, and this corner of the baggage, are ours.

**Read this out.** *It rained all night. In the morning the army went up the field, and you were told to stay
with the carts: the sick, the servants and the priests are all that is left here. A roar comes over the
rise to the north. Then horses scream at the lines, and men in quilted coats and no badges are among the carts
with axes and spears, a knight in harness behind them.*

{{pt:map}}

**The raiders.** Six of Ysembart's men reach this corner: four countrymen, a varlet and a man-at-arms on foot.
They came to plunder, not to die.

{{pt:foes}}

**How it runs.**

1. **Set-up (10 minutes).** Hand out the sheets and pick the setting. Each player places his man in the village
   or the baggage park, south of the carts' first row. The raiders start on box {{pt_track}} of the approach track;
   the GM keeps their numbers on the roster.
2. **Arrows (2 rounds).** Walter may shoot as they come in over the open field.
3. **The fight (45 to 60 minutes).** Keep a note of the time.
4. **Plunder.** A raider next to a cart may spend his whole turn taking something: put a *Round* marker on him for
   plunder. A raider with plunder makes for the north edge, and is gone when he steps off it. At the king's cart
   (the dark hex), the first raider to take plunder has the crown and the sword.
5. **The end.** The raiders' nerve check decides it (*The fight*), with a change: they also check at the end of a
   round in which one of them got off the map with plunder, and with the king's crown gone they run as soon as
   they fail. A man who surrenders is a prisoner, and the Leader decides what happens to him. **If the four lose,**
   the game goes on: the raiders take what they can carry and go, and the army comes back at dusk.

**Who won.** The four have done well if the king's cart is untouched, whatever else is gone. Count the plunder
carried off: it matters on the march.

# The march {#march}

*For the GM.*

**The news.** The army comes back down the field with its own wounded and its prisoners, and the lords' dead in
carts. The night is spent at Maisoncelles again. Then the army marches for Calais, about 45 miles to the north (The
National Archives), short of food; it got there in a few days. The four go with it, with whoever of them can't
walk.

**The week's weather.** Use this table, so nobody rolls for it. It is from the game's weather rules for this place
and the season.

{{pt:weather}}

**The decision that costs something.** How do the wounded go?

| Choice | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| March with the army | Every man who can't walk needs a place in a cart, or 2 men to carry him. −20 on any recovery check rolled on a march day. Calais on the fourth day (ours): quarters, if the Quartermaster can find any. |
| Stay at Maisoncelles | No march, but the dead of the field lie unburied within reach for {{dead_days}} days, the army is gone, and the countryside is the raiders'. |
| Go slowly behind the army | Half a day's march a day: Calais on the sixth day (ours). No −20 on recovery checks, but stragglers are fair game for the raiders. |

**Events.** Use one on a slow day, or roll d6. Each should make someone decide something.

| d6 | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | A wounded man-at-arms of another retinue begs a place in the four's cart, and offers his purse for it. |
| 2 | The flux is in the army. A man of the next company is down with it, and wants the Leech. |
| 3 | A countryman offers to sell back the king's things, if any were taken, for a price. Is he one of the raiders? |
| 4 | Forage: the Quartermaster rolls Persuade or Fieldcraft. On a success, 2 days' rations. |
| 5 | A captain wants every man who can walk to march with the prisoners. The Leader may send someone, or lie. |
| 6 | Rain all day: the road is mud, and a march takes the whole day for half the distance. |

**The end of the week.** Make the recovery checks and the sickness rolls, then the feedback. Who is alive, and who can walk?

**If you're using the roller only for the fight**, that's fine. Keep the week on paper.
