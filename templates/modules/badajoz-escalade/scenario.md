# The escalade {#escalade}

*For the GM.*

**Where and when.** Badajoz, on the Spanish frontier with Portugal, the night of 6 to 7 April 1812. Wellington's
army has three breaches in the walls, and at ten at night it storms them: about forty attacks are made on the
breaches, and all fail (historyofwar). Two escalades away from them succeed. Picton's 3rd Division climbs into
the castle, and Leith's 5th Division, late because its ladder party was lost, climbs the bastion of San Vicente.
The wall there was "thirty feet high, and the ladders were too short", so the men found a low point and got in by
three ladders (Napier, retold; Stanhope has the same height). The division beat off a counter-attack, then went
along the walls towards the breaches, and the French there gave way (historyofwar; Kincaid). The 5th lost about
600 men. That much is history. Our four men, this stretch of the rampart and the French on it are ours.

**Read this out.** *The breaches across the town are a roar of musketry and fire. Here it is darker. The ladder
is up against the wall, short, its top a man's height under the parapet. A man ahead of you goes up and is gone
over the top. Someone above is shouting in French. You climb.*

{{pt:map}}

**The French.** Three men of the garrison hold this stretch of the rampart, a sergent and two fusiliers. Most of
the garrison is at the breaches, and these few were not expecting anyone here.

{{pt:foes}}

**How it runs.**

1. **Set-up (10 minutes).** Hand out the sheets and pick the setting. The four start in the ditch at the foot of
   the ladders. The GM places the French anywhere on the rampart's walk, at least 3 hexes from the parapet, and
   lights the two fires of burning hay.
2. **Up the ladders.** A man at the foot of a ladder climbs on his turn (*the ladders*, on the quick reference) and
   comes out on the parapet hex above it on his next turn. The French may shoot down at a man on a ladder, or meet
   him at the top.
3. **The fight (45 to 60 minutes).** Keep a note of the time.
4. **The end.** The French nerve check decides it (*The fight*). A man who surrenders is a prisoner, and the Leader
   decides what happens to him. **If the four lose,** the game goes on: they are thrown back into the ditch, and
   the next company up the ladders takes the rampart an hour later.

**After the fight (10 minutes).** Bind what can be bound. Then the town opens below them.

# The week after {#week}

*For the GM.*

**The news.** By two in the morning the storm of the breaches is called off, and the French there give way when
the 5th Division comes along the walls. The governor surrenders at seven, across the river. The army has lost
about 4,670 men killed and wounded in the night. Then the town is sacked: for most of three days the army is out
of hand, and it is the people of Badajoz, Spaniards, not the French, who suffer for it. Officers who try to stop it
are shot at by their own men. Wellington has a gallows put up, and order comes back slowly (historyofwar;
Wikipedia, citing Fletcher and Oman).

**The decision that costs something.** What do the four do in the sack? Play it as a choice and its cost, not as
a scene: the sack happened, and this table needn't linger on it.

| Choice | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Keep together, and keep out of it | They stay on the rampart or with their officers. No plunder. The Leader rolls Command; on a failure one of the four slips away into the town and comes back with something, and trouble. |
| Guard a house | They put themselves between the soldiers and one family. Roll the Leader's Persuade or Command each day of the sack; on a failure there is a fight with their own side's men (the roller's rules, unarmed or with the butt). The family may be worth more to them later than anything they could take. |
| Go into the town | Plunder: each man rolls d100 once on *Finds* below, and the Quartermaster may sell what they take. Each man also rolls a stop check against his own Nerve at the end of the sack: on a failure he is drunk or in a fight, and starts the next day at −1 Blood. The provosts and the gallows are waiting. |

**Events.** Use one on a slow day, or roll d6. Each should make someone decide something.

| d6 | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | The surgeons' tents by the breaches are full. A wounded man of another company begs the Leech for help before his own. |
| 2 | A Spanish woman finds the four: her husband was killed in the sack. She wants them to name the man who did it. |
| 3 | The provosts are hanging no one, but they are flogging. One of the four is named by an officer who saw him in the town. |
| 4 | Forage: the Quartermaster rolls Persuade or Fieldcraft. On a success, 2 days' rations. |
| 5 | Burial parties for the breaches: the dead lie close, and the Leader is asked for men. The dead lie unburied for {{dead_days}} days unless they go. |
| 6 | The army is to march north. A man too weak to march must be left in Badajoz, among the people the army has sacked. |

**The end of the week.** Make the recovery checks and the sickness rolls, then the feedback. Who is alive, and who can march?

**If you're using the roller only for the fight**, that's fine. Keep the week on paper.
