## Why the team marches {#frames}

Five reasons for a team in the late wars. A frame adds no rules: it says who pays, what the team is
after and what ends it, and uses the [patrons](rules.html#patrons), [pay](rules.html#rank) and
[work](rules.html#work) in the Rules. The history in each frame is sourced below. The goals, first jobs
and complications are ours, written for play.

### A task force to the south {#frame-taskforce}

Argentina landed on the Falklands on 2 April 1982. Within days a British task force sailed, 8,000 miles,
with two parachute battalions, the Guards and the Royal Marines. 2 Para fought at Goose Green on 28 and
29 May; the Scots Guards took Tumbledown on the night of 13 June, and Stanley surrendered on the 14th.

- **Who pays:** the Crown, as in barracks.
- **The goal:** win it before the southern winter does.
- **What ends it:** the surrender, a wound, or the ship home.
- **First job:** the landing at San Carlos, and the march across the island.
- **What goes wrong:** the helicopters went down with the ship, so you walk. The peat will not hold a trench. The Argentines are dug in on the high ground.
- **Maps:** Goose Green, Tumbledown.

### A hundred hours {#frame-hundred}

The ground war of Desert Storm began on 24 February 1991 and ended a hundred hours later. On the 26th,
the 2nd Armored Cavalry Regiment met a brigade of the Republican Guard at the 73 Easting, in a
sandstorm, and destroyed it.

- **Who pays:** the government, with combat pay.
- **The goal:** finish it fast, and lose no one.
- **What ends it:** the cease-fire.
- **First job:** the breach, and the dash north and east.
- **What goes wrong:** the sandstorm blinds the air cover. Friendly fire. The front moves faster than the fuel.
- **Maps:** 73 Easting.

### A small team far out {#frame-team}

Some of the late wars' fights were a few dozen men far from help. In Mogadishu on 3 October 1993 Task
Force Ranger's raid turned into a night surrounded in the city after two helicopters were shot down. At
Debecka, in northern Iraq on 6 April 2003, two Special Forces teams and their Kurdish Peshmerga stopped
an Iraqi armoured attack.

- **Who pays:** the government, at a specialist's rates.
- **The goal:** do the job, and bring everyone home.
- **What ends it:** the job done, or the relief column.
- **First job:** a raid that should take an hour.
- **What goes wrong:** a helicopter goes down. The relief cannot get through the roadblocks. The friendly air strike hits your allies.
- **Maps:** Mogadishu, Debecka.

### A tour on an outpost {#frame-outpost}

From 2007 the US Army's tours in Iraq and Afghanistan ran fifteen months, cut back to twelve for units
leaving from August 2008. Platoons held small outposts in the valleys of eastern Afghanistan: Wanat,
attacked on 13 July 2008, and COP Keating, attacked on 3 October 2009. The Marines' tours were seven
months; they took Fallujah in November 2004.

- **Who pays:** the government, with combat pay, tax free.
- **The goal:** hold the outpost, and get every man home.
- **What ends it:** your tour, a wound, or the outpost closed.
- **First job:** fill sandbags, and a patrol to the village.
- **What goes wrong:** the enemy holds the high ground all round. The air support is an hour away. The tour is extended.
- **Maps:** Fallujah, Wanat, COP Keating.

### Conscripts into the city {#frame-conscripts}

On 31 December 1994 Russian columns drove into Grozny, the capital of breakaway Chechnya, many of them
conscripts weeks out of training. Chechen fighters who knew the streets let the armour in and destroyed
it with rocket-propelled grenades from the buildings.

- **Who pays:** the state, late, and little.
- **The goal:** survive your two years.
- **What ends it:** your discharge, a wound, or capture.
- **First job:** ride in on the armour to the station.
- **What goes wrong:** no one has a map of the city. The radios cannot talk to each other. The officers are as new as you are.
- **Maps:** Grozny.
