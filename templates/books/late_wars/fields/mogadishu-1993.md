### The campaign
In May 1993 the United Nations force UNOSOM II took over in Somalia from the American-led UNITAF. On 5 June militia of Mohamed Farrah Aideed's Somali National Alliance (SNA) ambushed Pakistani soldiers of UNOSOM II in Mogadishu, killing 24. The next day the Security Council passed Resolution 837, which the US Army's Center of Military History (CMH) calls a more aggressive stance towards Aideed. The UN's special representative, Admiral Jonathan Howe, put a price of $25,000 on information leading to Aideed's arrest, and through the summer American and UN troops raided SNA targets in the city.

In late August Task Force Ranger arrived, under Major General William F. Garrison of Joint Special Operations Command. It answered not to the UN but directly to General Joseph Hoar of Central Command. Wikipedia gives its parts as B Company, 3rd Battalion, 75th Ranger Regiment (Captain Michael D. Steele); C Squadron, 1st Special Forces Operational Detachment-Delta (Lieutenant Colonel Gary L. Harrell); 16 helicopters of the 1st Battalion, 160th Special Operations Aviation Regiment; and Navy SEALs and Air Force pararescuemen and combat controllers of the 24th Special Tactics Squadron. The UN's quick reaction force was the 2nd Battalion, 14th Infantry, 10th Mountain Division, under Lieutenant Colonel William C. David. That September the Secretary of Defense, Les Aspin, turned down a request from Major General Thomas Montgomery, the American commander, for tanks and armoured vehicles.

On the afternoon of Sunday 3 October word came that two of Aideed's lieutenants, Omar Salad Elmi and Abdi Hassan Awale, would meet in a building near the Olympic Hotel, close to the Bakara Market in the heart of Aideed's Habr Gidr clan. Delta would land at the building from MH-6 Little Birds, Rangers would fast-rope from Black Hawks to block the four corners, and a convoy of Humvees and trucks under Lieutenant Colonel Danny McKnight would carry the prisoners out. The raid was planned to take no more than 30 minutes.

### The places
- **The target building**: a house near the Olympic Hotel, a few blocks north of the Bakara Market.
- **The Bakara Market**: the city's largest market, in the clan's own district; Captain Haad says its people joined the fight.
- **Crash site 1**: where Super 61 came down, about three blocks from the target by the CMH's account; the Rangers and Delta held the houses round it all night.
- **Crash site 2**: where Super 64 came down, less than a mile to the south; overrun about an hour after the crash.
- **The airport**: Task Force Ranger's compound, about four minutes' flight from the target.
- **The K-4 traffic circle**: where the 10th Mountain's first company was pinned down in the evening.
- **The New Port**: where the relief column of Pakistani, Malaysian and American troops formed up.
- **National Street**: the road the relief column came in by; two Malaysian carriers that turned off it were ambushed.
- **The Pakistani stadium**: the UN base where the force came out at dawn and the wounded were treated.

### The leaders
- **William F. Garrison** (United States, Task Force Ranger): commanded from the airport; later wrote to President Clinton accepting full responsibility.
- **Thomas Montgomery** (United States, US forces and deputy UN force commander): asked for armour in September and was refused.
- **Danny McKnight** (United States, 3rd Battalion, 75th Ranger Regiment): led the ground convoy.
- **Michael D. Steele** (United States, B Company, 3rd Battalion, 75th Rangers): led the Rangers on the ground.
- **William C. David** (United States, 2nd Battalion, 14th Infantry): led the 10th Mountain troops of the relief column.
- **Michael Durant** (United States, chief warrant officer, pilot of Super 64): the one man from his crash site to live; held prisoner until 14 October.
- **Gary I. Gordon and Randall D. Shughart** (United States, master sergeant and sergeant first class, Delta snipers): defended crash site 2 and were killed; awarded the Medal of Honor.
- **Mohamed Farrah Aideed** (SNA, leader): the man the task force had come to catch; died of wounds in August 1995.
- **Sharif Hassan Giumale** (SNA, colonel): named by Wikipedia as the SNA's tactical commander in the battle.
- **Captain Haad** (SNA, sector commander): led about 30 militiamen that day and later told his story to PBS *Frontline*.
- **Abdul Latif Ahmad** (Malaysia, commander, Malaysian Battalion): his memoir gives the Malaysian account of the relief, led by Major Abdul Aziz's Alpha Company.

### The battle
The American accounts give local time (East Africa Time, GMT+3), and so do the times here. At 13:50 the code word went out. The helicopters lifted off at about 15:30, the convoy three minutes behind, and at 15:42 the assault force was at the target. Private First Class Todd Blackburn fell from his rope and was badly hurt; three Humvees took him back, and Sergeant Dominick Pilla was shot dead in one of them, the first American killed. The assault force took 24 prisoners. By Haad's account the militia had been warned from the airfield that helicopters were on their way to a serious attack, and fired at them as they flew low; men took cover under sheets held up by women and boys.

At about 16:20 Super 61, flown by Chief Warrant Officer Clifton Wolcott, was hit by a rocket-propelled grenade and came down about three blocks away. Both pilots were killed. A Little Bird landed beside the wreck and flew out two wounded men. The combat search and rescue team, 15 men under Captain Bill Coultrup, fast-roped in from Super 68, which was hit by a grenade as they went down but got back to the airport. The assault and blocking forces went to the crash on foot. The convoy could not find the site, lost two 5-ton trucks, and went back to the airfield with its prisoners and wounded.

At about 16:40 Super 64, Durant's aircraft, was hit in the tail rotor and came down less than a mile to the south. No ground force could reach it soon. Gordon and Shughart asked three times to be put down there, and were landed from Super 62, which was then hit by a grenade and crash-landed. Their Medal of Honor citations say they pulled the four badly hurt crew from the wreck, held a perimeter until their ammunition ran out, and were killed; Gordon gave the pilot a rifle with its last rounds. The citations say they saved Durant's life. The site was overrun at about 17:40, and Durant was taken prisoner.

The force round crash site 1 held a block of houses through the night, with Little Birds firing for them in pairs. The pararescuemen set up a casualty collection point by the tail of the wreck and lined it with the Kevlar floor panels torn from the aircraft. Technical Sergeant Timothy Wilkinson freed the surviving crewmen from the wreck. At about 17:30 he crossed an open crossroads three times to treat about a dozen men, and Master Sergeant Scott Fales, shot through the leg, worked beside him. Corporal James Smith, a Ranger, was hit in the thigh; the bullet cut his femoral artery. The men round him could not get him out, and he bled to death, his father said, over about three hours. At 19:08 Super 66 brought in water, ammunition and medical supplies and was badly damaged.

A company of the 2nd Battalion, 14th Infantry went out in the evening, was pinned down near K-4 and went back. The relief column formed at the New Port, with Pakistani tanks in the lead and Malaysian armoured carriers behind, more than 60 vehicles by the CMH's count, with Cobra gunships overhead. Two Malaysian carriers with the 2nd Platoon of A Company, 2nd Battalion, 14th Infantry turned south off National Street and were ambushed; their men took cover and were reached about four hours later. Lance Corporal Mat Aznan Awang of the 19th Battalion, Royal Malay Regiment, driving one of them, was killed when a rocket came through the window. At 01:55 on 4 October the Malaysian carriers and the 10th Mountain troops reached crash site 1. Another part of the column reached crash site 2 and found no trace of the crew or the snipers.

The men at crash site 1 worked through the night under fire to free Wolcott's body from the wreck. The wounded were loaded into the carriers. At 05:42 the move out began, and with no room in the vehicles many men ran and walked behind them, the "Mogadishu Mile". The main force reached the Pakistani stadium at about 06:30, where the medical staff gave the wounded emergency treatment. Wilkinson's carrier, which stopped for about 90 minutes to pick up another casualty, came in at about 08:00. On 7 October President Clinton set 31 March 1994 as the date by which all American forces would leave Somalia.

### Where the accounts disagree
- **The American losses.** The CMH gives Task Force Ranger 16 killed and 57 wounded, and the 2nd Battalion, 14th Infantry 2 killed and 22 wounded: 18 killed and 79 wounded. The Special Operations Command's own history of 1994 gives the task force 16 killed and 83 wounded. PBS *Frontline* and the National Museum of the US Air Force give 18 killed and 84 wounded; Wikipedia gives 84 in its summary and 73 in its text.
- **The Malaysian and Pakistani losses.** The CMH gives 2 Malaysians killed and 7 wounded, and 2 Pakistanis wounded. Wikipedia gives 1 Malaysian killed and 7 wounded; its article on Mat Aznan Awang, 1 killed and 9 wounded. Abdul Latif Ahmad, as the *Borneo Post* reports him, gives 1 killed and 6 hurt.
- **The Somali losses.** The CMH puts them at 500 to 1,500. The Special Operations Command history gives the Red Cross estimate of about 200 dead and 700 wounded, and Radio Mogadishu's figure of 364 killed and 754 wounded. Wikipedia gives the SNA's figure as 315 dead, 133 of them its own fighters, and 812 wounded; Haad gave the same 133 for the militia. Ambassador Robert Oakley's own estimate was 1,500 to 2,000 killed and wounded.
- **Civilians.** No source read separates the Somali civilian dead from the fighters: by the SNA's figures in Wikipedia about 180 of its 315 dead were not fighters, and Haad called the civilian deaths "almost uncountable".
- **The Pakistani tanks.** The CMH has the tanks lead the column. Abdul Latif says they would not go on because they had no night sights, and the Malaysians moved to the front.
- **When the relief left.** Wikipedia's timeline has the column move out at 23:23 and its main article at about 23:00; Abdul Latif, as reported, says the Malaysian operation began at about 20:30.
- **The size of the column.** The CMH says more than 60 vehicles; Wikipedia about 100. Abdul Latif gives 113 Malaysians, of whom Alpha Company had 5 officers and 42 other ranks.
- **Where Super 61 fell.** The CMH says about three blocks from the target; Wikipedia's main article about 300 yards east, and its timeline about four blocks north.
- **When Super 64 fell.** Wikipedia gives about 16:40; Haad remembered about an hour after the first, around 17:00.
- **How Gordon and Shughart died.** Gordon's citation has Shughart killed first. Wikipedia says Bowden's account, through Sergeant Paul Howe, who heard Shughart call for help on the radio, and Durant's own book differ on the order and on which weapon Durant was given.
- **The second lieutenant's name.** Wikipedia gives him as Abdi Hassan Awale in one place and Mohamed Hassan Awale in another.
- **The armour.** Aspin refused Montgomery's request for tanks, and said afterwards that he would have decided otherwise had he known what he knew later. *Stars and Stripes* reports that the Senate review co-written by Senator John Warner in 1995 found that tanks "may well have saved U.S. lives".
