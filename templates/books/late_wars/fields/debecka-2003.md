### The campaign
Turkey would not let the invasion of Iraq be launched from its soil in March 2003, so the northern front was left to a special operations task force, Combined Joint Special Operations Task Force North, called Task Force Viking, under Colonel Charles Cleveland of the 10th Special Forces Group. His own briefing gives it 5,200 men: the 10th Group, the 3rd Battalion of the 3rd Group with its gun trucks, the 173rd Airborne Brigade, which jumped onto Bashur airfield on the night of 26 March, and others. With them fought the Peshmerga of the two Kurdish parties, the KDP and the PUK, each with 25,000 to 30,000 fighters.

Against them, along the Green Line between the Kurdish region and Baghdad's territory, stood about two-fifths of the Iraqi army, the Army's official history says, with Republican Guard divisions, Fedayeen and Ba'ath militia behind them. On 6 April Task Force Viking made four attacks at once south of Erbil. In one of them two Special Forces A-teams, ODAs 391 and 392 of Company C, 3rd Battalion, 3rd Special Forces Group, with about 80 KDP Peshmerga, were to break through the Iraqi line on the ridge north of Debecka and seize the crossroads beyond, where the road from Erbil met Highway 2 between Kirkuk and Mosul. A Peshmerga attack alone the day before had been beaten back.

### The places
- **The ridge** (Zurqah Ziraw Dagh): the long ridge, about 400 m high, that the Iraqis held, about 5 km north of the crossroads.
- **Pir Da'ud**: the Kurdish town north of the ridge, where the teams met the Peshmerga.
- **The Debecka crossroads**: where the Erbil-Qayyarah road meets Highway 2, about 40 km south-south-west of Erbil. It is the only place in this fight with a published coordinate.
- **Debecka** (Dibagah): the town south-east of the crossroads.
- **The Alamo**: the low ridge about 900 m north of the crossroads, so named by the teams, where they stopped the Iraqi counter-attack.
- **Press Hill**: the second ridge behind it, named for the press who gathered there, with an abandoned Iraqi tank on it.
- **Makhmur**: the district town, about 22 km to the west-south-west.

### The leaders
- **Charles Cleveland** (American, colonel, Task Force Viking): commanded the northern front.
- **Curtis Hubbard** (American, major, ODB 390): brought his cut-down 5-ton trucks forward to resupply the teams under fire.
- **Eric Howard** (American, major, AOB 040): agreed the dawn attack with the KDP commander, and was wounded by the bomb.
- **Eric Wright** (American, captain, ODA 391) and **Matthew Saunders** (American, captain, ODA 392): commanded the two teams at the crossroads.
- **Jason D. Brown** and **Jeffrey Adamec** (American, staff sergeants): Javelin gunners, both awarded the Silver Star.
- **General Mustafa** (Kurdish, KDP, Western Military District): agreed the combined attack; his full name was not found.
- **Wajih Barzani** (Kurdish, head of the KDP's special forces and brother of the party's leader, Masoud Barzani): badly wounded by the bomb.
- No Iraqi commander at Debecka is named in any source read.

### The battle
Iraq kept summer time in 2003, four hours ahead of Greenwich. Gresham's times are given here as he prints them, "local", though they do not fit the CENTCOM release for the bomb (see below).

B-52s struck the ridge on the evening of 5 April and again at 04:01 on the 6th. The teams crossed the line of departure at 07:00, an hour late. The Peshmerga breached the earth berm in front of the Iraqi line with captured Iraqi explosives, and some Iraqis gave themselves up. About 09:00 the teams drove south down the paved road to Highway 2 and held the crossroads for about 45 minutes. Staff Sergeant Brown fired the first Javelin at a truck about 3,000 m off.

By 09:40 MT-LBs and T-55s were coming up out of the haze from the south. The teams fell back about 900 m to the low ridge they called the Alamo, dodging and bounding through artillery and mortar fire without loss. From there they fought the Iraqis off with Javelins and their gun trucks' heavy machine guns and grenade launchers. Gresham counts 19 Javelins fired for 17 hits; not one was fired at less than 2,200 m, the official maximum being 2,000, and the longest went 4,200 m. The official history counts five T-55s, three armoured carriers and eight trucks destroyed and about 90 Iraqis put out of action. No American or Kurd was hurt by Iraqi fire.

{{picture:debecka-javelin}}

Close air support was slow to come. When it did, the lead of two Navy F-14s put its laser-guided bomb not on the Iraqi tanks but on the abandoned T-55 on Press Hill, where Peshmerga, the American command group and a BBC team had gathered with their vehicles. Kurdish officials counted 18 dead, 17 Kurdish fighters and the BBC's translator, Kamaran Abdurazaq Muhamed, and about 45 wounded, Wajih Barzani among them. Sergeant Mike Ray, the only Special Forces medic there, took 15 minutes to sort the wounded, and the men of ODA 391 tied 19 tourniquets in half an hour. Navy F/A-18s then bombed the remaining tanks. By about 11:15 the Iraqis were breaking, and a dozen tried to surrender; by 16:00 they had all surrendered, died or gone.

### Where the accounts disagree
- **The clock.** Gresham puts the first close air support, and the bomb, just before 10:00. The CENTCOM release puts the bomb at about 04:15 Eastern Daylight Time, which is 12:15 in Iraq, and CNN, the *Washington Post* and Radio Free Europe all put it about noon. Nothing found explains the two hours between them.
- **The Iraqi strength.** A company of about 100 (Wikipedia); an armoured battalion of 12 T-55s and 15 MT-LBs (Gresham); an infantry brigade (Antenori); three brigades of the 34th Infantry Division (Gresham again). The official history names the 1st Mechanized Division instead and counts at the crossroads at least five tanks, four armoured carriers, two troop trucks and several command vehicles; the field uses its figures.
- **The Iraqi losses.** Five T-55s, three carriers and eight trucks (the official history); five T-55s, eight carriers and four trucks (Wikipedia); eight tanks and 16 carriers left behind (Antenori). About 30 prisoners, or 20.
- **The bomb.** A 1,000 lb GBU-16 (Gresham, Wikipedia); a 2,000 lb bomb (the official history); a 750 lb bomb (Task & Purpose). An F-14 by Gresham and by John Simpson, who was there; an F-15 by Wikipedia's article on Task Force Viking.
- **The dead and wounded of the strike.** 18 dead and 45 wounded by the KDP's count, carried by CNN, AP and the *Washington Post*; 17 and 45 (AP, Gresham); ten to fifteen killed or wounded by the official history, a single report lower than every count made at the time; one civilian killed by CENTCOM's first report. The book uses the KDP's count.
- **The American wounded.** One (CENTCOM), three (the *Washington Post*), four (Wikipedia), or none. Only Major Howard is named.
- **Where it happened.** CENTCOM put the bombing "in the vicinity of Kalak", some 45 km away.
- **Not reached.** The Army's *On Point* passage on Debecka, Robinson's *Masters of Chaos*, Antenori's *Roughneck Nine-One*, and any Kurdish or Iraqi account.

!!! story "The translator"
    Kamaran Abdurazaq Muhamed was 25, a Kurd working as translator for the BBC's John Simpson. On
    the morning of 6 April he was with the convoy of Kurdish and American vehicles gathered round a
    burned-out Iraqi tank on Press Hill, behind the fighting at the crossroads. The American bomb
    meant for the Iraqi tanks fell 10 or 12 feet from Simpson's car. Simpson was hit in the leg and
    lost the hearing of one ear; the producer, Tom Giles, was hurt in the foot. Kamaran was hit in
    the lower legs by fragments, and bled to death.
