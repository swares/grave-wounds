### The campaign
On 31 March 2004 insurgents ambushed four armed security contractors of Blackwater USA there. A crowd burned the bodies and hung two from the Old Bridge over the river. Lieutenant General Ricardo Sanchez ordered an attack, although Lieutenant General James Conway and Major General James Mattis of the Marines urged restraint. On 5 April Colonel John Toolan's Regimental Combat Team 1 (RCT-1) closed a cordon round the city, and on the 6th the 2nd Battalion, 1st Marines attacked into the Jolan, the old north-western quarter. The Marines halted on 9 April and pulled out in early May. They left the city to the Fallujah Brigade, an Iraqi force under former Ba'athist officers, which broke up and gave its weapons to the insurgents.

Over the summer Fallujah became the base of Abu Musab al-Zarqawi and of insurgent groups under Abdullah al-Janabi and Omar Hadid. The Marines reckoned there were 3,000 to 4,500 fighters by late October, with more than 300 prepared positions, many of them mined or booby-trapped. Planning for a second attack began in September. Lieutenant General John Sattler's I Marine Expeditionary Force gathered nine American battalions and six Iraqi ones, about 12,000 men. The Marines asked for Army armour, and the 2nd Battalion, 7th Cavalry came from Taji with 14 tanks and 30 Bradleys. Leaflets and broadcasts told the people to leave, and most did.

On the night of 7 November Iraqi commandos took the hospital and Marines the bridges west of the city. The next night two regimental combat teams attacked south across the railway: RCT-1 in the west through the Jolan, RCT-7 to the east. The Americans called the operation Phantom Fury; after Prime Minister Ayad Allawi was briefed in late October it was renamed al-Fajr, "New Dawn".

### The places
- **The Jolan**: the old north-western quarter by the river, of narrow, irregular lanes and walled houses.
- **The railway and the train station**: the northern edge of the city and RCT-1's start line; the breach was cut in the embankment west of the station.
- **The apartment complex**: the blocks north of the railway, taken by 3/5 on the 8th, from which Colonel Shupp watched the assault.
- **The Jolan cemetery and the Ma'ahidy Mosque**: 3/5's first objectives on the night of 8 November.
- **Jolan Park**: the one open space in the quarter, with a Ferris wheel that could be seen from afar, and the 2-7 Cavalry's objective.
- **Phase Line Henry**: the north-south road down which the Army armour drove.
- **Phase Line Fran and the Martyrs' Cemetery**: Highway 10 across the middle of the city, and the cemetery on it.
- **The hospital and the bridges**: on the peninsula west of the river, taken on the night of 7 November.

### The leaders
- **John F. Sattler** (Coalition, lieutenant general, I Marine Expeditionary Force): took over from Conway on 12 September 2004.
- **Richard F. Natonski** (Coalition, major general, 1st Marine Division): commanded the assault.
- **Michael A. Shupp** (Coalition, colonel, RCT-1): the Marine main effort, in the west of the city.
- **Patrick J. Malay** (Coalition, lieutenant colonel, 3rd Battalion, 5th Marines): attacked into the Jolan along the river.
- **Willard A. Buhl** (Coalition, lieutenant colonel, 3rd Battalion, 1st Marines): followed the Army armour to clear the houses.
- **James E. Rainey** (Coalition, lieutenant colonel, Task Force 2-7 Cavalry): argued for a wider front than the first plan gave him.
- **Peter C. Glass** (Coalition, captain, C Company, 3rd Battalion, 8th Cavalry): led the night attack to Phase Line Fran.
- **Robert J. Bodisch Jr.** (Coalition, captain, C Company, 2nd Tank Battalion): found the breach sites and led the Marine tanks.
- **Abdullah al-Janabi** (Insurgents): led one of the groups that ruled the city after the spring.
- **Omar Hadid** (Insurgents): led another; Wikipedia gives him as killed in November 2004.

### The battle
Times are as the Marine history gives them, presumably local Iraq time (GMT+3); no source read names the clock. At about 14:00 on 8 November four F/A-18Ds dropped eight GBU-31 bombs to cut the railway west of the overpass. Company L of 3/1, with a company of the Iraqi National Guard, took the train station and held it by 20:34. The line charges failed to clear the lanes, so engineers cut the rails with TNT and bulldozers pushed them aside. At 19:26 3/5 went in through the cut. Company I cleared the Jolan cemetery and took the Ma'ahidy Mosque; Company L pushed along the river towards the palm groves.

At 00:14 on 9 November C Company, 3rd Battalion, 8th Cavalry crossed the breach and led Task Force 2-7 down Phase Line Henry; the rest followed at 01:30. The chemical lights marking the lanes did not show in the Bradleys' thermal sights. Before sunrise A Company, 2nd Battalion, 7th Cavalry drove from the station and took Jolan Park, its tanks and Bradleys shooting into the buildings. Company K of 3/1 came in at 04:00 and Company I by 06:00, with Company L behind in reserve.

{{picture:fallujah-jolan}}

The Marines cleared house by house behind the armour. Captain Timothy Jent of Company K kept two platoons forward, one with the vehicles and one lighter platoon to guard a flank or carry out the wounded. The insurgents aimed their rockets at the tanks' tracks, sights and bore evacuators. At about 08:30 one disabled Bodisch's tank. Company L of 3/1 lost two Marines in a house where insurgents waited for them, and met mortars firing from underground near Phase Line Cathy. Company I reached Phase Line Donna at about 12:30, passed through A Company of 2-7, and held its objective for nearly five hours under rifle and rocket fire. In the afternoon Bodisch came back in a fresh tank to help Marines of Company I hit by snipers in a building. In 3/5's sector the Marines fought at 50 to 100 m for about four hours that morning.

At about 18:00 3/5 halted for the night, and 3/1 stopped level with Phase Line Cathy at Jolan Park. There snipers killed a Marine and an Iraqi soldier. At about 23:00 Rainey sent Glass's company down Henry and onto Fran, to the Martyrs' Cemetery. A mosque beside it fired on them. Glass's call for artillery or air was refused, and Apaches of the 4th Aviation Brigade silenced the fire. The task force was on Highway 10 ahead of the plan. On the 10th 3/5 took what one Marine's citation calls the last strongpoint in the Jolan, and by 13 November most of the heavy fighting in the city was over, though the clearing went on into December.

The sources read say little of the battalion aid stations; the companies carried their own wounded back, Jent's lighter platoon among them, and some of RCT-7's went out through the Army's breach. Alpha and Bravo Surgical Companies were at Al Asad and Camp Fallujah, and a surgical shock trauma platoon at Camp Taqaddum. In the first six days of the offensive that platoon alone took 157 patients, 29 of them Iraqis, and operated 73 times; at the peak four surgeons did 46 operations in 12 hours. Most of the wounds were blast and gunshot, 75 to 80 per cent in the arms and legs. Patients went on by CH-46 helicopter, with nurses aboard, to the next level of care, the combat surgical hospitals; the blood came forward by helicopter from the hospital in Baghdad, and many of the wounded were kept under anaesthetic all the way to Landstuhl in Germany.

### Where the accounts disagree
- **The April operation.** Estes, for the Marines, and Wikipedia call it Vigilant Resolve; Matthews, for the Army, calls it Valiant Resolve. Wikipedia gives 27 Americans killed in its text and "39+" in its table.
- **The dead in April.** McWilliams gives about 220 Iraqi civilians killed in the first two weeks. Wikipedia, after Iraq Body Count, gives about 800 Iraqi dead, 572 to 616 of them civilians. Ahmed Mansour of Al Jazeera, who was in the city, reported heavy civilian losses.
- **Who was left in the city.** McWilliams says the Marines believed fewer than 500 civilians remained by late October. Wikipedia gives an American estimate of 30,000 to 90,000 left of about 300,000, and a *Guardian* correspondent's 30,000 to 50,000.
- **The insurgents.** Matthews puts them at about 2,000 in April; McWilliams at 3,000 to 4,500 in October, with about 1,000 more nearby; Wikipedia's table at 3,700 to 4,000. A senior US official in September put Zarqawi's forces at about 5,000, mostly not Iraqis.
- **The losses.** Wikipedia gives 54 Americans killed and 425 wounded from 7 to 16 November, and 95 killed and 560 wounded by 23 December, with 8 Iraqi soldiers killed. It gives the insurgent dead as about 1,200 by the Defense Department's count, 1,500 by *Asia Times*, and more than 2,000 by some estimates, with about 1,500 captured.
- **The civilian dead.** The Red Cross was reported on 17 November 2004 as fearing about 800 civilians dead; Iraq Body Count records 581 to 670 from bodies recovered in nine of the city's 27 neighbourhoods; and Mike Marqusee in the *Guardian* (November 2005), as Wikipedia gives him, quoted Iraqi aid groups and medical workers estimating 4,000 to 6,000 dead, mostly civilians.
- **Who led the Jolan attack.** The game has 3/1 coming in behind the Army armour and its own Marine tanks, as McWilliams has it. He also has 3/5 attacking along the river from 19:26 on the 8th, some hours before the armour crossed; it was 3/1 that followed the 2-7 Cavalry.

!!! story "The last strongpoint"
    On 10 November Private First Class Christopher Adlesperger of Company K, 3rd Battalion, 5th
    Marines went into a house in the Jolan with his squad. A machine gun inside killed the point man
    and wounded two others. By his Navy Cross citation, Adlesperger, himself hit by grenade fragments,
    fought his way up the stairs and onto the roof and got the wounded out. He set up firing positions
    there that kept the insurgents pinned in the house or drove them into the fire of the Marines next
    door. He then took the point for the last assault and was the first man back into the courtyard.
    The citation calls the house the last strongpoint in the Jolan. He was promoted lance corporal in a
    lull in the fighting, and was killed in action the next month, before the award was made.
