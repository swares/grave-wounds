### The campaign
Argentina occupied the Falkland Islands on 2 April 1982, and Britain sent a task force to retake them. On 21 May 3 Commando Brigade, under Brigadier Julian Thompson, landed at San Carlos on the west coast of East Falkland. Ships were lost to air attack in the days that followed; the Argentine Army's history of the battle says the British attacked the isthmus to reverse a run of losses that was wearing down support at home. On 26 May the 2nd Battalion, The Parachute Regiment (2 Para) was ordered south to the Darwin and Goose Green isthmus, where the Argentines held two settlements and an airstrip.

The battalion was commanded by Lieutenant Colonel Herbert "H" Jones. Its battle group, about 690 men on Wikipedia's count, had three 105 mm guns, a Milan platoon, Scout helicopters and the guns of HMS *Arrow*; Farrar-Hockley, who commanded A Company, put the battalion at about 450 men when it reached Camilla Creek House. The loss of the *Atlantic Conveyor* with most of the force's heavy-lift helicopters had left little artillery forward. On the eve of the attack the BBC World Service announced that 2 Para was about to attack Darwin and Goose Green.

Against them was Task Force Mercedes under Lieutenant Colonel Ítalo Piaggi: his 12th Infantry Regiment, less a company, C Company of the 25th Infantry Regiment under First Lieutenant Carlos Daniel Esteban, a section of the 8th Regiment, engineers, anti-aircraft guns and three 105 mm pack howitzers. The Argentine Army gives the task force 643 men on 27 May, with 202 Air Force men at Base Cóndor besides. About 100 islanders were held under guard in the Goose Green Social Club.

### The places
- **Camilla Creek House**: the farmhouse north of the isthmus where 2 Para gathered, and the forward base of the Scout helicopters.
- **Burntside House**: the first objective, which A Company attacked in the dark; four islanders were found there unhurt.
- **Coronation Point**: a headland on A Company's line of advance, found empty.
- **Darwin Hill**: the ridge above Darwin settlement, with its gorse gully, where an Argentine company dug in with overhead cover and stopped A Company at first light.
- **Boca House**: the ruined house at the west end of the ridge, where B Company was held, and which it took with Milan support.
- **Darwin**: the small settlement on an inlet below Darwin Hill.
- **Goose Green**: the larger settlement and airstrip to the south, where the islanders were held.
- **The schoolhouse**: at Goose Green, near which Argentine aircraft were shot down in the late afternoon.
- **Mount Osborne**: the height from which, Farrar-Hockley found afterwards, Argentine observers directed artillery onto the ridge.
- **San Carlos**: the beachhead, from which the battalion came and to which its wounded went.

### The leaders
- **H. Jones** (British, lieutenant colonel, 2 Para): killed on Darwin Hill; awarded the Victoria Cross after his death.
- **Chris Keeble** (British, major, second-in-command of 2 Para): took command after Jones's death, closed round Goose Green and arranged the surrender. He received the DSO.
- **Dair Farrar-Hockley** (British, major, A Company): held in the gorse gully below Darwin Hill for more than three hours.
- **John Crosland** (British, major, B Company): fought along the western side to Boca House.
- **Phil Neame** (British, major, D Company): his company advanced between A and B Companies, and one of its platoons fought for the airfield in the afternoon.
- **Julian Thompson** (British, brigadier, 3 Commando Brigade): ordered the attack on 25 May.
- **Ítalo Piaggi** (Argentine, lieutenant colonel, Task Force Mercedes and 12th Infantry Regiment): commanded the garrison; wrote *Ganso Verde* (1986) in defence of his decisions.
- **Pedrozo** (Argentine, air force, Base Cóndor): the senior Argentine officer at Goose Green, who surrendered the garrison to Keeble; naval-history.net gives his rank as group captain.
- **Carlos Daniel Esteban** (Argentine, first lieutenant, C Company, 25th Infantry Regiment): his company held parts of the line; he was flown back to Goose Green during the battle.
- **Roberto Estévez** (Argentine, lieutenant, platoon of C Company, 25th Infantry Regiment): led a counter-attack and was killed directing artillery fire; awarded Argentina's highest decoration for valour.

### The battle
The British kept their signals on Zulu time (GMT), as the Ministry of Defence's files of 1982 show; the islands kept local time, four hours behind. The sources read here mostly print bare times. Wikipedia, after Fitz-Gibbon, marks its 13:13 for Darwin Hill as local, and the others read as local too, since they put the start in darkness and the first stand at first light. C Company and the engineers left at 22:00 on 27 May to clear the route. The attack began at about 03:35 on the 28th, after a delay in bringing in *Arrow*'s fire. A Company took Burntside House, empty of Argentines, while B and D Companies went down the western side of the isthmus.

At first light the advance stopped. A Company was held under fire from the trenches on Darwin Hill and went to ground in the gorse gully; B Company was pinned on the bare slope short of Boca House. Wikipedia has A Company pinned from 09:00 to 10:00 and a third attempt beaten back at about 10:30. Farrar-Hockley puts the fight on the hill at something over three hours, against a company of 92 men with snipers and fixed-line machine guns. A sniper well back from the position, by Corporal David Abols's account, did much to hold the attack.

Jones came forward to the gully. He led a charge up a re-entrant, in which his adjutant Captain David Wood, A Company's second-in-command Captain Chris Dent and Corporal David Hardman were killed. He then went on alone at a trench and was shot from another he had passed. As he lay dying his men called for a helicopter. Two Scouts set out from Camilla Creek House; Pucarás caught them about five minutes after take-off, and shot down Lieutenant Richard Nunn's, killing him. Naval-history.net puts the loss at about 11:55.

A Company closed with the trenches using light anti-tank rockets and machine guns, and the Argentine company surrendered. A Company reported Darwin Hill taken at 13:13. B Company, with Milan, took Boca House at 13:47. Keeble pushed on towards Goose Green. In the afternoon D Company's 12 Platoon met fire near the airfield in which Lieutenant Jim Barry and two others were killed. Argentine aircraft attacked; a Blowpipe brought down an MB-339 at about 17:00. After last light Argentine helicopters landed a company of the 12th Regiment south of Goose Green, and J Company, 42 Commando, flew in to reinforce 2 Para.

In the night Keeble sent terms by two Argentine prisoners, and by CB radio through the Goose Green farm manager, with a deadline of 08:30 local. On 29 May Pedrozo agreed to surrender. Piaggi burned his regiment's colours, and the islanders were set free. Freedman's official history counts 961 prisoners, and says the count may not have been exact.

The battalion's dressing station dealt with nearly 80 British casualties on the 28th. The chief naval medical officer at San Carlos recorded 47 seriously wounded paratroopers who needed surgery under general anaesthetic, and the worst went on to the hospital ship *Uganda*. On Darwin Hill A Company's sergeant major, Colin Price, tended British and Argentine wounded together in the gully. The Argentine Army records its own helicopters landing at Goose Green under fire to take wounded out.

### Where the accounts disagree
- **The clock.** Wikipedia labels only two of its times; naval-history.net and the Argentine Army labels none. The Argentine Army's times (06:00 for Estévez's counter-attack, 08:30 for the withdrawal to the settlement, 12:30 for the final attack) do not match the British sequence, and its 2019 account dates the fighting to 29 May and the ceasefire to 11:00 on 30 May.
- **Argentine strength.** Piaggi, as Fitz-Gibbon and Wikipedia give him, had 1,083 men in all. The Argentine Army gives 643 in the task force plus 202 of the Air Force; Wikipedia's table gives 903. Forces News says about 1,200.
- **British dead.** Van der Bijl gives 18: 16 paratroopers, a Royal Marine pilot and a sapper. ParaData lists 15 of 2 Para killed on 28 May, naming Nunn and Corporal Mick Melia, RE, apart; naval-history.net and Forces News give 17.
- **British wounded.** Wikipedia's infobox gives 64; Van der Bijl 33 paratroopers and a Royal Marine; naval-history.net 30 to 40. The dressing station dealt with nearly 80 casualties.
- **Argentine dead and wounded.** Freedman gives 45, Boyce 55, Frontera 57; the Argentine Army names 12 dead of the 25th Regiment and 35 of the 12th. Wounded run from 86 to 145.
- **Who shot Jones.** Snow and Snow point to Corporal Osvaldo Olmos; Bicheno to Corporal José Luis Ríos. Two conscripts of the 25th Regiment said that one of them shot him; Sergeant Barry Norman, beside him, thought it was a machine gun.
- **Jones's charge.** His citation calls it an action of the utmost gallantry, and Margaret Thatcher called his death the turning point of the battle. Fitz-Gibbon argues that Jones did more to hinder than help, by not letting his company commanders fight their own battle.
- **Barry's death.** Private John Graham says Barry went forward under a white flag and was fired on. Sub-Lieutenant Gómez-Centurión, the Argentine officer there, describes a British advance and makes no mention of a flag.
- **The islanders.** MercoPress gives about 100 held in the Social Club for almost seven weeks; Wikipedia says over a hundred in the community hall.
