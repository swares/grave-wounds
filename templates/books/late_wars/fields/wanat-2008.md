### The campaign
American troops came to the valleys of Kunar and Nuristan, on the Pakistan border, in strength in April 2006. In Operation Mountain Lion the 3rd Brigade Combat Team, 10th Mountain Division (Task Force Spartan) moved in, and its 1st Battalion, 32nd Infantry built outposts in the Pech, Korengal and Waygal valleys. In the Waygal, Lieutenant Colonel Christopher Cavoli put Combat Outpost Ranch House near Aranas and Combat Outpost Bella near the hamlet of that name, each held by half a platoon; the Combat Studies Institute (CSI) says Bella was picked partly because a medical clinic run by a charity stood there. In July 2006 troops of the 3rd Squadron, 71st Cavalry also landed at Kamdesh, to the north, where the base later named Keating grew up. In September and October 2006 American engineers, with about 50 local labourers, built a new bridge at Wanat. Ambushes and roadside bombs killed six Americans in the Waygal that year, among them Captain Douglas Sloan.

In May 2007 the 2nd Battalion (Airborne), 503rd Infantry of the 173rd Airborne Brigade, Task Force Rock, took over, with Chosen (C) Company in the Waygal. Bella could be reached only by helicopter, 16 km from Forward Operating Base Blessing on the Pech. In January 2008 Sergeant First Class Matthew Kahler of Chosen Company was shot dead by an Afghan soldier; Wikipedia says the death was ruled an accident. A new district centre was finished at Wanat in June 2008. Through June and early July Bella was harassed and there were reports of fighters massing. On 4 July an American helicopter attacked vehicles near Bella that it said were firing on it; 17 people were killed, and Wikipedia gives reports that they were civilians, among them the staff of a clinic.

Chosen Company was near the end of its tour, and Bella was to be given up. In Operation Rock Move, on the nights of 8 and 9 July, 1st Platoon was flown out of Bella, and 2nd Platoon of Chosen Company drove up from Blessing to build a new base beside the district centre at Wanat, about 8 km away. It was named Vehicle Patrol Base Kahler. With the paratroopers came engineers, Marine trainers and a platoon of the Afghan National Army. The engineers' Bobcat loader broke down for a day, so the barriers were about 4 feet high, not the 7 intended, and much of the digging was done by hand. The Afghan contractor's heavy equipment was due on 13 July.

### The places
- **Vehicle Patrol Base Kahler**: the new base, an open field about the size of a football pitch beside the road and the district centre.
- **Observation Post Topside**: a post on the terraces east of the base, held by nine men; it had a northern and a southern fighting position.
- **The draw**: a brush-filled gully running past the north side of Topside, about 10 yards from the post.
- **The bazaar and the hotel**: north of the base; the first machine-gun burst came from a two-storey building on that side.
- **The mosque and the village**: Wanat's houses on the terraces to the west, from which the fighters also fired.
- **The traffic-control point**: at the base, where a casualty collection point was set up.
- **Forward Operating Base Blessing**: on the Pech near Nangalam, about 8 km away; its two 155 mm guns fired for Wanat, and the quick reaction force drove from there.
- **Combat Outpost Bella**: up the valley, given up in Operation Rock Move.
- **Camp Wright**: where the wounded were flown, and where the helicopters were refuelled and rearmed.

### The leaders
- **Jonathan Brostrom** (United States, 1st Lieutenant, 2nd Platoon): the platoon leader; killed taking ammunition up to Topside.
- **David Dzwik** (United States, Staff Sergeant): 2nd Platoon's platoon sergeant.
- **Matthew Myer** (United States, Captain, Chosen Company): commanded from the command post in the middle of the base.
- **Ryan Pitts** (United States, Sergeant, forward observer): directed the guns from Topside and held the post when he was the last man left there; Medal of Honor in 2014.
- **Jeffrey Scantlin** (United States, Specialist): a combat lifesaver who took over as medic when the medic was hit; Silver Star.
- **William Ostlund** (United States, Lieutenant Colonel, 2nd Battalion, 503rd Infantry): commanded Task Force Rock.
- **Charles Preysler** (United States, Colonel, 173rd Airborne Brigade): reprimanded in 2010 with Ostlund and Myer; the reprimands were withdrawn.
- **Dost Mohammad, Maulavi Uthman and Sadiq Munibullah** (Insurgents): named by Wikipedia as the attackers' leaders; nothing more about their parts is given in the sources read.

### The battle
Times here are local, Afghanistan time (GMT+4:30, with no summer time); the sources do not say which clock they kept, but they read as local time. The platoon stood to at 04:00 on 13 July. Through the TOW's thermal sight men saw figures on the high ground to the west, and Pitts and Sergeant Matthew Gobble began a request for artillery. At about 04:20 a machine gun opened from a two-storey building to the north, and Military Times says about 20 RPGs followed. The fire came from the bazaar, the hotel, the mosque, the village and the hills, and Wikipedia says about 100 more fighters came at Topside from the farmland to the east. The mortar pit was hit and the 120 mm mortar knocked out, its ammunition set off, and an RPG destroyed the TOW.

At Topside, fighters in the draw hit the post with rifles, RPGs and grenades, and the Army's narrative says all nine men there were wounded; Specialists Matthew Phillips and Gunnar Zwilling were killed. Pitts, hit in both legs and his left arm, threw grenades, fired the M240 and later an M203, and reported by radio to Myer while he called the guns from Blessing onto targets planned round the post. At about 04:45 Brostrom and Specialist Jason Hovater ran up from the base. They were killed, as were Corporals Jason Bogar and Pruitt Rainey. Pitts then found himself alone at the northern position. A second party, Staff Sergeant Sean Samaroo, Sergeant Israel Garcia and Specialists Michael Denton and Jacob Sones, reached the post and was caught by an explosion; Garcia died. Three times teams ran from the base through the fire to bring up ammunition and bring down the wounded.

The Apaches and an armed Predator arrived, by Wikipedia's account, about 30 minutes in. A danger-close gun run let a third party take the post. The 1st Platoon of Chosen Company drove up from Blessing as a quick reaction force and cleared fighters from the village and the hills. Blessing's guns fired 96 rounds. Later B-1B, A-10 and F-15E aircraft came on, and Wikipedia says the fighters drew off about four hours after the attack began. The base was given up a few days later.

The wounded were gathered at two casualty collection points: one at Topside's southern position, the other at the traffic-control point in the base. The platoon medic, Private First Class William Hewitt, was shot in the arm after about half an hour, and Scantlin, trained as a combat lifesaver, took over his work. RAND notes that most of the American losses came at Topside or on the runs to it to bring up ammunition and carry down the wounded. At about 06:15 a medical evacuation helicopter landed under fire and took out Pitts, Samaroo, Sones and Denton. The wounded were flown to Camp Wright.

### Where the accounts disagree
- **The Americans' strength.** The CSI foreword gives 49 Americans and 24 Afghan soldiers; RAND gives 48 soldiers, 3 Marines and 24 Afghans; the army.mil graphic gives 48 Americans. Ricks, writing of the CSI draft, speaks of 42 soldiers and 3 Marines.
- **The insurgents' strength.** The Army's narrative says about 200. Wikipedia says the Americans first thought 100 to 200, and that an Afghan defence official spoke of 400 to 500. Military Times says more than 100.
- **How long it lasted.** Wikipedia and the CSI speak of about four hours; RAND of nearly nine, until enough air strikes could be brought in; Military Times of about 90 minutes.
- **The air support.** Wikipedia has the Apaches and an armed Predator overhead about 30 minutes in. RAND says high winds grounded the unmanned aircraft. A commenter on Small Wars Journal (Cole) says the Apaches and medical evacuation helicopters came within the hour after a 29-minute flight. The game puts the Apaches at about 05:20, the medical evacuation helicopter at 05:52 to 06:05 and the quick reaction force at 06:01; the Army's narrative puts the evacuation of Pitts at about 06:15.
- **The danger-close strike.** The army.mil battle page puts it about 10 m from the Americans; the narrative on DVIDS says about 30 m.
- **The losses.** All give 9 Americans killed. The infobox and RAND give 27 wounded and 4 Afghans wounded; the Army's 2010 release says "27 casualties". The Americans claimed 21 to 65 fighters killed and 20 to 45 wounded, but Wikipedia says only two bodies were found. A Taliban claim reported in the *Pak Tribune* spoke of 20 Americans killed.
- **The province.** Wanat is in the Waygal district of Nuristan; the army.mil Medal of Honor page puts it in Kunar.
- **Who was to blame.** The first investigation (August 2008) found the fighters had help from the local police and a district leader. Ricks says the CSI draft (2009) found failures by the battalion and brigade commanders; Wikipedia says the final study (2010) laid the weight on the uncertainty of war, junior officers' mistakes and a poor grasp of local politics and intelligence. After a Central Command review under Lieutenant General Richard Natonski, General Charles Campbell began adverse action against Preysler, Ostlund and Myer in March 2010; in June he found them neither negligent nor derelict and withdrew it.
