### The campaign
After the landings at San Carlos on 21 May 1982 and the fight at Goose Green on the 28th, the British closed on Stanley from the west. On the night of 11-12 June 3 Commando Brigade took Mount Longdon, Two Sisters and Mount Harriet. The second night's attacks, on 13-14 June, were on Mount Tumbledown and Mount William, by 5th Infantry Brigade, and on Wireless Ridge, by 2 Para. 5th Infantry Brigade had been built round two Guards battalions taken off public duties in London and a battalion of Gurkhas.

The 2nd Battalion, Scots Guards, under Lieutenant Colonel Michael Scott, had come ashore at Bluff Cove. On the morning of 13 June it was flown by helicopter to the western end of Goat Ridge, and was shelled there for most of the day. Wikipedia gives it 641 men, with four light tanks of the Blues and Royals; no source read gives the companies' strengths.

Against it stood the Argentine 5th Marine Infantry Battalion (BIM 5) under Capitán de Fragata Carlos Hugo Robacio, flown in from Río Grande in April. It was a cold-weather battalion, trained for night fighting and better clothed than the army's conscripts, though about 70% of the Marine Infantry's men were conscripts too. Its companies held Tumbledown, Mount William and Sapper Hill; Wikipedia gives it 700 marines and 200 soldiers. On Tumbledown stood N Company, "Nácar", under Capitán Eduardo Villarraza, with platoons of the army's 4th and 12th Infantry Regiments beside it.

### The places
- **Mount Tumbledown**: a long rocky ridge about 7 km west of Stanley, running west to east, with a western end, the summit crags in the middle and an eastern end. Its summit is about 212 m high.
- **Goat Ridge**: the lower ridge to the west, where the Scots Guards gathered on 13 June.
- **Moody Brook**: the valley below the northern slopes, with the old Royal Marines barracks.
- **Mount William**: the height south-east of Tumbledown across a saddle, taken by the Gurkhas on the 14th.
- **Sapper Hill**: the last height before Stanley, where BIM 5 fell back on the morning of the 14th.
- **The Fitzroy-Stanley track**: the track south of the mountain, along which the diversion went in.
- **Stanley** (Puerto Argentino): the islands' capital, where the Argentine garrison surrendered on 14 June.

### The leaders
- **Michael Scott** (British, lieutenant colonel, 2nd Scots Guards): chose a silent attack along the ridge from the west, the open southern slopes being "far too hazardous". He received the DSO.
- **John Kiszely** (British, major, Left Flank): led the bayonet assault onto the summit.
- **Iain Dalzel-Job** (British, major, G Company): took the western end.
- **Simon Price** (British, major, Right Flank): went round Left Flank to clear the eastern end.
- **Richard Bethell** (British, major): led the diversion on the south, and was wounded.
- **Robert Lawrence** (British, lieutenant, Right Flank): shot in the head at the moment his platoon's charge succeeded.
- **Carlos Hugo Robacio** (Argentine, capitán de fragata, BIM 5): meant to counter-attack and fight on, and was ordered back to Sapper Hill.
- **Eduardo Villarraza** (Argentine, capitán, N Company): held Tumbledown and Mount William.
- **Carlos Daniel Vázquez** (Argentine, teniente de corbeta, 4th Platoon): held near the summit covering the western approaches, with 40 men.

### The battle
The British accounts give local time, four hours behind Greenwich; the Argentine Ministry of Defence's times run one hour ahead of them throughout, which fits Buenos Aires time, though no source says so. Local time is used here. It was dark from before five in the afternoon, and snowing. The moon, a little over half lit, did not rise until 22:45.

At about 20:30 Major Bethell's party, about 30 men of the Reconnaissance Platoon and the Pipes and Drums with the four light tanks, went along the track towards Mount William to draw the defenders' eyes south. Lance Corporal John Pashley, a sapper at the head of the party, was killed outright when a machine gun opened at about 20 m, and Drill Sergeant Danny Wight was killed too. After a fire-fight of about two hours the party withdrew through an unmarked minefield, where a light tank was disabled.

At 21:00 G Company moved off along the ridge. It found the western end empty and held it by 22:30. Left Flank passed through and was stopped by machine guns and snipers in the summit crags. The snipers had night sights; within minutes they killed three men and wounded two. The light rockets did little against the bunkers, and 13 Platoon worked forward among the rocks with grenades. For four hours Left Flank could not get on, and Scott thought of pulling back to attack the next night. Vázquez called his own artillery down on his position more than once. About 02:00, under snow, an Argentine counter-attack by La Madrid's army platoon and Miño's engineers went in and was driven back with five killed.

At 02:30 the British guns could at last fire ahead of 15 Platoon, and Kiszely led Left Flank in with the bayonet. He shot two men and bayoneted a third; a round lodged in his compass. He reached the summit with six men, three of whom were hit at once. Right Flank then went round Left Flank and cleared the eastern end with rockets, grenades and bayonets. Lawrence's platoon made a flanking move and charged; he was shot in the head on the eastern slopes. The Argentine account has the eastern part of the hill falling at 05:45 local.

At about 07:15 the order came from Stanley for BIM 5 to fall back to Sapper Hill; Robacio and Major Oscar Jaimet, commanding the army company in reserve, had meant to counter-attack. Vázquez's last trench held out until about then. By about 08:00 the mountain was in the Scots Guards' hands, well after dawn. The Gurkhas went on to Mount William, and O Company of BIM 5 covered the withdrawal to Sapper Hill. The garrison of Stanley was ordered to lay down its arms at noon on 14 June, and BIM 5 marched into the town in formation with its weapons.

The Scots Guards lost eight men killed, with Pashley of the Royal Engineers, and about 43 wounded; Left Flank alone lost seven killed and 21 wounded in its seven hours among the crags. The Argentine Ministry of Defence gives BIM 5's loss in the whole battle for its positions as 16 killed and 45 wounded. Freedman, as Wikipedia cites him, has the British finding 30 Argentine dead on Tumbledown and taking about 30 prisoners; those dead may include the army platoons. On 14 June 165 Argentine wounded were flown to the hospital ship *Almirante Irízar*, many with open fractures from shell fragments.

### Where the accounts disagree
- **The clock.** No source read names its time zone except one Wikipedia time marked local. The British times are read as local (UTC−4) and the Argentine Ministry's as Buenos Aires time (UTC−3), from the light and the steady one-hour offset between them. No British time in Zulu was found.
- **The official history.** Freedman's official history was not reached, except for the one page Wikipedia cites; the British figures here have not been checked against it.
- **The Argentine dead.** 16 for BIM 5 (the Ministry of Defence and Corbacho); 30 found on the mountain (Freedman, as cited); 30 dead and 105 wounded (Radio Gráfica). Argentine wounded run from 45 to 100.
- **The British dead and wounded.** Eight or nine killed, as Pashley is counted or not; 41 or 43 wounded; about 60 wounded across all the British units on Tumbledown and Mount William (Wikipedia's infobox).
- **The diversion.** Four wounded and four more in the minefield (HistoryNet), or 16 more (a television interview, as Wikipedia cites it). Wikipedia's article on BIM 5 dates it to 12 June; the others to the 13th.
- **Lawrence's platoon.** Wikipedia has him leading 3 Platoon in the flanking move; naval-history.net has 3 Platoon giving covering fire while 1 Platoon and Lawrence's platoon assaulted.
- **The heavy weapons.** Wikipedia gives BIM 5 a company of 27 heavy machine guns; the Ministry of Defence puts a heavy machine-gun company at Mount Longdon. How many stood on Tumbledown, and where, is not known. Only HistoryNet speaks of 106 mm weapons, which it calls mortars.
- **The ships.** HMS *Active* and HMS *Avenger* gave naval gunfire by naval-history.net; *Yarmouth* and *Active* by HistoryNet.
- **How long it lasted.** Eleven hours (Hastings and Jenkins, as Corbacho cites them); some twelve (HistoryNet); eight and a half for Vázquez's section, by his own account.

!!! story "Guardsman Reynolds"
    James Reynolds was 19, a guardsman in Left Flank. When his platoon sergeant was killed among
    the crags he took over, and silenced the snipers who had been holding up the platoon. Then he
    went forward to a wounded man. He was hit in the hand, and went on helping. He was killed by
    mortar fire. He was awarded the Distinguished Conduct Medal after his death, and his name is
    one of the eight on the Scots Guards' memorial on Tumbledown.

!!! gm "On the field map"
    The engine does not yet fight this battle as it went. Played by the enemy commander on both
    sides, the Scots Guards lose every run: shelled by the mortars and under fire they cannot
    answer in the dark, the companies break long before they close, where the real Left Flank lay
    pinned below the summit for three hours and then went in with the bayonet. A player who keeps
    his companies in hand, and closes, can do better than the commander.
