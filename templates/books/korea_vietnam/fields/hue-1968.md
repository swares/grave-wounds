### The campaign
Many ARVN soldiers were on leave for the Tet truce of 1968. Shortly after midnight on 30 January attacks went in early in parts of I and II Corps, and the truce was cancelled. At about 03:00 on 31 January the main offensive struck: by one count about 77,000 North Vietnamese and NLF (Viet Cong) troops, against more than 100 towns and cities, among them 36 of the 44 provincial capitals. Hanoi hoped for a general uprising. It did not come.

In October 1967 Major General Trần Văn Quang, commanding the Tri-Thien-Hue region, was ordered to take Hue at Tet; Camp says he asked for more time and was refused. A Hue City Front was formed with two wings. The 6th Regiment took the Citadel, north of the Perfume River. The 4th Regiment took the new city to the south, with the provincial headquarters, the prison and the MACV compound as its objectives. Sappers came in on 28-30 January dressed as peasants. The occupiers carried lists of officials, officers and others to be rounded up. When no uprising came, Bowden writes, the aim became to stay close to the Americans, so that they could not use their firepower freely.

Early on 31 January they took most of the city. The ARVN 1st Division held its headquarters at Mang Ca, in the Citadel, and about 100 American advisers held the MACV compound south of the river. That morning A Company, 1st Battalion, 1st Marines was sent up Highway 1 from Phu Bai. It was ambushed at the edge of the city and its captain, Gordon Batcheller, was badly wounded; Gunnery Sergeant John Canley took over. Lieutenant Colonel Marcus Gravel came up with a relief force and reached the compound at about 15:15. An attempt that afternoon to cross the bridge into the Citadel was driven back. The two Marine companies lost 10 killed and 56 wounded that day.

Over the next three days 2/5 Marines came in: F Company by helicopter on 1 February, H Company by convoy on the 2nd, and the battalion commander, Lieutenant Colonel Ernest Cheatham, with Colonel Stanley Hughes of the 1st Marines on the 3rd. The university was taken on 2 February. Hughes gave 1/1 the task of keeping Highway 1 open and 2/5 the task of clearing the blocks along the river bank. Cheatham was sent bazookas, 106 mm recoilless rifles on Mule carriers, flamethrowers and tear gas. On the evening of 3 February the limits on supporting fire south of the river were lifted.

### The places
- **The Truong Tien bridge** (Camp: the Nguyen Hoang bridge): the road bridge into the Citadel, which Gravel's men tried to cross on 31 January; blown on 7 February.
- **The MACV compound**: the advisers' walled compound, about 300 by 200 yards, a block and a half south of the river by Highway 1.
- **Hue University**: across from the compound, by the bridge; 2/5's command post and starting line.
- **The Treasury, the post office and the Public Health building**: the first strong points in 2/5's way, taken on 4 February.
- **The Jeanne d'Arc school**: the Catholic school and chapel cleared by A Company, 1/1 on 4 February.
- **The hospital**: a complex of buildings, taken on 5 and 6 February.
- **The provincial headquarters**: a two-storey building inside walls, the 4th Regiment's command post, taken on 6 February.
- **The An Cuu bridge and Phu Bai**: the bridge on Highway 1 over the Phu Cam canal, blown on the night of 4 February, and the Marine base to the south from which the reinforcements came and the wounded went back.

### The leaders
- **Foster C. LaHue** (US, Task Force X-Ray): brigadier general; he wrote that the first moves were made "with limited information".
- **Stanley S. Hughes** (US, 1st Marines): colonel in charge of the fighting south of the river from 3 February.
- **Ernest C. Cheatham** (US, 2/5 Marines): lieutenant colonel; he marked targets for the 106 mm guns with tracer himself.
- **Marcus Gravel** (US, 1/1 Marines): lieutenant colonel; reached the MACV compound on 31 January.
- **Ronald Christmas** (US, H Company, 2/5): captain; he received the Navy Cross for 5 February at the hospital, where he directed a tank's fire from on top of it.
- **John L. Canley** and **Alfredo Gonzalez** (US, A Company, 1/1): gunnery sergeant and sergeant; Gonzalez was killed at the Jeanne d'Arc school on 4 February.
- **Frank A. Thomas Jr.** (US, gunnery sergeant, H Company): Nolan has him raise the flag over the provincial headquarters.
- **Trần Văn Quang** (North Vietnamese, Tri-Thien-Hue region): major general who planned the attack on Hue.
- **Nguyen Trong Dan** (North Vietnamese, 6th Regiment): lieutenant colonel commanding in the Citadel. Camp names the 4th Regiment's commander, in the new city, only as "Lieutenant Colonel Nguyen Van".

### The battle
Times are local, as the American accounts give them. At about 07:00 on 4 February 2/5 went for the Treasury. A 106 mm gun on a Mule knocked out a heavy machine gun while Cheatham marked the target with tracer. H Company took the Public Health building and fired from it, with a 106 mm in a second-floor room. Captain Michael Downs's F Company attacked from a Catholic school across the street, and Lance Corporal Richard Carter fired more than 100 rockets from a 3.5-inch launcher into gates and walls. Gas from E-8 launchers, rigged by the battalion's executive officer, helped clear the Treasury, and a tank came up in support. By about 17:00 the Treasury, the post office and the Public Health building were held. Gas thrown down a bunker by the post office that evening left 24 North Vietnamese dead inside.

Next door A Company, 1/1 cleared the Jeanne d'Arc school and its chapel. Some of the Treasury gas drifted over them; they had no masks. Gonzalez was killed by a rocket. More than half of the company's 147 men were killed or wounded that day. That night sappers blew the An Cuu bridge, cutting the road to Phu Bai.

On 5 February 2/5 pushed on to the hospital. By the afternoon the Marines were inside the complex, fighting room by room with an Ontos in support, and the first building was cleared at about 16:30. Christmas crossed open ground to a tank hit by two rockets and directed its fire standing on it. The province chief, Lieutenant Colonel Phạm Văn Khoa, was found in the hospital.

On 6 February the hospital was cleared and H Company went for the provincial headquarters. Gas blew away on the river breeze; a tank was hit by two rockets and kept going. An Ontos moving up to blast the wall was knocked out by a B-40. A 106 mm on a Mule breached the building, and the Marines cleared it room by room, then the spider holes round it. Nolan says that a little after 14:00 Gunnery Sergeant Thomas and two others pulled down the North Vietnamese flag and raised an American one. South Vietnamese law did not allow it to fly alone, and it came down the next morning. The prison nearby was found empty. Hanger says resistance in the southern city largely collapsed once the headquarters fell.

The wounded were dragged back across the streets by those less badly hurt, or brought in by tanks. On 31 January the advisers' dispensary took the first casualties, abandoned civilian vehicles served as ambulances, and the worst wounded went out by truck or, at night, by a Marine CH-46 that landed in the fog. Later, wounded men such as Carter were flown to Phu Bai by helicopter, though the low cloud often kept aircraft away. Hanger counts 38 Marines killed and 320 wounded in clearing the new city. During the occupation the PAVN and NLF rounded up and killed civilians and prisoners, many of them named on their lists; almost 2,800 bodies were recovered from mass graves in 1968 and 1969, and estimates of the dead run from several hundred (Porter) to about 6,000 (Wikipedia, after Pike, Vennema and others).

### Where the accounts disagree
- **The clock.** South Vietnam kept GMT+8 from 1960 to 1975 and North Vietnam GMT+7 from 1 January 1968 (Wikipedia, "Time in Vietnam"). The American times are taken here as South Vietnamese local time; no account says so.
- **The signal.** Wikipedia, after Pike, has a flare start the attack at 02:33 on 31 January; Camp has the flare and the rockets at about 03:40, which Wikipedia, after Villard, gives as the time the rockets hit the new city.
- **The flag.** Nolan has Thomas and two other Marines raise it a little after 14:00, on Christmas's order. Gurske, writing a day-by-day account online, has Privates First Class Walter Kaczmarek and Alan McDonald pull down the Viet Cong flag at 16:03, another Marine raising the American one. Wikipedia, after Shulimson, says it was ordered down for want of a South Vietnamese flag beside it.
- **Who took the Treasury.** Nolan has F Company assault the Treasury with H Company firing from the Public Health building. Gurske has H Company open the attack on the Treasury at 07:00 and F Company clear it. Hanger has the Treasury and post office taken on 3 February and given up for want of men, before the attack of the 4th.
- **The province chief.** Wikipedia, after Bowden, says the Marines rescued Phạm Văn Khoa at the hospital. Gurske says they doubted his story at first and held him.
- **The losses on 6 February.** Nolan has two Marines killed at the provincial headquarters; Gurske, one killed in the whole southern sector that day.
- **The whole battle.** Bowden, as reviewed in *Studies in Intelligence*, gives 250 Americans killed and 1,554 wounded, 458 ARVN killed and about 2,700 wounded, 2,400 to 5,000 North Vietnamese and NLF dead, and about 8,000 civilian dead. Wikipedia gives the North Vietnamese and NLF dead as 1,042 to 5,133.
- **Canley's award.** Camp gives Canley the Navy Cross; Wikipedia says he and Gonzalez both received the Medal of Honor.
- **The enemy units.** Camp gives the 4th Regiment the 804th and K4B Battalions; Wikipedia, after Villard, names the 804th and 810th, and after Bowden the 815th, which stopped the Marines on 2 February.

    the 4th Regiment had used as its command post. A flag of the other side flew
