### The campaign
Khe Sanh combat base stood on a plateau in the far north-west of South Vietnam, near the border with Laos and below the Demilitarized Zone. Colonel David E. Lownds took over the 26th Marines there in August 1967. In early January 1968 American intelligence reported two, perhaps three, North Vietnamese divisions moving into the area: the 325C Division to the north and the 304th to the south. On 16 and 17 January Lieutenant Colonel Heath's 2nd Battalion, 26th Marines flew in and took post on Hill 558, north of the base.

The siege began on the night of 20/21 January. About 300 North Vietnamese attacked K Company, 3/26 Marines on Hill 861; they broke into the position and were driven out by 05:30, leaving 47 dead. At 05:30 on 21 January the shelling of the base began. One of the first rounds set off the main ammunition dump, which held more than 1,500 tons. Khe Sanh village was attacked the same day and given up. The Marines lost 14 killed and 43 wounded that day.

From then on the base and its hill outposts lived on what came by air: C-130s and C-123s onto the strip or by parachute, and helicopters out to the hills. On 22 January E Company, 2/26 moved from Hill 558 to a ridge north-east of Hill 861, which the Marines called 861 Alpha, to cover the battalion's flank. On the night of 30/31 January the Tet Offensive began across South Vietnam, but the expected main blow at Khe Sanh did not come.

### The places
- **Khe Sanh combat base**: the airstrip, bunkers and guns on the plateau, held by the 26th Marines, 1/9 Marines from 22 January and the 37th ARVN Ranger Battalion from 27 January.
- **Hill 861A**: the knoll north-east of Hill 861 held by E Company, 2/26 from 22 January; it closed the gap in observation between Hill 861 and the battalion on Hill 558.
- **Hill 861**: the outpost of K Company, 3/26, attacked on the first night of the siege.
- **Hill 558**: north of the base, covering a valley along the Rao Quan river; 2/26's main position.
- **Hill 881 South**: a Marine outpost held by I Company, 3/26, with three 105 mm howitzers.
- **Hill 881 North**: the North Vietnamese-held twin of 881 South, retaken on 14 April.
- **Hill 950**: a Marine outpost from 1966 to 1969; men of 1/26 held it through the siege.
- **Khe Sanh village**: the village by the base, attacked and given up on 21 January.
- **Lang Vei**: the Special Forces camp, held by Americans and Bru irregulars, overrun by tanks on 7 February.

### The leaders
- **David E. Lownds** (US, 26th Marines): colonel commanding the base. He ordered a red alert when 861A was attacked.
- **Heath** (US, 2/26 Marines): lieutenant colonel; his battalion held Hill 558 and lent E Company to 861A. The sources read give no first name.
- **Earle G. Breeding** (US, E Company, 2/26): captain. He shifted his men to stiffen the broken line and called artillery and mortar fire close round the hill; he received the Silver Star.
- **Donald E. R. Shanley** (US, 1st Platoon, E Company): first lieutenant. He pulled his platoon back to its alternate positions and led the counter-attack at 05:00.
- **John A. Hennelly** (US, 1st Battalion, 13th Marines): lieutenant colonel commanding the base's artillery battalion.
- **Mirza "Harry" Baig** (US, 26th Marines): captain. He later judged that the artillery had kept the attackers' reserves away from the hill.
- **Tom Eichler** (US, corporal, E Company): Fixler says he killed three men hand to hand and destroyed a machine gun inside the wire; he received the Silver Star.
- **Trần Quý Hai** (North Vietnamese, local commander): major general commanding the forces round Khe Sanh, with **Lê Quang Đạo** as political commissar.
- **Võ Nguyên Giáp** (North Vietnamese): Wikipedia names him the theatre commander.

### The battle
Times are local, as the American accounts give them. Shortly after 03:00 on 5 February the North Vietnamese opened on the combat base with rockets, guns and mortars. Soon after, Captain Breeding reported about 200 North Vietnamese coming through the wire on top of Hill 861A. Sappers had cut lanes, and the infantry came up behind them under mortar and small arms fire. There were no sensors near the hill to give warning. Lownds put the whole regiment on red alert, and the 1st Battalion, 13th Marines answered with a heavy barrage round the hill.

The attackers broke into the 1st Platoon's sector on the north of the perimeter. Lieutenant Shanley drew his platoon back to its alternate positions, and the North Vietnamese held part of the trench line. The Marines threw CS gas grenades; Fixler, a lance corporal in the company, remembers the gas blowing back over them, so that men fought in masks and could hardly see. At about 05:00 Shanley led the platoon out of its holes in a counter-attack. The fighting was hand to hand, with knives, bayonets, rifle butts and fists. The North Vietnamese fell back, gathered again, and made a second, half-hearted attack, which was beaten off before dawn.

Through the night the base's guns fired round the hill. Baig later counted almost 2,000 rounds from the 1st Battalion, 13th Marines alone, and thought this fire had stopped the North Vietnamese reserves from joining the attack. Fixler says the Marines were also hit by their own shells more than once.

At first light a sweep of the slope and the perimeter found more than 100 North Vietnamese dead. E Company lost 7 killed and 24 wounded (Shulimson). Fixler describes the wounded patched up by the corpsmen, wrapped in ponchos and carried to a staging area by the landing zone, to be flown out by helicopter to a field hospital.

Two days later, on the night of 6/7 February, Lang Vei was overrun by PT-76 tanks and infantry. On 8 February a North Vietnamese battalion partly overran A Company, 1/9 Marines' outpost west of the base. The siege went on until Operation Pegasus reached the base on 8 April.

### Where the accounts disagree
- **The date.** Shulimson, the Khe Sanh Veterans' chronology and Fixler put the attack on 5 February. Wikipedia's article on the 325th Division gives 3 February.
- **The clock.** South Vietnam kept GMT+8 from 1960 to 1975; North Vietnam kept GMT+7 from 1 January 1968 (Wikipedia, "Time in Vietnam"). The American times here are taken to be South Vietnamese local time, though no account says so.
- **The hour.** The game opens at 03:05. Shulimson, as read for this page, says only that the shelling began around 03:00 and that Breeding's report came shortly after.
- **The attackers.** Breeding reported about 200 men. The Khe Sanh Veterans' chronology calls it a North Vietnamese battalion, and Wikipedia a battalion of the 325C Division. No account read for this page names the battalion or its regiment, and no North Vietnamese account of the fight was found in English.
- **The losses.** Shulimson gives E Company 7 dead and 24 wounded. Fixler gives 7 dead and at least 30 seriously wounded. Shulimson has more than 100 North Vietnamese dead counted; Fixler and the Khe Sanh Veterans' chronology give 109. Breeding's citation speaks of more than 100 killed or captured.
- **When E Company took the hill.** The Khe Sanh Veterans' chronology and Shore put the move to 861A on 22 January, about a week after 2/26 reached Hill 558. Wikipedia's article on the battle has E Company on 861A after 2/26 arrived. One sentence in Shulimson, as read for this page, seemed to say the company had occupied the hill that same morning; we could not check it against the printed page.
- **Where.** Breeding's citation places the action at "Hill 861"; the other accounts use 861A.

!!! story "Find my hand"
    Lance Corporal Barry Fixler of E Company was in the trenches on Hill 861A when the North
    Vietnamese came through the wire before dawn on 5 February. He later wrote that artillery,
    rockets, mortars, small arms, bayonets and tear gas all came at once, and that the gas blew
    back into the Marines' own faces. It took all night to clear the hill. The wounded were
    wrapped in ponchos and carried down to the landing zone for the helicopters. One of them asked
