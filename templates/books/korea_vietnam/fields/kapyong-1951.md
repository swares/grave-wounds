### The campaign
By April 1951 Ridgway's offensives had pushed the Chinese and North Koreans back across the 38th parallel. On 22 April Peng Dehuai opened the Chinese Spring Offensive, the first stage of the fifth phase campaign. It aimed to destroy the US I and IX Corps north of the Han and take Seoul. The 39th and 40th Armies were put under Song Shilun's 9th Army Group. Wen Yucheng, of the 40th Army, was given the task of destroying the ROK 6th Division and blocking the Kapyong valley against help.

The ROK 6th Division, under Major General Chang Do Yong, had stopped at 16:00 on 22 April to dig in. The 118th and 60th Divisions struck at 17:00 and slipped through the gaps, and by 23:00 Chang had lost touch with his regiments. The division broke, rallied and broke again, and its men streamed south down the Kapyong valley.

The 27th British Commonwealth Infantry Brigade was in IX Corps reserve near Kapyong. Brigadier Basil Coad had gone to Hong Kong on 23 March, and Brigadier Brian Burke commanded. The 1st Argyll and Sutherland Highlanders had already left for Pusan, so Burke had three battalions: the 3rd Battalion, Royal Australian Regiment (Lieutenant Colonel Bruce Ferguson), the 2nd Battalion, Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry (Lieutenant Colonel James Stone), and the 1st Battalion, Middlesex Regiment. With them were the 16th Field Regiment, Royal New Zealand Artillery, with 25-pounders; A Company of the US 72nd Heavy Tank Battalion with 15 Shermans; and two companies of the 2nd Chemical Mortar Battalion. On 23 April the brigade was sent forward to block the valley.

### The places
- **The Kapyong valley**: where a tributary joins the Kapyong River, less than 3 km wide at its widest by Veterans Affairs Canada's reckoning (the Australian War Memorial says 7 km), and about 30 km south of the front.
- **Hill 504**: the height east of the river held by 3 RAR. D Company held the summit, A Company the north-west spur and C Company a rear spur.
- **B Company's knoll**: a small hill by the river and road, below Hill 504.
- **Hill 677**: the height west of the river held by 2 PPCLI.
- **Sudok San (Hill 794)**: a height between the two battalions, left undefended.
- **Chuktun-ni**: the hamlet in the valley behind Hill 504, where 3 RAR's headquarters stood.
- **The ford**: where 3 RAR crossed the Kapyong River on the evening of 24 April to reach the Middlesex.
- **Kapyong**: the town to the south, on the road to Seoul.

### The leaders
- **Brian Burke** (UN, 27th British Commonwealth Infantry Brigade): ordered the Australians out on 24 April and told the Canadians by loudspeaker that they were on their own.
- **Bruce Ferguson** (UN, 3 RAR): withdrew his headquarters before dawn on 24 April and directed the battalion from the rear. He received the DSO.
- **Ben O'Dowd** (UN, A Company, 3 RAR): held the north-west spur and was left in charge on the hill.
- **Reg Saunders** (UN, C Company, 3 RAR): his company stood ready to reinforce A and B Companies.
- **James Stone** (UN, 2 PPCLI): refused to give up Hill 677 and held his fire on the attack on his headquarters until it was 40 m off.
- **Michael Levy** (UN, 10 Platoon, 2 PPCLI): when his platoon was nearly overrun, his request for gunfire on his own position went back through Captain J. G. W. Mills.
- **Kenneth W. Koch** (UN, A Company, 72nd Heavy Tank Battalion): his Shermans fought in the valley, carried supplies up and took out wounded.
- **Chang Do Yong** (ROK, 6th Division): his division's collapse opened the valley.
- **Wen Yucheng** (Chinese, 40th Army): charged with the drive down the valley.
- **Deng Yue** (Chinese, 118th Division): Wikipedia names him over the 118th and 60th Divisions, together perhaps 20,000 men.

### The battle
The sources do not say which clock they keep; the times here are taken as local. From about 20:00 on 23 April South Korean soldiers came back through the brigade, mostly through 3 RAR, with Chinese among them. At 21:30 the Chinese struck the forward platoon of tanks by the road; two tank commanders, fighting from open turrets, were killed, and the tanks fell back. At about 22:00 the 354th Regiment of the 118th Division reached 3 RAR. Two battalions went for A and B Companies. The third worked round the Australian left and reached the headquarters at Chuktun-ni. The 16th Field Regiment pulled back about 4 miles at about 03:00, and the 2nd Chemical Mortar Battalion's company with 3 RAR left its mortars and walked away before midnight.

A Company's 1 Platoon was attacked from 21:30, and its survivors fell back at about 01:00. At 00:50 the Chinese struck B Company's 4 Platoon and were beaten off after an hour; at 03:30 they broke into 6 Platoon and were thrown out with the tanks' help; a last attack at 04:45 failed. Between 05:00 and 06:00 Ferguson took his headquarters back down the valley, the Shermans escorting it. At dawn O'Dowd's 3 Platoon took back the lost position.

At 07:15 B Company was ordered off its knoll to join the rest of the battalion; at 09:30, the order rescinded, it went back, and Lieutenant Leonard Montgomerie's 4 Platoon took the knoll again with the bayonet. On the summit D Company's 12 Platoon beat off six attacks between 07:00 and 10:30, and more until 13:30. The Shermans brought food, water and ammunition up and took the wounded out. After 15:00 American Corsairs dropped napalm on D Company in error and killed two men.

At 14:30 Burke's order to withdraw was given out. The barrage meant to cover it was late, and the tanks fired over open sights instead. C Company went at 16:30 and A Company followed, down a ridge to the ford; D Company broke clean after dark. B Company carried the wounded, and Chinese prisoners were made to carry stretchers. 3 RAR passed through the Middlesex that evening.

At 22:00 on 24 April the Chinese turned on 2 PPCLI. Private Wayne Mitchell, wounded, charged them three times with his Bren gun. About 500 Chinese came at Stone's headquarters; he held fire until they were 40 m off, then opened with his half-tracks and mortars. At 01:10 on the 25th D Company's 10 Platoon was nearly overrun, and Mills asked for gunfire on his own position. The New Zealand guns fired on it while the men lay in their trenches. At dawn the attacks stopped. The battalion was short of food and ammunition. At 14:00 Canadian patrols found the road behind them open.

### Where the accounts disagree
- **The Chinese.** Mossman and Wikipedia name the 118th Division of the 40th Army and the 60th Division of the 20th Army. The Anzac Portal says the 19th Chinese Corps broke the ROK 6th Division, and gives 2,800 UN troops against the 118th Division's 10,600.
- **The scale of the offensive.** Wikipedia gives over 700,000 men in all, 337,000 in the western thrust. McGibbon says about 270,000 were set in motion.
- **The Australian losses.** Wikipedia and *The Canadian Encyclopedia* give 32 killed and 59 wounded; the Australian War Memorial 32 and 53; the Anzac Portal 32 killed, more than 50 wounded and 3 taken prisoner. Mossman gives 31 killed, 58 wounded and 3 missing.
- **The Canadian losses.** Veterans Affairs Canada, *The Canadian Encyclopedia* and Mossman give 10 killed and 23 wounded; Wikipedia gives 18 and 38.
- **The Chinese losses.** The Anzac Portal says over 2,000 lost to the Australians and Canadians; *The Canadian Encyclopedia* about 2,000 killed and wounded. Wikipedia's infobox gives about 6,000 killed, and cites Bjarnason for 4,000 killed by the New Zealand guns alone.
- **The gunfire on 10 Platoon.** *The Canadian Encyclopedia* says 2,300 rounds in under an hour. Wikipedia describes barrages of 15 to 20 minutes each.
- **The airdrop.** Mossman says C-119s from Japan dropped supplies about six hours after Stone asked. Wikipedia says no resupply reached the battalion. A veteran told Veterans Affairs Canada that some supplies were dropped.
- **The order of the withdrawal.** Wikipedia has C Company go first at 16:30, then A Company. The Memorial's anniversary blog says B Company left first, up Hill 504 and down the ridge.
- **When 3 RAR got clear.** The Anzac Portal has it inside the Middlesex lines at 19:30; Mossman shortly after dark; Wikipedia says 23:30.
- **Who flanked the headquarters.** The Anzac Portal has the 354th's third battalion go round the Australian left, the west of the hill, and the game follows it.
- **O'Dowd's rank.** The Memorial's exhibition calls him Major; its anniversary blog, Captain.

!!! story "The signaller from Cronulla"
    Private Horace "Bill" Madden was a signaller at 3 RAR's headquarters. Laying telephone line
    on 24 April, he was knocked out by a grenade, and later thrown into a weapon pit by another
    blast. He was taken in the withdrawal with Privates Robert Parker and Keith Gwyther, concussed.
    As a prisoner he would not cooperate. He was beaten, had ribs broken with a rifle butt, and was
    starved, yet shared the food he could buy with sick men. He died of malnutrition and ill-treatment
    in November 1951, aged 27, the only Australian to die in Chinese hands. He was given the George
    Cross after his death. The Anzac Portal gives his death as 9 November; Wikipedia, about the 6th.
