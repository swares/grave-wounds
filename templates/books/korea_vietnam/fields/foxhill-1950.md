### The campaign
After the Inchon landing and the breakout from the Pusan perimeter in September 1950, the United Nations armies went north to unite Korea before the year was out. The mountains split them. The Eighth Army went up the west side; X Corps, under Major General Edward Almond, and the South Korean I Corps went up the east. The 1st Marine Division, under Major General Oliver P. Smith, landed at Wonsan with X Corps and was sent inland up a single mountain road towards the Chosin (Changjin) Reservoir.

China had given warnings. The Chinese People's Volunteers began to cross the Yalu on 19 October, about 200,000 men, and opened their First Phase Offensive on 25 October. On 2 November the Marines met the Chinese 124th Division, which then drew back north. The 9th Army Group under Song Shilun (Sung Shih-lun to Simmons; Wikipedia calls it the 9th Corps) reached the reservoir about 17 November, with the 20th, 26th and 27th Armies, 11 or 12 divisions. Smith warned General Cates privately that his division could end up strung out along one mountain road. On 25 November, as the Chinese struck the Eighth Army on the Ch'ongch'on River, X Corps ordered the Marines west from Yudam-ni as the northern arm of a pincer.

On 27 November the 5th and 7th Marines were at Yudam-ni, the division base at Hagaru-ri to the south-east. Between them the road climbed through Toktong Pass. The 7th Marines ordered Captain William E. Barber's Fox Company, 2nd Battalion, 7th Marines, to hold the pass. It left Hagaru-ri at 15:00 in trucks lent by H Battery, 11th Marines, and arrived at 17:00. With sections of water-cooled machine guns and 81 mm mortars from the battalion's Weapons Company it was 240 officers and men, by Simmons' count. That night the 9th Army Group attacked all along the road. The 79th and 89th Divisions went for Yudam-ni; the 59th Division went round to cut the road at the pass. At Yudam-ni the thermometer fell to -20°F (about -29°C).

### The places
- **Yudam-ni**: the village west of the reservoir where the 5th and 7th Marines were attacked on 27 November.
- **Hagaru-ri**: the division base at the south end of the reservoir, with its howitzers and an airstrip that opened on 1 December.
- **Toktong Pass**: the pass on the road between them. Fox Company was to keep three miles of road open, starting four miles out of Hagaru-ri.
- **Fox Hill**: the hill at the middle of the pass, north of the road; one Marine called it a saddle-like hill to the right of the road. The 3rd Platoon held its crest.
- **The road**: below the hill to the south, where the headquarters and service platoon lay. The corpsmen used a Korean hut as a sick bay.
- **The high hill to the north**: the ground above Fox Hill from which the Chinese fired machine guns on 1 December.
- **The valley to the south**: across the road, from which the Chinese attacked on 30 November.
- **Hill 1419**: a height south of Yudam-ni, where C Company, 1st Battalion, 7th Marines was attacked on 28 November and which the 1st Battalion took on 1 December on its way to Fox Hill.

### The leaders
- **William E. Barber** (American, Fox Company): an Iwo Jima veteran, he had taken command on 7 November. He was wounded on the second night and went on commanding.
- **Robert C. McCarthy** (American, 3rd Platoon): his platoon held the crest and lost most of its forward squads in the first attack.
- **Elmo G. Peterson** (American, 2nd Platoon): held the left, the west side of the perimeter.
- **John M. Dunne** (American, 1st Platoon): held the right, the east side.
- **Raymond G. Davis** (American, 1st Battalion, 7th Marines): led the march over the mountains that reached Fox Hill on 2 December. He received the Medal of Honor.
- **Litzenberg** (American, 7th Marines): the regimental commander, who sent Fox Company to the pass and twice ordered relief attempts.
- **Randolph S. D. Lockwood** (American, 2nd Battalion, 7th Marines): Fox Company's battalion commander, at Hagaru-ri; his attempt to reach the pass got nowhere.
- **Oliver P. Smith** (American, 1st Marine Division): his aide-memoire is the basis of Rasula's account.
- **Song Shilun** (Chinese, 9th Army Group): commanded the Chinese attack at the reservoir.
- **59th Division** (Chinese, 20th Army): the division that attacked Fox Hill. No source read for this page names its commander or the regiments engaged.

### The battle
Times here are local, as the Marine records give them; none of the sources read says which clock, but Korea's standard time was GMT+9. Barber dug in on the hill at the midpoint of the pass. McCarthy's 3rd Platoon held the high ground at the centre, Peterson's 2nd Platoon the left and Dunne's 1st Platoon the right, with headquarters and the mortars near the road. At about 02:30 on 28 November the Chinese attacked from several sides at once. A company-sized attack overran McCarthy's two forward squads: of 35 men, 15 were killed, 9 wounded and 3 missing, and 8 fell back. The wing platoons held. Simmons credits much of this to three men: Private Hector A. Cafferata and a Private First Class Benson of the 2nd Platoon, and Private First Class Gerald J. Smith of the 1st. The Chinese got as far as the company's mortars, and the fighting was hand to hand until dawn, with the perimeter almost cut in two. By 06:30 they had been driven off. Fox Company had lost 20 killed, 54 wounded and 3 missing. Rasula's account counts about 450 Chinese dead.

On 28 November H Battery, 11th Marines at Hagaru-ri registered its howitzers on the ground round the hill. Australian F-51 Mustangs made a strike for the company, and Marine R5D transports dropped medical supplies and mortar bombs; fetching the drops cost two more wounded. The regiment ordered Fox Company to break out towards C Company, 1st Battalion, but with 54 wounded it could not move. Davis's battalion fought its way from Yudam-ni to C Company that day and was then called back.

The second attack came from 02:15 to 06:00 on 29 November. Five Marines were killed and 29 wounded, among them Barber, hit in the leg. At 10:30 a helicopter bringing radio batteries was hit by small-arms fire and left without taking out any wounded. That afternoon Flying Boxcars of the Combat Cargo Command dropped supplies some 600 yards off target. At 02:30 on 30 November the Chinese attacked again from across the valley to the south and were stopped by the machine guns and mortars. A second battery helicopter was hit at 14:30, and an evening drop landed up to 800 yards away. At 01:00 on 1 December the Chinese set up four machine guns on the high hill to the north; the artillery, aimed by the light of 81 mm illuminating rounds, knocked them out. Marine aircraft struck the hill at 10:30.

On 1 December Davis's 1st Battalion took Hill 1419. Wikipedia says the 59th Division put in its last five platoons against it. After dark the battalion set out across the mountains east of the road, over three ridges, in deep snow and a snowstorm, about 8 miles by Davis's citation. It made radio contact with Fox Company at 06:30 on 2 December and reached its lines at 11:25. Together they cleared the pass for the regiments coming down from Yudam-ni. Rasula's totals for Fox Company are 26 killed, 3 missing and 89 wounded; six of its seven officers had been hit. Barber's citation says only 82 of his men could walk off the hill.

The wounded lay in the corpsmen's hut. One, Corporal Howard W. Koone, was told he was third on the list for a helicopter; it never came. Barber himself refused morphine. Across the campaign, Wikipedia says, morphine syrettes had to be thawed in a corpsman's mouth and plasma froze. Fox Company's wounded went out with Davis's column, which reached Hagaru-ri on 4 December with 22 litter cases and many walking wounded. By the caption of a Marine Corps photograph, 4,312 wounded and frostbitten men were flown out from the airstrip there, by C-47s among others, before the division went south.

### Where the accounts disagree
- **When the first attack began.** Simmons and Rasula say about 02:30. *Armchair General*'s 2011 game article gives 02:07.
- **Fox Company's strength.** Simmons gives 240 with the attachments; Rasula about 220 and a mortar section; Barber's citation 220; Wikipedia's article on Barber 246. The game uses 240.
- **The Chinese strength.** Simmons calls the first attack company-sized; Rasula says two battalions; Barber's citation calls it of estimated regimental strength; Wikipedia's article on Barber says more than 1,400 men. The game uses about a company with others fed in.
- **The Chinese losses.** Simmons says Fox Company never knew, with guesses up to 500 on the first night. Rasula counts 450 dead that night and probably more than 1,000 in all, as does Wikipedia's article on Barber.
- **The first night's losses.** Simmons gives 20 killed and 54 wounded; Rasula adds 3 missing; Wikipedia's article on Barber says 20 killed and 45 wounded.
- **Barber's wound.** His citation says he was hit in the leg early on 29 November; Wikipedia's article says a ricochet lodged in his pelvis and broke the bone.
- **The relief.** Rasula, Wikipedia and Davis's citation put it on the morning of 2 December; *Armchair General* says 1 December. Davis's citation has his battalion reach Hagaru-ri on the morning of 4 December; Wikipedia's article on Barber, on the night of 3-4 December.
- **Benson's name.** Simmons calls him Private First Class Robert Benson. Cafferata's obituaries, as Wikipedia gives them, call him Kenneth Benson, later awarded the Silver Star.
- **The cold.** Simmons gives -20°F at Yudam-ni on the night of 27 November. Wikipedia says temperatures in the campaign fell to about -36°F.
- **The photograph.** The Marine Corps caption of the picture above dates the relief attempt 27 November; Fox Company was not cut off until early on 28 November, and Wikipedia puts the failed attempts on 29 November.

!!! story "Without his boots"
    Private Hector Cafferata, a reservist called up in September 1950, was in the 2nd Platoon
    on the west side of Fox Hill. When the Chinese came on in the dark of 28 November, the rest
    of his fire team was hit. By the account in his obituaries he could not find his boots or
    coat, and fought for hours in the snow without them. Kenneth Benson, blinded for a time by a
    grenade, kept reloading his rifle. His citation credits him with 15 enemy killed. Later a
    grenade fell into a shallow trench holding wounded Marines. Cafferata "seized the deadly
    missile in his right hand" and threw it; it burst and took off part of a finger. A sniper
    then wounded him. He reached Japan in December and the Navy hospital at St Albans in January.
