## Why the team marches {#frames}

Four reasons for a world-wars team to be in the line. A frame adds no rules: it says who pays, what the
team is after and what ends it, and uses the [patrons](rules.html#patrons), [pay](rules.html#rank) and
[work](rules.html#work) in the Rules. The history in each frame is sourced below. The goals, first jobs
and complications are ours, written for play.

### The Regulars, August 1914 {#frame-regulars}

The British Expeditionary Force that crossed to France in August 1914 was the Regular Army, filled out
with reservists called back to the colours: men who had served their years and gone home. It met the
German First Army at Mons on 23 August, and fell back from there to the Marne.

- **Who pays:** the King, a shilling a day for a private.
- **The goal:** do the job you were trained for, and see the war out, said to be over by Christmas.
- **What ends it:** a wound, capture, or the battalion worn down and filled with strangers.
- **First job:** the canal at Mons, and a day's digging before the enemy comes.
- **What goes wrong:** the order comes to retreat, and goes on for two weeks. Your boots give out on the cobbles. The reservists are not as fit as they were.
- **Maps:** Mons.

### A battalion from home {#frame-home}

Newfoundland, then a dominion of its own, raised a regiment of volunteers in 1914. Its first 500 sailed
that October. On the first day of the Somme, 1 July 1916, some 800 of its men went forward at
Beaumont-Hamel. The next morning 68 answered the roll call.

- **Who pays:** the government at home, and the town that sent you off.
- **The goal:** do the island proud, and come home with your friends.
- **What ends it:** the war, a wound, or the battalion being the men you joined with no longer.
- **First job:** the line in front of the village, and a raid to learn the ground.
- **What goes wrong:** the wire is not cut. Half the street you grew up on is in one company. The letters home are opened.
- **Maps:** Beaumont-Hamel.

### An army in retreat {#frame-retreat}

In October 1915 Austria-Hungary and Germany invaded Serbia from the north, and Bulgaria from the east.
The Serbian army fought its way back, then took the only road left: over the mountains of Montenegro and
Albania to the Adriatic, in winter, with the king, the government and thousands of civilians. The
survivors were taken off by the Allies to Corfu early in 1916.

- **Who pays:** the king's army, when it can, and the villages you pass, when they will.
- **The goal:** hold long enough for the army to get away, and reach the sea.
- **What ends it:** the coast, capture, or the cold.
- **First job:** a rearguard at a pass, until the column is through.
- **What goes wrong:** the snow closes the road. The oxen die, and the guns are left. The Albanian clans charge for passage.
- **Maps:** Serbia, 1915.

### Called up for the duration {#frame-duration}

On the day Britain declared war on Germany, 3 September 1939, Parliament made every man aged 18 to 41
liable to be called up, to serve for the duration of the war. The United States had begun its peacetime
draft in 1940. Most of the men who fought from El Alamein to Italy and the Pacific were conscripts.

- **Who pays:** the government, by rank, with something sent home to your family.
- **The goal:** get through it, and keep your section together.
- **What ends it:** the war, a wound bad enough to send you home, or a posting away.
- **First job:** the replacements' camp, and the truck up to a battalion you do not know.
- **What goes wrong:** you are posted to another unit just as you know your own. The leave is cancelled. The new officer has his own ideas.
- **Maps:** Snipe, Anzio, Cassino, Bougainville.
