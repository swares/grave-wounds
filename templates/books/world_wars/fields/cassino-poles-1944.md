### The campaign
After the March battle failed, General Sir Harold Alexander gathered his armies for one offensive along the whole front, Operation Diadem. It was timed to fall close to the landing in Normandy, so that the German divisions in Italy would be held there. Four corps were to attack: from right to left, the II Polish Corps and the British 13 Corps of the Eighth Army, then the French Expeditionary Corps and the US II Corps of the Fifth Army (Wikipedia). 13 Corps was to cross the Rapido in the valley below Monte Cassino; the Poles were to take the massif from the north, the task in which the Americans, the Indians and the New Zealanders had failed.

Lieutenant General Władysław Anders's corps was about 50,000 strong (Wikipedia). It held the right of 13 Corps with two infantry divisions, the 3rd Carpathian and the 5th Kresowa, of two brigades each, and an armoured brigade (Fisher). Opposite stood the 1st Parachute Division, which still held the abbey and the town. The US Army staff study of 1984 places the 3rd Parachute Regiment on Monte Cassino and the heights beside it, and the 100th Mountain Regiment round Monte Castellone and Colle Sant'Angelo.

At 23:00 on 11 May some 1,660 guns opened fire along the front, 1,060 of them on the Eighth Army's (Fisher). At 01:00 on 12 May the Poles went in.

### The places
- **Point 593**: the height at the north-west end of Snakeshead Ridge, about 1,000 yards north-west of the abbey; the Poles call it Monte Calvario.
- **Point 569**: the height beyond 593, taken and lost by the Carpathians on 12 May.
- **Phantom Ridge (Widmo)**: about 1,800 yards north-west of the abbey (Fisher), the Kresowa Division's objective.
- **Colle Sant'Angelo and Point 575**: the heights beyond Phantom Ridge, from which German fire swept the Polish attack.
- **Albaneta**: the farm below 593, and the ravine in front of it (the Gardziel, "the gullet") where the 1st Battalion was caught.
- **The Doctor's House**: a former hermitage about 250 metres behind the front, the forward dressing station (Polska Zbrojna).
- **Caira**: the village at the foot of the hills, with the forward surgical station.
- **The abbey**: in ruins since 15 February, still held by the paratroops.

### The leaders
- **Władysław Anders** (Allies, II Polish Corps): took the task, and at 14:00 on 12 May ordered his divisions back to their start line (Fisher).
- **Bronisław Duch** (Allies, 3rd Carpathian Division): his 1st Carpathian Rifle Brigade made the attack on 593 and Albaneta.
- **Nikodem Sulik** (Allies, 5th Kresowa Division): afterwards wrote of the shock to his men of losing close comrades and junior officers on Phantom Ridge (montecassino.info.pl).
- **Lieutenant Colonel Domoń** (Allies, Kresowa Division): wounded on Phantom Ridge about midday (Dookoła Rzeszy).
- **Sidney Kirkman** (Allies, 13 Corps): his divisions fought across the Rapido below the Poles.
- **Richard Heidrich** (German, 1st Parachute Division): held the massif and the town.
- **Ludwig Heilmann** (German, 3rd Parachute Regiment): commanded the regiment from 15 November 1943 (Traces of War); its 1st Battalion made the counter-attacks on 593.
- **Valentin Feurstein** (German, LI Mountain Corps): his corps held a line of about 80 kilometres on 10 May (Dookoła Rzeszy).

### The battle
The bombardment stunned the Germans only for a time. On the right the 2nd Battalion of the 1st Carpathian Rifle Brigade went up for Point 593 and took it, about 02:00 according to the Dookoła Rzeszy account and about 02:30 according to warhist.pl. Dookoła Rzeszy has Point 569 taken at 02:50, and reserves of I./Fallschirmjäger-Regiment 3 pushing back at the Poles from 03:15. At 04:28 the brigade's 1st Battalion was ordered into the ravine in front of Albaneta. Two companies got in and were stopped there by crossfire.

On the left the Kresowa Division moved off about 01:30 up the gully towards Phantom Ridge. Its sappers clearing the mines lost about 90% of their men (warhist.pl). The 15th Battalion reached Phantom Ridge about 02:30, having lost about a fifth of its strength, and the 18th Battalion came up by 06:00 into fire from bunkers. A German counter-attack from Albaneta at 11:30 failed; another at 13:00 drove the Poles back (Dookoła Rzeszy).

At daybreak the Poles were in full view of the German guns, mortars and Nebelwerfers on the heights round them and in the abbey. Fisher says their losses became so heavy that they could not stand against the counter-attacks, and that almost half the attackers were killed or wounded. On Point 593 the 2nd Battalion beat off attack after attack; Tadeusz Czerkawski, an ensign there, counted six (Radio Kraków). Point 569 was lost about 11:30. At 14:00 Anders ordered the corps back to its start line after dark. The last men left 593 at 19:30 (warhist.pl).

The wounded were passed back down the hill by hand. Stretcher-bearers stood about every 100 metres along the 2 kilometres from the Doctor's House to the surgical station at Caira, and the journey could take four hours. Dr Alfons Wawrzyniak's team at the Doctor's House treated more than 500 men in the first 24 hours of the attack on 593 (Polska Zbrojna). The corps attacked again on 17 May. That night Kesselring ordered the paratroops off the massif, and on the morning of 18 May a patrol of the 12th Podolian Lancers under 2nd Lieutenant Kazimierz Gurbiel raised the regiment's pennant on the ruins of the abbey (Radio Kraków).

### Where the accounts disagree
- **The clock.** None of the accounts says which clock it used. Italy and the German army had gone over to summer time, GMT+2, in April 1944 (tz database).
- **When 593 was lost.** warhist.pl has the Poles forced off 593 by the fifth counter-attack at about 08:40, and the last defender leave at 19:30. Dookoła Rzeszy has the Germans retake it at 19:00 with the assault guns of Sturmgeschütz-Kompanie 14. Fisher speaks only of a series of counter-attacks. Dookoła Rzeszy also dates the opening a day early, to the night of 10-11 May.
- **The last officer on 593.** warhist.pl names 2nd Lieutenant Romański. Other Polish accounts give the name as Radwański or Rynkiewicz.
- **The counter-attacks.** Polish reports spoke of a German battalion; Dookoła Rzeszy says it was a platoon.
- **The German regiments.** The 1984 staff study puts the 3rd Parachute Regiment on Monte Cassino and the 4th in the town. Wikipedia, citing Whiting, has the Poles attack "Heilmann's 4th Parachute Regiment"; Heilmann commanded the 3rd. Radio Kraków names the 1st Parachute Division and the 5th Mountain Division; Rickard names Kampfgruppe Ruffin in the mountains north-west of the abbey.
- **Who attacked where.** Fisher has the Carpathians on 593 and the Kresowa on Phantom Ridge. Radzilowski in *The Polish Weekly* (2024) gives it the other way round.
- **The losses.** Fisher says almost half the attackers were killed or wounded. dzieje.pl gives about 1,800 killed and wounded in the first attack; Monte Cassino Battlefield Tours says some companies lost 40% to 70%. For the whole battle Radio Kraków gives 923 killed, 2,931 wounded and 345 missing; warhist.pl, 924, 2,930 and 345; Wikipedia, citing Whiting, 281 officers and 3,503 other ranks.
- **The flag.** dzieje.pl and Lipinski's memoir put the pennant on the ruins at 09:50 on 18 May; Radio Kraków and Wikipedia say 10:20.

!!! story "Torn shirts"
    The company medics went up with the infantry, four to a company. Józef Brzeziński was one of
    them. On Point 593, Polska Zbrojna says, he was wounded three times and kept on, and saved almost
    40 men. On Phantom Ridge Ludwik Radziszewski was hit too, and he also carried on dressing the
    wounded; when his bandages ran out he tore up his shirt to make more. Both men were given the
    Virtuti Militari, Poland's highest military decoration. Behind them the Doctor's House,
    250 metres from the front, treated more than 500 men in the first day.
