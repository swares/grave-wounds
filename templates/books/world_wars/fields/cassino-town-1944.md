### The campaign
The New Zealand Corps' first attack failed on 18 February 1944. Freyberg planned the next one from the north, through the town of Cassino itself, after a bombing of the town as heavy as the one that had fallen on the abbey. Rain put it off for more than three weeks; the 6th New Zealand Brigade's attack, first set for 24 February, waited for weather fit for the bombers (Wikipedia, citing Puttick). On 2 March Major General Howard Kippenberger, commanding the New Zealand Division, lost both feet to a mine, and Brigadier G. B. Parkinson took over (NZHistory).

The Germans used the wait. The 1st Parachute Division under General Richard Heidrich took over from the 15th Panzer Grenadier Division. *The Tiger Triumphs* counts seven paratroop battalions on Monastery Hill and in the town, three more in reserve, and about 180 guns behind them. In the town the paratroops had cellars and steel shelters that the bombs did not break.

The plan was for the 6th New Zealand Brigade to take Castle Hill and clear the town, with tanks of the 19th Armoured Regiment, while the 5th Indian Brigade climbed from Castle Hill by the hairpin bends of the road to Hangman's Hill, below the abbey. On the night of 14 March the New Zealanders drew back from the edge of the town to clear it for the bombers. *The Tiger Triumphs* says that the signal "Bradman will be batting to-morrow" may have puzzled the German listeners.

### The places
- **Cassino**: the town at the foot of Monte Cassino, on Route 6 (Highway 6), the road to Rome.
- **Castle Hill (Point 193)**: the rock above the north-west of the town, with the ruined castle of Rocca Janula.
- **Points 165 and 236**: the lower and upper hairpin bends of the road from Castle Hill towards the abbey.
- **Point 202**: a knoll on the slope, held by a New Zealand company until 24 March.
- **Hangman's Hill (Point 435)**: a rocky platform below the abbey with the concrete pylon of an aerial ropeway on it, about 250 yards from the ruins.
- **The Continental Hotel**: on Route 6 at the foot of the hill; the paratroops held it to the end.
- **The railway station**: south of the town, lost by the Maori in February and taken by the New Zealanders on 17 March.
- **Venafro**: a town about 10 air miles away, bombed in error on 15 March.

### The leaders
- **Bernard Freyberg** (Allies, New Zealand Corps): planned the attack and recommended stopping it on 23 March.
- **G. B. Parkinson** (Allies, New Zealand Division): commanded the division after Kippenberger was wounded.
- **John McDuff** (Allies, 25th Battalion): his battalion led the attack into the town.
- **Major Beckett** (Allies, 1/4th Essex): twice wounded, held the castle walls against the paratroops' charge.
- **Colonel Nangle** (Allies, 1/9th Gurkha Rifles): sent his companies up to Hangman's Hill.
- **Captain Drinkall** (Allies, C Company, 1/9th Gurkhas): led his company through the dark to Hangman's Hill.
- **Colonel Noble** (Allies, 1/4th Essex): killed by a sniper on Castle Hill after the charge.
- **Richard Heidrich** (German, 1st Parachute Division): held the town and the abbey.
- **Heinrich von Vietinghoff** (German, Tenth Army): ordered the massif held "at all costs" at 10:40 on 15 March (Blumenson).

### The battle
Blumenson has the medium bombers over the town at 08:30 and the heavy bombers from 08:45, in waves about every 15 minutes until noon: 435 aircraft and nearly 1,000 tons of bombs, of which only about 300 tons fell in the town. At noon the guns took over. Blumenson counts 746 guns and howitzers, firing almost 200,000 rounds between 12:20 and 20:00. Short bombs killed 28 Allied soldiers and wounded more; at Venafro bombs killed 17 soldiers and 40 civilians.

The 25th Battalion went into the rubble from the north with the 19th Armoured Regiment's tanks. Craters 40 to 50 feet across and heaps of masonry stopped the tanks, and paratroops came up from the cellars and fired from the ruins. About four hours after the infantry moved, Castle Hill was taken (*The Tiger Triumphs*). The 25th Battalion was to clear the west of the town as far as the Continental Hotel, and did not reach it (Wikipedia). That evening the rain came, and the craters filled. Blumenson says no tank could pass for about 36 hours.

At dusk the 1/4th Essex took over Castle Hill, and a company reached Point 165. The 1/6th Rajputana Rifles came up under fire; their headquarters was hit and their colonel wounded. At 01:30 Colonel Nangle sent two Gurkha companies forward; one lost 15 men in a minute, and Drinkall's C Company passed the castle and disappeared into the night. At midday on the 16th a faint wireless message showed that it held Hangman's Hill (*The Tiger Triumphs*). On the night of 16-17 March the rest of the 1/9th Gurkhas joined it, passing the Continental Hotel in the dark.

The fighting in the town went on from house to house. On 17 March the New Zealanders took the station. Each rifle company of the 25th Battalion was down to about 50 men. One morning, as the night thinned, a battalion of the 1st Parachute Regiment came down the spur from the abbey, overran the posts at the hairpin bend and reached the castle walls. The Essex and the gunners of 1st Field Regiment held them there with grenades and tommy-guns at point-blank range (*The Tiger Triumphs* gives no date). On 19 March a tank thrust behind the massif lost about a dozen tanks. On the 21st the 21st Battalion's attack down Route 6 was caught in crossfire near the Continental Hotel (Phillips). The attack was given up on 23 March. On the night of 24 March the men on Hangman's Hill came down: 10 officers and 247 other ranks (*The Tiger Triumphs*).

The wounded from Castle Hill were carried along a track under fire and over the escarpment to ambulances on the Rapido, many of them driven by American Field Service volunteers over a route of about five miles (*The Tiger Triumphs*).

### Where the accounts disagree
- **The clock.** None of the accounts says which clock it used. Italy and the German army both kept GMT+1 that winter (tz database).
- **The bombers and guns.** Blumenson gives 435 aircraft, nearly 1,000 tons and 746 guns. Rickard gives 435 aircraft, 1,000 tons and about 750 guns. *The Tiger Triumphs* gives 610 guns and about 1,200 tons of shell. Wikipedia says nearly 500 aircraft were planned and gives 750 tonnes of bombs (Laurie).
- **Castle Hill.** *The Tiger Triumphs*, NZHistory and the 25th Battalion's history have it taken on 15 March; Rickard puts it on the 17th.
- **The 26th Battalion.** Blumenson says the New Zealanders could not reach Highway 6; Rickard has the 26th held up north of it.
- **The end.** Blumenson, *The Tiger Triumphs* and NZHistory give 23 March; Rickard has Alexander cancel it on the 22nd.
- **The losses.** Blumenson gives about 2,000 New Zealand and Indian losses in nine days: almost 300 killed, nearly 250 missing and more than 1,500 wounded. Wikipedia gives about 3,000 for the 4th Indian Division and 1,600 for the New Zealanders. *The Tiger Triumphs* says the 4th Indian Division lost 4,000 men at Cassino; NZHistory gives 343 New Zealand dead by early April. The paratroops' 2nd Battalion, 3rd Parachute Regiment lost half its men and still held (Rickard); Blumenson says about 300 men in a tunnel came through the bombing.

!!! story "Scissors and a pocket-knife"
    Edmond Hazle and Leonard Piper were lance-corporals of the 1/4th Essex. They went up to Hangman's
    Hill with the men who joined the Gurkhas there, below the abbey and cut off from the rest of the
    division. For eight days, supplied only by air drops and porters at night, the garrison held on.
    Hazle and Piper stayed with the wounded the whole time. *The Tiger Triumphs* says that Hazle cut off
    shattered limbs with scissors and a pocket-knife. He already held the Distinguished Conduct Medal
    and was given a bar to it.
