### The campaign
On 6 October 1915 the German Eleventh Army and the Austro-Hungarian Third Army, under August von Mackensen, attacked Serbia across the Sava and the Danube. Belgrade fell on 9 October. Bulgaria joined the Central Powers, attacked across the border from 11 October and declared war on 14 October. Its First Army struck west towards Niš. Its Second Army, under Georgi Todorov, went into Macedonia to cut the railway from Niš to Salonika. It cut the line on 16 October and entered Skopje and Veles on 22 or 23 October.

A French and British force had begun to land at Salonika on 5 October. The French went up the Vardar to Krivolak, hoping to join hands with the Serbs. Between them and the Bulgarians at Veles stood a small Serbian force on the Babuna pass. The pass carried the road from Veles to Prilep, and so to Monastir (Bitola). While the Serbs held it, the road south to Monastir, and the hope of supplies from the south, stayed open.

The Nelson history says about 5,000 Serbs held the heights above the northern approach. In the first days of November they beat off a Bulgarian division and drove it back to Izvor, about a dozen miles from Veles. But that was only an advanced guard. Todorov's main force came down from the Veles front, and the Serbs were pushed back to the crest. They held it, Nelson says, for a week and more. A French column took the Vozartzi bridge on the Crna (the Tcherna) on 5 November and tried to climb the heights towards them, but it was beaten back.

### The places
- **The Babuna pass**: the saddle where the road from Veles to Prilep crosses the Babuna mountains.
- **Izvor**: the village at the northern foot of the pass, about a dozen miles from Veles, where the Bulgarians were first driven back.
- **Veles**: the town on the Vardar, in Bulgarian hands from late October, from which the main attack came.
- **Prilep**: the town south of the pass, where Flora Sandes worked in the military hospital. The Bulgarians occupied it on 17 November.
- **Bitola (Monastir; Sandes's "Bitol")**: the town further south, which gave the Serbian division its name, and the next stage of the retreat.
- **Skopje (Uskub)**: taken by the Bulgarians in late October, with Veles.
- **Krivolak and the Crna**: where the French held a bridgehead on the Vardar and tried to reach the Serbs.
- **Salonika**: the Greek port where the Allies landed, and where Sandes came ashore on 3 November.

### The leaders
- **Radomir Putnik** (Serbian, commander of the army): the stand at Babuna, Nelson says, bought him time to bring the main army away.
- **Colonel Wasitch** (Serbian, Bitol Division): so Sandes calls him; the Wikipedia article on Sandes names him General Miloš Vasić. He let her stay with the regiment through the retreat and later enrolled her as a soldier.
- **Colonel Militch** (Serbian, 2nd Regiment): the regiment's commandant, whose ambulance Sandes joined.
- **Flora Sandes** (British, with the Serbian 2nd Regiment): a nurse aged 39, attached to the 2nd Regiment's ambulance near Babuna.
- **August von Mackensen** (German, commander of the invasion).
- **Georgi Todorov** (Bulgarian, Second Army): Nelson spells him Teodorov.
- **Maurice Sarrail** (French, at Salonika): reached Salonika on 12 October and pushed the French up the Vardar.

### The battle
The sources give no day-by-day account of the fighting at Babuna. Nelson gives the outline: a first Bulgarian attack beaten back to Izvor in the first days of November, then the main force from Veles, the Serbs pressed back to the crest, and the crest held for a week and more. Nelson says the Serbs faced in the end "the better part of six divisions". Sandes says they were outnumbered more than four to one.

Sandes explains why so few could hold so long, and why they could not hold longer. The position was strong, she writes, almost impregnable. But the commandant had an enormous front to hold with two regiments, and since the Bulgarians could walk round the lines, every available man was already in the firing line: there were no reserves. The Bulgarians had big guns. The Serbs had a few small cannon about ten years old. With a few big guns, she thought, they could have held the pass for any length of time.

Sandes reached the 2nd Regiment's ambulance about the middle of November, after a week at the military hospital in Prilep, which was full of men with dysentery lying on the floor. The ambulance was in tents by the road near Babuna. The next morning a battle was going on at Babuna, and the wounded came in all day. The path down the mountain was so steep that a badly wounded man could hardly be carried on a stretcher, so most of those who came in had walked down. Men who could not walk were sent on to the base hospital in ox wagons. They came in faster than the wagons could take them away.

A day or two later, at ten o'clock at night by Sandes's clock, the regiment was ordered to retreat from Babuna. The ambulance packed up in the wind and rain and went down the road for about eight hours. It passed through Prilep in the night and was between Prilep and Bitola at sunrise. Her dates put this in the middle of November, just before the Bulgarians entered Prilep on 17 November. The French could not now reach the Serbs, and fell back. On 25 November the Serbian government and high command decided to take the army across the mountains of Albania to the Adriatic. The 2nd Regiment went back by Bitola, fighting rearguard actions, Sandes says, for the next six weeks.

### Where the accounts disagree
- **How many attacked.** Nelson says the Serbs faced the better part of six divisions. Sandes says they were outnumbered more than four to one. Which Bulgarian division made the first attack, and which units came from Veles, is not given by either.
- **How many defended.** Nelson gives about 5,000 Serbs. Sandes gives no number, but names the 2nd and 14th Regiments.
- **The height.** Nelson calls the pass "a little over 2,000 feet high". A modern cycling survey puts the road summit at 1,090 m, about 3,600 feet.
- **The dates.** Nelson puts the first Bulgarian repulse in the first days of November and the end of the stand a week and more after that, but gives no date for the end. Sandes's diary dates only her arrival at Salonika (3 November) and one evening in Prilep ("Monday, 8th"). The Wikipedia article on Prilep gives 17 November for its occupation.
- **Skopje and Veles.** The Wikipedia article on the campaign has the Bulgarians reach Skopje on 22 October; the article on the Kosovo offensive, citing Hall, has them enter Skopje and Veles on 23 October.
- **The commander.** Sandes calls the commandant of the Bitol Division Colonel Wasitch; the Wikipedia article on her calls him General Miloš Vasić.
