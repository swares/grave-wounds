### The campaign
When the Serbs gave up the Babuna pass in the middle of November 1915, the road from Prilep to Bitola (Monastir) was the only way left for the 2nd and 14th Regiments of the Bitol Division. The Bulgarians occupied Prilep on 17 November. The regiments fell back south-west along the road towards Bitola, fighting rearguard actions as they went, with Flora Sandes still with the 2nd Regiment's ambulance. After Bitola they went on by Resen and Struga into the mountains of Albania.

### The places
- **Topolčani (Sandes's "Topolchar")**: a village by the road from Prilep to Bitola. The small deserted hahn (a roadside inn of two rooms) where the ambulance stopped was close to it.
- **The hillside**: where the Serbs held little shallow trenches "dotted about", a dozen men in each.
- **The river and the bridge**: the road crossed the river behind the hahn by a bridge mined to be blown once the Serbs were over. The river is not named in her account.
- **Prilep**: the town behind the Bulgarians, occupied on 17 November.
- **Bitola (Monastir; Sandes's "Bitol")**: the town ahead of the Serbs, where the road went on.

### The leaders
- **Colonel Militch** (Serbian, 2nd Regiment): the commandant, who appeared at the hahn about nine that night to take the staff away.
- **Flora Sandes** (British, with the Serbian 2nd Regiment): at the hahn with the ambulance through the day.
- **Georgi Todorov** (Bulgarian, Second Army).

### The battle
Sandes's account is the only one found, and she gives no date. It snowed hard all night. At daybreak the ambulance moved a short way to the hahn by the roadside. A blizzard blew all morning; a company of reinforcements went past and floundered off through the deep drifts towards the trenches. The Serbs, she writes, were outnumbered by more than four to one, holding an enormous front with two regiments. About midday the blizzard stopped for a while, and she heard the Bulgarians' wild cheering as they took the trenches.

In the afternoon the big guns opened again. The bridge, though it had ten mines in it, had failed to blow up: the mines would not go off, and no one knew why. The Bulgarian guns came across. At twilight the Greek doctor sent to the commandant, and the ambulance was told to pack up and go. The staff left about nine, and they rode through the night to a village Sandes thought was called Mogilee. Most of the wounded she dressed that day had been hit in the right arm or wrist; one man with a wound in the abdomen she thought was done for.

### Where the accounts disagree
- **The date.** Sandes gives none. The fight falls after Prilep was occupied (17 November) and before Bitola fell, which the Wikipedia article on Bitola puts on 21 November, though a Geneva press report of 7 December still has Serbs leaving Monastir. The battlefield takes about 19 November.
- **The numbers.** She gives only the odds, more than four to one, and the trenches' dozen men each. The companies and the Bulgarian battalions on the battlefield are round numbers for play.
- **The trenches.** The trenches she visited the day before were on Kalabac, about two hours' ride from the hahn; whether the trenches taken at midday were those is not clear.
