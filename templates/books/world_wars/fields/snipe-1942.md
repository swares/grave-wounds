### The campaign
Rommel's German and Italian army beat the British at Gazala in June 1942 and followed them into Egypt. It was stopped at El Alamein in July and at Alam Halfa in August. The line there ran about 40 miles from the sea to the Qattara Depression, so any attack had to go in from the front.

Montgomery's Eighth Army attacked on the night of 23 October 1942 (Operation Lightfoot). The infantry broke into the minefields, but the lanes were not cleared in time and the tanks could not get through. Rommel, away sick, came back on 25 October; his stand-in, Stumme, had died on the 24th. The tanks of both sides fought round the Kidney feature for three days.

On 26 October the 7th Motor Brigade, of the 1st Armoured Division, was ordered to push two battalions west at 23:00 onto two points, "Woodcock" and "Snipe", for the armoured brigades to pass through in the morning. The 2nd King's Royal Rifle Corps went for Woodcock. The 2nd Battalion The Rifle Brigade, under Lieutenant-Colonel Victor Buller Turner, went for Snipe. With Turner went his own six-pounders, two troops of 239 Battery, 76th (Royal Welch Fusiliers) Anti-Tank Regiment RA, and 16 sappers of the 7th Field Squadron RE. With 76 riflemen and 22 carriers the force came to about 300 men.

### The places
- **El Alamein**: the railway halt on the coast that gave the battle its name.
- **The Kidney feature**: a kidney-shaped contour west of the British gaps, fought over from 24 October.
- **Snipe**: the objective, about 1.5 miles south-west of the Kidney.
- **The Snipe hollow**: a shallow oval depression, about 1,000 by 500 yards, with camelthorn and soft sand, where the battalion dug in short of the objective.
- **Woodcock**: the 2nd KRRC's objective, north of Snipe; the battalion fell short of it.
- **The eastern ridge**: the low rise behind the outpost, where the battalion's ambulances and lorries waited.
- **The western ridge**: the low rise in front, over which the Italian tanks came.

### The leaders
- **Victor Buller Turner** (British, 2nd Rifle Brigade): 42, the brother of a First World War VC. He was wounded in the head and went on.
- **Tom Bird** (British, S Company): commanded the battalion's anti-tank company, and drove ammunition round the guns in a jeep.
- **Charles Calistan** (British, sergeant): his six-pounder held the south-west side at midday.
- **Ralph Noyes** (British, artillery observer): went out to reconnoitre at 04:00 and did not come back, so the outpost had no guns on call.
- **Raymond Briggs** (British, 1st Armoured Division): decided not to send his tanks forward to save the outpost.
- **T. J. B. Bosvile** (British, 7th Motor Brigade): arranged the relief and the withdrawal.
- **Erwin Rommel** (German, commander): ordered the counter-attack of 27 October.
- **Gustav von Vaerst** (German, 15th Panzer Division): its tanks lay north of the outpost.
- **Stiffelmayer** (German, battle group): his Gruppe of about 35 tanks and self-propelled guns had leaguered close to the hollow. The sources give no first name or rank.
- **Capitano Preve** (Italian, Littorio): led 13 tanks of the Littorio's tank regiment against the west side.

### The battle
The barrage came down at 22:55 on 26 October. Turner followed it on a bearing of 270 degrees rather than his navigator's 233, and the battalion set off at 23:10. At about 00:15 on 27 October, 4,000 yards out, it reached a hollow it took for Snipe, some 800 or 900 yards short. The carriers took prisoners and shot up three lorries at a leaguer close by. By 03:45 19 of the 27 six-pounders were dug in: 13 of the battalion's and 6 of the battery's.

At first light the tanks of 15th Panzer and the Littorio left their leaguers and moved off past the hollow, side-on to the guns. A Panzer IV Special was hit at about 30 yards. At 07:30 the 47th and 41st Royal Tank Regiments came over the eastern ridge, took Snipe for an enemy position and fired on it. They then lost heavily, and by 09:00 were pulled back, 47 RTR down to five Shermans and six Crusaders. The outpost was on its own.

At about 10:00 the carriers drove off Italian infantry massing to the west. By noon six carriers had been hit and set on fire, and ammunition was short. Preve's 13 tanks came over the western ridge; four were knocked out and the rest drew off. At about 13:00 eight more tanks and a self-propelled gun came in from the south-west, where only Calistan's gun was left. Turner and Lieutenant Jack Toms joined him, and the attack was stopped.

At about 16:00 British guns firing from the east hit the outpost again. At about 17:00 some 40 German and Italian tanks passed across the front of 239 Battery and S Company and lost heavily. A second wave of 15 Panzer IIIs turned on the north-west side, where only three guns could fire, with about 10 rounds each. It too was stopped. Dusk ended the attacks. The last anti-tank round was fired at 19:40.

Three carriers took the worst wounded back over the eastern ridge in the morning; of three more sent later, one was knocked out. The ambulances could not come forward through the fire. That night the 5th Royal Sussex, of the 133rd Lorried Infantry Brigade, came up to relieve the battalion, but dug in about 1,000 yards to the south-east and was not found. The survivors came out on foot late that night, the wounded on six carriers and three jeeps, with one gun. The other guns were left without their sights and breech blocks, and the dead were left behind.

### Where the accounts disagree
- **The clock.** Wikipedia, after Playfair, gives the start of Lightfoot in Egyptian Summer Time (GMT+3). Egypt put its clocks back to GMT+2 at midnight on 26/27 October 1942, and no account of Snipe says which clock it used. The game keeps GMT+3, on which the sun rose about 07:16 and set about 18:23 (our reckoning).
- **The guns.** Wikipedia, after Latimer, gives 19 guns dug in of 27. Miskimon has the battalion bring 16 and the battery 11.
- **Where they dug in.** Wikipedia puts the hollow 800 to 900 yards south-east of Snipe; Miskimon, south.
- **The tanks destroyed.** Turner's citation counts not less than 90 German tanks attacking, 35 set on fire and at least 20 more immobilised. The committee that went over the ground a month later, as Lucas Phillips gives it, counted at least 32 tanks burnt out (21 German, 11 Italian) and 5 self-propelled guns, with 15 or 20 more knocked out and recovered. Wikipedia gives the committee 22 German and 10 Italian tanks. Pitt allows 34 tanks and self-propelled guns.
- **The losses.** Lucas Phillips gives 72 riflemen and gunners killed and wounded, the sappers' loss not known. The New Zealand official history says fewer than 100 of about 300.
- **The withdrawal.** The New Zealand history says the guns were wrecked and the men started back at about 21:00. Wikipedia gives 22:30; Miskimon says they waited until 23:00 for leave to go.
- **When the attacks began.** Turner's citation has the position attacked from 05:30; Miskimon has the guns open at about 06:00.
- **Turner's rank.** The New Zealand history calls him "Major V. B. Turner"; the Gazette names him a lieutenant-colonel, a temporary rank.
