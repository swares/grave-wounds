## Why the team marches {#frames}

Five reasons for a black-powder team to be on the road. A frame adds no rules: it says who pays, what
the team is after and what ends it, and uses the [patrons](rules.html#patrons), [pay](rules.html#rank)
and [work](rules.html#work) in the Rules. The history in each frame is sourced below. The goals, first
jobs and complications are ours, written for play.

### A colonel's regiment {#frame-colonel}

In the Thirty Years' War a prince did not raise his own regiments. He gave a colonel a commission and
money to recruit one, and the colonel raised, paid and kept it, at a profit if he could. Wallenstein
built the emperor's army of the 1620s this way, and fed it by levying **contributions** on the lands it
stood in. A regiment that could not be paid was disbanded, and its men went to whoever was recruiting.

- **Who pays:** your colonel, from the prince's money and what the country gives up.
- **The goal:** pay, plunder, and a colonel who keeps his regiment.
- **What ends it:** the money stops, the regiment is disbanded, or your colonel falls.
- **First job:** the muster, where the colonel counts his men for the prince's money.
- **What goes wrong:** the pay is months behind. The country is eaten bare. A rival colonel offers more.
- **Maps:** Lützen.

### A regiment of the line {#frame-line}

A British soldier of the Napoleonic Wars enlisted for life until 1806, when an Act let infantry enlist
for seven years instead. He was paid by the day, less stoppages for food and kit, and Wellington's army
in the Peninsula was often months behind with it. A few soldiers' wives a company went abroad with the
regiment, chosen by lot. The Crimean War's army was still raised and paid the same way.

- **Who pays:** the King, by the day, less the stoppages, when the paymaster has it.
- **The goal:** serve out your term, rise to sergeant, and come home with your pension.
- **What ends it:** your term, a wound that gets you discharged, or the regiment going home.
- **First job:** the march up from the port to join the army.
- **What goes wrong:** the pay is months behind. A storm offers plunder, and the provost's rope. Your wife's name is not drawn.
- **Maps:** Albuera, Ciudad Rodrigo, Badajoz, Burgos, Sevastopol.

### The militia called out {#frame-militia}

Militia were neighbours called out for a short term. At Cowpens in January 1781 Daniel Morgan asked his
militia for only two volleys before they fell back behind his Continentals, and they gave them. In 1812
New York militiamen refused to cross the Niagara into Canada, and watched the regulars beaten at
Queenston Heights from their own bank.

- **Who pays:** your state, little and late.
- **The goal:** do your part and be home before the term runs out.
- **What ends it:** the term runs out, the enemy leaves, or the company goes home anyway.
- **First job:** the call-out, and the march to join the army.
- **What goes wrong:** the term ends on the eve of a battle. The regulars think little of you. The farm needs you now.
- **Maps:** Cowpens, Niagara.

### Ninety days to end it {#frame-ninety}

After Fort Sumter, on 15 April 1861, Lincoln called for 75,000 militia for ninety days. Most of the men
who marched to Bull Run that July expected one battle to settle the war. Some regiments' terms ran out
on its eve, and they went home as the army marched.

- **Who pays:** the state and the Union, at a private's eleven dollars a month (thirteen from August 1861).
- **The goal:** win the one battle, and be home by harvest.
- **What ends it:** the term, a defeat, or a three-year enlistment if you sign again.
- **First job:** a camp of instruction, and the march into Virginia.
- **What goes wrong:** the term ends before the battle. The officers you elected know no more than you. The battle does not end the war.
- **Maps:** Bull Run.

### A fort on the trail {#frame-fort}

Fort Phil Kearny was built in July 1866 to guard the Bozeman Trail through the Powder River country,
which the Lakota, Cheyenne and Arapaho held. Red Cloud's warriors kept it under watch. On 21 December
1866 Captain Fetterman and 80 men followed decoys over Lodge Trail Ridge and none came back. The
treaty of 1868 gave the forts up.

- **Who pays:** the army, on the paymaster's rare visits.
- **The goal:** keep the trail open and the wood trains safe, and live to your discharge.
- **What ends it:** your enlistment, a treaty, or the fort abandoned.
- **First job:** guarding the wood train to the pinery.
- **What goes wrong:** a decoy draws the column over the ridge. The winter closes the trail. Men desert for the gold fields.
- **Maps:** Fort Phil Kearny.
