### The campaign
After the fall of Fort Sumter in April 1861, Virginia joined the Confederacy, and on 20 May its government moved to Richmond. Washington's eye turned to the new capital. Its best overland road ran through Manassas Junction, where the Orange & Alexandria Railroad met the Manassas Gap line from the Shenandoah Valley. Beauregard held the crossings of Bull Run in front of the junction with about 20,000 men. Johnston had about 12,000 in the Valley.

Winfield Scott's plan was to press both at once. Patterson was to hold Johnston in the Valley while McDowell marched on Manassas. McDowell left Washington on 16 July with about 35,000 men, mostly new volunteers. He reached Centreville on 18 July, and his probe at Blackburn's Ford was beaten back the same day. He spent two days scouting the Confederate left. Meanwhile Johnston slipped away from Patterson, and most of his brigades came by rail to Manassas on 20 and 21 July.

McDowell's plan was a flank march. Tyler's division would make a show at the Stone Bridge. Hunter and Heintzelman would go round by Sudley Ford, cross Bull Run and come down behind the Confederate left.

### The places
- **Manassas Junction**: the railway junction both armies were fighting for.
- **Centreville**: McDowell's base, about 5 miles from Bull Run, and the rallying point after the rout.
- **Blackburn's Ford**: a ford on Bull Run where the first clash came on 18 July.
- **The Stone Bridge**: the Warrenton Turnpike's bridge over Bull Run, held at first by Evans; Tyler fired the opening shot here.
- **Sudley Ford and Sudley Springs**: where the Union flank column crossed Bull Run, late in the morning.
- **Matthews Hill**: north of the turnpike, where the Confederates lost the morning fight.
- **Henry Hill and the Henry house**: the open plateau south of the turnpike and Young's Branch, where the Confederates rallied and the afternoon fight was fought. This is the field the game shows.
- **The Sudley road**: running past the west side of Henry Hill, with woods along it.
- **Chinn Ridge**: the next ridge west, where the last Union brigade was broken.
- **Portici (the Lewis house)**: Johnston's headquarters, later a field hospital.
- **Cub Run bridge**: on the turnpike east of Bull Run, where an overturned wagon blocked the retreat.

### The leaders
- **Irvin McDowell** (Union, commanding): planned the flank march. He was replaced by George B. McClellan after the defeat.
- **Samuel P. Heintzelman** (Union, Third Division): led the Fire Zouaves forward on Henry Hill himself, and was wounded.
- **William F. Barry** (Union, chief of artillery): sent the two regular batteries onto the hill and posted their supports.
- **Charles Griffin** and **James B. Ricketts** (Union, regular battery captains): Griffin's Battery D, 5th US Artillery, and Ricketts's Battery I, 1st US Artillery. Ricketts was wounded in the thigh and taken prisoner.
- **William T. Sherman** (Union, brigade): crossed Bull Run in the morning and sent his regiments up the hill in turn.
- **P. G. T. Beauregard** (Confederate, Army of the Potomac): commanded on the field under Johnston, and ordered the general attack.
- **Joseph E. Johnston** (Confederate, senior commander): brought his army from the Valley by rail and fed reinforcements to the left.
- **Thomas J. Jackson** (Confederate, brigade): formed his Virginians and guns on the edge of Henry Hill. Here he got his name "Stonewall".
- **Barnard E. Bee** (Confederate, brigade): fought on Matthews Hill, and was mortally wounded.
- **Arthur C. Cummings** (Confederate, 33rd Virginia): a Virginia Military Institute graduate who had served in Mexico. He led the charge on Griffin's guns.
- **J. E. B. Stuart** (Confederate, 1st Virginia Cavalry): guarded Jackson's left and rode into the Fire Zouaves.
- **E. Kirby Smith, Arnold Elzey** and **Jubal A. Early** (Confederate, late brigades): Smith was wounded on arrival and Elzey took over. With Early they broke the Union right.

### The battle
Tyler's signal gun fired across Bull Run just after 06:00. The flank column was slow, and Burnside's leading brigade was not clear of the woods by Sudley Ford until about 09:00. Evans, at the Stone Bridge, guessed that Tyler was only making a show. He took most of his men to Matthews Hill and opened fire on Burnside about 10:15. Bee and Bartow came up about 11:00, bringing the Confederates to about 2,800, but more Union troops kept arriving, and Sherman crossed Bull Run above the bridge. By about 11:30 the Confederates were going back over Young's Branch to Henry Hill. Jackson says he posted his guns on the far edge of the hill at 11:30. Johnston and Beauregard rode up about 12:30 and rallied the broken brigades behind him.

The game's field begins here, about 14:00. McDowell sent Ricketts's and Griffin's batteries onto the hill near the Henry house, a few hundred yards from Jackson's guns. Barry guided the 11th New York (the Fire Zouaves) to support them, with the 14th Brooklyn and a battalion of Marines, most of them recruits of less than three weeks. Union shells aimed at sharpshooters in the Henry house mortally wounded Judith Henry, a bedridden widow, in her bed.

Heintzelman led the Zouaves against men in a clump of pines; the first volley broke most of them. Stuart, on Jackson's left, at first took them for his own side, then saw their flag and charged. Then the 33rd Virginia came out of the woods on the left. Griffin took them for Confederates and wanted to fire canister. Barry told him they were Union troops coming to support the guns (Joint Committee report, 1863). The Virginians fired a volley that cut down gunners and horses. Griffin lost 27 of 95 men and 55 of 101 horses. Ricketts was wounded and his lieutenant, Ramsay, killed.

For about two hours the guns changed hands; Heintzelman says Ricketts's battery was taken and retaken three times. Jackson sent the 4th and 27th Virginia in with the bayonet at about 15:30, and they broke the Union centre. Sherman's regiments went up the hill one after another. The 2nd Wisconsin, in grey, was fired on by both sides. Colonel Cameron of the 79th New York was mortally wounded. Sherman says his brigade was in disorder by about 15:30.

Fresh Confederate brigades came in on the left. Kirby Smith was wounded at once and Elzey led his brigade on. Howard's brigade, arriving tired, was driven off Chinn Ridge. Early's three regiments, with Stuart and Beckham's battery, struck the Union right in flank while Beauregard attacked in front. Heintzelman says the retreat began about 16:30. Regulars covered it, but the volunteers streamed back over Bull Run. On the turnpike a wagon overturned on the Cub Run bridge, and the retreat became a rout among the carriages of spectators from Washington. Jefferson Davis reached the field as it ended. The Confederates were too disordered to follow far, and it rained all the next day. The remnants of McDowell's army were back at Washington by 22 July.

### Where the accounts disagree
- **The armies.** The NPS gives McDowell 35,000 and Beauregard 22,000, with about 10,000 more from Johnston. The American Battlefield Trust, following Salmon and Livermore, gives 28,450 Union and 32,230 Confederate. Ballard puts about 18,000 on each side actually engaged. Johnston's own report claims he faced 35,000.
- **The losses.** The NPS booklet (Davis) and the Trust give Union losses of 2,896 (460 killed, 1,124 wounded, 1,312 missing), as does Eicher; the Union return gives 2,708. The Trust gives 1,982 Confederate losses; Davis, nearly 2,000. Ballard says each side lost a little over 1,700. Jackson's brigade alone lost 111 killed, 368 wounded and 5 missing, by his report.
- **The artillery duel.** The Davis booklet has it last nearly an hour; the older NPS handbook, about 15 minutes.
- **The guns taken.** The NPS trail marker has the 33rd Virginia take two of Griffin's howitzers. Barry says the enemy took ten guns in all. Jackson claims seven pieces besides the battery Cummings took and lost.
- **The blue regiment.** Wikipedia's regimental history says Jackson's men wore blue like the Union troops. The Davis booklet says the 33rd were in mixed uniforms.
- **Stuart's charge.** Stuart reports about 300 men fit for duty, half of them sent with Major Swann. A 2021 Emerging Civil War article puts the charging force at about 150. The handbook puts the charge before the 33rd's volley.
- **Kirby Smith's arrival.** Johnston says about 15:00. The Davis booklet has Howard's attack about 16:00 and Early's arrival between 16:00 and 17:00. The handbook has the final combined attack break the Union line about 15:45.
- **The "stone wall".** Bee was mortally wounded and left no report, so his words are not certain. Burnett Rhett claimed he meant them as a reproach, because Jackson's men were lying behind the crest.
- **Judith Henry.** The NPS gives her age as 84, Wikipedia as 85. The NPS says artillery fire struck her; the Metropolitan Museum's note says she was found dead of gunshot wounds.
- **Blackburn's Ford.** The NPS dates it 18 July; the American Battlefield Trust, 17 July.

!!! story "No words can describe"
    Captain James Ricketts was hit in the thigh beside his guns on Henry Hill and was taken
    prisoner. In Washington his wife Fanny heard only conflicting reports of his fate. On 25 July she
    set out for the Confederate lines, and that evening, at their headquarters, learned that he was
    alive. Next morning she reached the Lewis house, Portici, which had become a hospital, its kitchen
    and hallways used for operations. She nursed him and other wounded men there for about a week,
    writing in her journal that "no words can describe the horrors around me". When he was sent to
    prison in Richmond on 2 August, she went with him, and stayed until his release in December.
