### The campaign
In May 1861 the Confederate government moved to Richmond. The best road there from Washington ran through Manassas Junction, where the Orange & Alexandria Railroad met the line from the Shenandoah Valley. Beauregard held the crossings of Bull Run in front of the junction with about 20,000 men. Johnston had about 12,000 in the Valley.

Winfield Scott's plan was to press both at once: Patterson would hold Johnston while McDowell marched on Manassas. McDowell left Washington on 16 July with about 35,000 men, mostly new volunteers. His probe at Blackburn's Ford was beaten back on 18 July. While he scouted, Johnston slipped away from Patterson, and most of his brigades came by rail to Manassas on 20 and 21 July.

McDowell chose a flank march. Tyler's division would make a show at the Stone Bridge. Hunter and Heintzelman would go round by Sudley Ford and come down behind the Confederate left.

### The places
- **Centreville**: McDowell's base, about 5 miles from Bull Run.
- **The Stone Bridge**: the Warrenton Turnpike's bridge over Bull Run, held at first by Evans.
- **Sudley Ford**: where the Union flank column crossed Bull Run in the morning.
- **Matthews Hill**: north of the turnpike, where the Confederates lost the morning fight.
- **Henry Hill and the Henry house**: the plateau south of the turnpike and Young's Branch, where the Confederates rallied. The game's field.
- **Chinn Ridge**: the next ridge west, beyond the Sudley road, where the last Union brigade was broken.
- **Portici (the Lewis house)**: Johnston's headquarters, later a field hospital.
- **Cub Run bridge**: on the turnpike east of Bull Run, where an overturned wagon blocked the retreat.

### The leaders
- **Irvin McDowell** (Union, commanding): planned the flank march. George B. McClellan replaced him after the defeat.
- **Samuel P. Heintzelman** (Union, division): led the Zouaves forward himself.
- **William F. Barry** (Union, chief of artillery): sent the two regular batteries onto the hill and posted their supports.
- **Charles Griffin** and **James B. Ricketts** (Union, regular batteries): Battery D, 5th US Artillery, and Battery I, 1st US Artillery. Ricketts was wounded and taken prisoner.
- **Joseph E. Johnston** and **P. G. T. Beauregard** (Confederate, commanders): Johnston, the senior, fed reinforcements to the left; Beauregard ordered the general attack.
- **Thomas J. Jackson** (Confederate, brigade): formed his Virginians and guns on the edge of Henry Hill, and got his name "Stonewall".
- **Arthur C. Cummings** (Confederate, 33rd Virginia): a Virginia Military Institute man who had served in Mexico. He led the charge on Griffin's guns.
- **J. E. B. Stuart** (Confederate, 1st Virginia Cavalry): guarded Jackson's left and rode into the Fire Zouaves.
- **Kirby Smith, Arnold Elzey** and **Jubal A. Early** (Confederate, late brigades): Smith was wounded on arrival and Elzey took over. With Early they broke the Union right.

### The battle
The flank column was slow and did not reach Sudley Ford until after 09:00. Evans, at the Stone Bridge, guessed Tyler was only making a show, and met the column on Matthews Hill about 10:15. Bee and Bartow came up about 11:00, but Union troops kept arriving, and by about 11:30 the Confederates were falling back over Young's Branch. Jackson says he posted his guns on the far edge of Henry Hill at 11:30. Johnston and Beauregard rode up about 12:30 and rallied the broken brigades behind him.

The game's field shows Henry Hill from about 14:00 to the break after 16:00. At about 14:00 McDowell sent Ricketts's and Griffin's batteries onto the hill near the Henry house, some 300 yards from Jackson's guns. Barry led up the Fire Zouaves (11th New York) in support, with the 14th Brooklyn and a battalion of Marines, most of them recruits of under three weeks. Union shells aimed at sharpshooters in the Henry house mortally wounded Judith Henry, a bedridden widow.

The Zouaves' first advance broke under a volley from the pines. Stuart at first took them for friends, then saw their flag and charged. Then the 33rd Virginia came out of the woods on the left. Griffin took them for Confederates and wanted to fire canister. Barry said they were the battery's supports (Joint Committee report, 1863). The Virginians' volley cut down gunners and horses. Griffin lost 27 of 95 men and 55 of 101 horses. Ricketts was wounded and his lieutenant, Ramsay, killed.

For about two hours the guns changed hands. Heintzelman says Ricketts's battery was taken and retaken three times. About 15:30 Jackson sent the 4th and 27th Virginia in with the bayonet and broke the Union centre. Sherman's regiments went up in turn. Colonel Cameron of the 79th New York was mortally wounded. Sherman says disorder spread through his brigade about 15:30.

On the left, Elzey, leading after Kirby Smith fell, drove Howard's tired brigade off Chinn Ridge. Early's three regiments, with Stuart and Beckham's battery, struck the Union right in flank while Beauregard attacked in front. Heintzelman says the retreat began about 16:30. When a wagon overturned on the Cub Run bridge, it became a rout among the carriages of spectators. The Confederates were too disordered to follow far. McDowell's army was back at Washington by 22 July.

### Where the accounts disagree
- **The armies.** The NPS gives McDowell 35,000 against Beauregard's 22,000 and Johnston's 10,000. The American Battlefield Trust, following Salmon and Livermore, gives 28,450 Union and 32,230 Confederate.
- **The losses.** The Trust and the Davis booklet give 2,896 Union (460 killed, 1,124 wounded, 1,312 missing); the Union return, 2,708. The Trust gives 1,982 Confederate; Davis, nearly 2,000.
- **The guns taken.** The NPS trail marker has the 33rd Virginia take two of Griffin's howitzers. Barry says ten guns were lost in all. Jackson claims seven besides the battery Cummings took and lost.
- **Stuart's charge.** Stuart reports about 300 men fit for duty, half sent with Major Swann. Emerging Civil War (2021) puts the charge at about 150 horsemen.
- **The last attack.** Johnston has Kirby Smith arrive about 15:00. Davis puts Howard's attack about 16:00 and Early's between 16:00 and 17:00. The older NPS handbook has the line break about 15:45.
- **The "stone wall".** Bee was mortally wounded and left no report, so his words are uncertain. Burnett Rhett claimed he meant them as a reproach.
- **Judith Henry.** The NPS gives her age as 84, Wikipedia 85. The NPS says artillery fire struck her; the Metropolitan Museum, gunshot wounds.

!!! story "No words can describe"
    Captain James Ricketts was hit in the thigh beside his guns and taken prisoner. In Washington
    his wife Fanny heard only conflicting reports. On 25 July she set out for the Confederate lines,
    and that evening learned at their headquarters that he was alive. Next morning she reached
    Portici, the Lewis house, where the kitchen and hallways had become operating rooms. She nursed
    him and other wounded men there for about a week, and wrote in her journal that "no words can
    describe the horrors around me". When he was sent to prison in Richmond on 2 August, she went
    with him, and stayed until his release in December.
