### The campaign
After 18 June the allies went back to digging. Lord Raglan died of dysentery on 28 June, and James Simpson, his chief of staff, reluctantly took command of the British. On the Russian side Admiral Nakhimov, a leader of the defence, was shot in the head by an allied marksman and died on 10 July.

On 16 August Gorchakov tried to break the siege from outside. He attacked the French and Piedmontese at the Traktir bridge on the Chernaya with some 58,000-60,000 men and was beaten off with the loss of about 8,800. After that the garrison could expect no relief, and the French trenches crept up until they were a few yards from the Malakoff.

The allies bombarded the town from 17 August and again, more heavily, from 5 September. At noon on 8 September the French would storm the Malakoff and the works either side of it. When they were established, the British would storm the Redan, and the French would go in against the town front further west.

### The places
- **The Redan's salient**: the point of the work, chosen by Simpson because it was "less exposed to the heavy flanking fire". The ditch there was about 15 feet deep.
- **The advanced parallel**: the British forward trench, from which the stormers went over. Codrington watched from the fifth parallel.
- **The Malakoff**: the work east of the Redan, taken by the French at noon and held.
- **The Mamelon**: the French-held hill in front of the Malakoff, where Pélissier gathered his storming columns.
- **The Barrack Battery**: Russian guns west of the Redan.
- **The raft bridge and the North Side**: the floating bridge across the harbour by which the garrison left that night.

### The leaders
- **James Simpson** (British, commander): 63. He watched from the trench with his cloak over his head.
- **Sir William Codrington** (British, Light Division): planned the attack with Markham of the 2nd Division.
- **Charles Windham** (British, 2nd Division): led the assaulting column into the Redan. Known after as "Redan Windham".
- **Colonel Unett** (British, 19th): senior officer of Shirley's brigade. Badly wounded before reaching the abatis.
- **Major Augustus Welsford** (British, 97th): led half the ladder party. Killed.
- **Sir Colin Campbell** (British, Highland Division): held the trenches that night, ready to attack again.
- **Aimable Pélissier** (French, commander): watched from the French observatory on the right.
- **Patrice de MacMahon** (French, 1st Division): took the Malakoff.
- **Mikhail Gorchakov** (Russian, commander): withdrew the garrison to the North Side.
- **Colonel Ventsel** (Russian, Vladimirski Regiment): ordered the counter-attack that cleared the Redan.

### The battle
It was a cold day, with a strong wind and dust. At 10:30 the stormers of the Light and 2nd Divisions moved into the advanced parallels. Shortly before noon the French left their trenches in front of the Malakoff, crossed the few yards to it, and got in. The tricolour went up on its parapet. Simpson says this was the British signal.

The covering party went first: 100 men of the Buffs under Captain Lewes and 100 of the 2nd Battalion, Rifle Brigade, under Captain Hammond. Then came the ladders, 160 men of the Buffs under Captain Maude and 160 of the 97th under Welsford. The stormers followed, the Light Division leading. The guns of the Redan swept them with grape as they crossed. The British bombardment had torn up the abatis, and they got through it. But the ladders were too short, and some had been broken or left behind. Only six or seven reached the salient. Men jumped down into the ditch and climbed the parapet.

At first there was little opposition. The Russians fell back behind their traverses and a breastwork, and fired from there. Hammond and Welsford were killed. Two brigadiers, Shirley and Van Straubenzee, were put out of action. Of the senior officers, only Windham and Captains Fyers, Lewes and Maude got into the Redan unhurt.

Windham sent three officers back in turn for reinforcements. All three were wounded on the way. The supports came forward in disorder and crowded into the salient. Then Russian reserves came up from the town and drove the British back over the parapet into the ditch. Simpson says the fight lasted "nearly an hour".

Simpson meant to attack again next morning with the Highlanders under Sir Colin Campbell and the 3rd Division. About 23:00 the Russians began blowing up their magazines. A small party sent forward by Campbell found the Redan empty. The Russians crossed to the North Side by the raft bridge, then took it up, and sank their remaining warships. Campbell's men occupied the Redan at daylight on 9 September.

### Where the accounts disagree
- **The signal.** Simpson says the tricolour on the Malakoff's parapet. The eyewitness in Dickens's *Household Narrative* says four rockets fired from Chapman's attack, the British left, once the flag was seen. The Wikipedia article says the flag of St George raised over the Mamelon.
- **How long it lasted.** Simpson says "nearly an hour", the *Household Narrative* "an hour". The Wikipedia article says Windham held the Redan for about 2 hours. The game gives about an hour and a half.
- **The ground to cross.** The British Empire site, following Collins, says the nearest parallel was nearly 300 yards from the Russian ramparts. The Wikipedia article calls it a 400 m field. The game gives about 200 yards.
- **The stormers.** Simpson gives about 1,000 men, half from each division, besides the 200 covering and 320 with ladders. The *Household Narrative* lists 760 from the 2nd Division alone. The Wikipedia article counts about 11,000 men in the whole right attack.
- **British losses.** The Wikipedia article gives 2,447: 385 killed, 1,886 wounded and 176 missing. Fletcher and Ishchenko give 390 killed, 2,043 wounded and 177 missing.
- **Russian losses.** For the whole day Russell (1865) gives 11,692; Fletcher and Ishchenko, 11,666 (2,684 killed, 7,243 wounded, 1,739 missing).
- **The Russian counter-attack.** The Wikipedia article has Ventsel send in the Selenginski and Iakutski regiments, about 3,000 men. The game has the Kamchatski and Iakutski.
- **Windham.** Simpson says he stayed with the troops throughout. The *Dictionary of Canadian Biography* says he went back himself to ask for men, found the retreat already ordered, and was criticised for it by soldiers, while William Howard Russell of *The Times* wrote that he had "saved the honour of the army".

!!! story "Redan Massy"
    Lieutenant W. G. D. Massy of the 19th was about 17. His captain went down with cholera, and Massy led
    the Grenadier Company in the assault. He was the first of his regiment into the Redan, and few followed
    him. He stood on the rampart, in view of both sides, waiting for the men to come on, until he was hit in
    the leg. The Russians left him where he lay. That night, when they blew up the work, falling masonry
    crushed him. He was brought in the next day, nearly dead, and spent about six months in bed. The army
    knew him after as "Redan Massy". Brigadier Collins (1964) set his account against the *Dictionary of
    National Biography*, which implied that Massy had run.
