### The campaign
In 1854 Britain, France and the Ottoman Empire were at war with Russia. Their aim in the Crimea was Sevastopol, the base of the Russian Black Sea fleet. The allies landed near Eupatoria, north of the town, from 14 September 1854, and beat Menshikov's army on the River Alma on 20 September. They then marched round the town and laid siege to it from the south, the British supplied through the harbour at Balaklava. Meanwhile the Russians, under their engineer Eduard Todleben, dug a line of earthen batteries and took the guns and crews of their ships ashore.

The first bombardment opened on 17 October 1854. The Russian field army then attacked twice and failed: at Balaklava on 25 October, where the Light Brigade lost 247 of its 673 men, and at Inkerman, in fog, on 5 November. Then came winter. A storm on 14 November wrecked about 30 supply ships, the road from Balaklava turned to mud, and by February 1855 the British were down to about 12,000 men fit for duty (National Army Museum).

In May 1855 Pélissier replaced Canrobert in command of the French. On 7 June the French took the Mamelon and the British the Quarries, works in front of the Malakoff and the Great Redan. The fourth bombardment opened on 17 June. Next morning the French were to storm the Malakoff and the British the Redan.

### The places
- **The Great Redan**: the Russian Third Bastion, a V-shaped earthwork pointing south, with a ditch and an abatis, a fence of felled trees, in front.
- **The Malakoff**: the earthwork on the hill east of the Redan, the main French objective.
- **The Quarries**: the Russian works taken on 7 June. Campbell's column formed behind their parapet.
- **The Karabelnaya**: the suburb behind the Redan and the Malakoff.
- **The Lancaster Battery**: on the Victoria Ridge, where Pélissier went to give the signal.
- **The cemetery by Dockyard Creek**: west of the Redan, taken by Eyre's brigade and given up that night.
- **Cathcart's Hill**: the British burial ground, where Sir John Campbell was buried.

### The leaders
- **Lord Raglan** (British, commander): 66. He watched from the trenches, in the line of fire. He died of dysentery on 28 June.
- **Sir John Campbell** (British, left column): had commanded the 4th Division until 7 June, then volunteered to lead its men. Killed.
- **Lacy Yea** (British, right column): colonel of the 7th Royal Fusiliers, whom he had led at the Alma. Killed at the abatis.
- **Colonel Shadforth** (British, 57th): led Campbell's storming party. Killed.
- **Aimable Pélissier** (French, commander): chose to attack at 03:00 without the further bombardment Raglan wanted.
- **General Mayran** (French, right division): went in early and was mortally wounded.
- **Eduard Todleben** (Russian, chief engineer): had the works repaired in the night, and set field guns on the Malakoff.
- **General Khrulev** (Russian): commanded the infantry in the Karabelnaya.

### The battle
The bombardment of 17 June went on through the night, with mortars only. Under it, Kinglake says, the Russians repaired their batteries.

Pélissier was late. Before he reached the Lancaster Battery, General Mayran, on the far right, saw a fuse shine out in the dark and took it for the signal. His leading brigade went in a little before 03:00 against the works east of the Malakoff. It was driven back by grape, musketry and six steamers firing from the harbour, and Mayran was mortally wounded. The divisions of Brunet and d'Autemarre went in late and one at a time. Some got into the Karabelnaya and the Gervais Battery, but no support reached them and they were driven out.

Raglan watched the French checked. He felt it his duty, he wrote, to help them. He sent his two flank columns in without bombarding the Redan first. Each had 100 riflemen in front, 12 engineers, about 170 or 180 sailors and soldiers with ladders and wool-bags, 400 men to storm and 800 in reserve. The signal was a flag waved three times.

Campbell's column had 470 yards to cross. The riflemen and the ladder party went out ahead of the main body, into grape and musketry from the Redan's western face. Lieutenant Gerald Graham took the ladders on with the sailors. The 57th found the trench blocked by soldiers who had slipped away from their camps to join in. They filed out at the far end of the parapet, under fire from the Redan and the Artakoff Battery. Campbell and Shadforth were killed almost at once.

On the other flank, Yea's column met what Raglan called "such a shower of grape" as he had never imagined. The survivors of the covering party reached the abatis, some 80 yards from the work. The engineer A'Court Fisher found it scarcely damaged. Yea came up with his sword drawn and was shot dead. Fisher had the 150 or so men left lie down and wait. No supports came, and he ordered them back.

To the west, Eyre's brigade of about 2,000 men took the cemetery and some houses, about 07:00 by the Russian account, and withdrew that night. Pélissier pulled his men back at 07:00 or a little later, says Kinglake. Kinglake counts 62 officers and 717 men killed or wounded at the Redan, among them 52 of the 120 sailors.

### Where the accounts disagree
- **The French start.** Raglan and Pélissier set the attack for 03:00. Kinglake has Failly's brigade of Mayran's division go in "a little before three o'clock", after Mayran took a fuse for the signal. The Wikipedia article says the French went in nearly an hour early.
- **The ground to cross.** Kinglake gives 400 yards to the Redan's point and 470 to the re-entering angle; the National Army Museum, about 250 yards.
- **The columns.** Raglan's despatch names three columns, the centre aimed at the salient, but only the two flank columns went out. Kinglake gives each about 1,300 bayonets. The Wikipedia article speaks of 10 battalions.
- **British losses.** Behan's *Bulletins* (1856) give 1,433 for the whole day: 155 killed, 1,126 wounded and 152 missing. Airapetov gives 1,728. The National Army Museum says over 1,500. Kinglake's 779 counts only the two Redan columns.
- **Russian losses.** Russell (1865) gives 4,352 for the day, the bombardment and the French attacks included. Airapetov gives more than 4,818.
- **When the French withdrew.** Kinglake says 07:00 or a little later; Pélissier's report, 08:30.
