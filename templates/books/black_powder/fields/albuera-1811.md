### The campaign
In spring 1811 Marshal Beresford, with about 23,000 British and Portuguese, besieged French-held Badajoz on the Guadiana. Marshal Soult, commanding in Andalusia, stripped his garrisons to gather just under 25,000 men to relieve it: the 5th Corps, Latour-Maubourg's cavalry and two borrowed brigades under Godinot and Werlé. He left Seville at midnight on 9-10 May. Spanish patriots passed the news on, and it reached Beresford on 12 May.

Beresford took the 2nd Division and Hamilton's Portuguese to Valverde, covering the roads to Badajoz, and met General Blake, whose Spanish army was coming up to join him. On 15 May, sure that Soult was on the main road, he moved to La Albuera, where the road crosses a low ridge.

That afternoon the allied cavalry under General Long fell back across the Albuera stream too fast, giving up the far bank and its woods, and Beresford gave the cavalry to General Lumley. Blake's divisions arrived about 23:00 and camped in the dark. Cole's 4th Division marched from Badajoz at 02:00 and arrived at 06:30, without Kemmis's brigade, cut off by a rising river. Soult's infantry marched from Santa Marta before dawn. He believed Blake had not yet joined.

### The places
- **Badajoz**: the French-held fortress on the Guadiana; the siege was raised on 15 May and resumed on 19 May.
- **Valverde**: where Beresford waited from 13 May, covering the roads to Badajoz, and met Blake.
- **Santa Marta**: where Long's cavalry was pushed back on 15 May and Soult's infantry camped that night.
- **La Albuera**: a large village on a knoll in front of the ridge, held by Alten's two German battalions.
- **The Albuera stream**: formed just south of the village where the Nogales and Chicapierna brooks meet. In May it was a trickle, fordable almost anywhere.
- **The olive woods**: on the French bank. With a low wooded hill between the brooks, they hid Soult's march round the allied right.
- **The heights**: bare, rolling ground 60 to 150 feet above the water. The main fighting was in a dip about a mile south of the village.
- **Jerumenha**: the crossing Kemmis's brigade had to use, about 30 miles round; it arrived the day after the battle.

### The leaders
- **William Carr Beresford** (Allies, commander): a British general and marshal of the Portuguese army, which he had reformed. He kept his troops hidden behind the crest.
- **Joaquín Blake** (Allies, Spanish commander): about 14,000 men; slow to turn his line to face the flank attack.
- **José de Zayas** (Allies, Spanish division): his four battalions turned to face the French on the heights and held for over an hour.
- **William Stewart** (Allies, 2nd Division): sent Colborne's brigade into the attack before the rest of the division was up.
- **John Colborne** (Allies, 1st Brigade): his brigade was ridden down by the lancers.
- **Daniel Hoghton** (Allies, 2nd Brigade): killed as his brigade stood in line.
- **Lowry Cole** (Allies, 4th Division): advanced on his own decision, urged by Colonel Henry Hardinge of the Portuguese staff.
- **Nicolas Soult** (French, commander): planned the feint at the village and the march round the allied right.
- **Jean-Baptiste Girard** (French, 5th Corps): led the two divisions of the turning column in one deep mass.
- **Victor de Latour-Maubourg** (French, cavalry): sent in the lancers and hussars against Colborne's flank.

### The battle
About 08:00, by one modern account, Godinot's 3,500 men attacked the village, while Werlé's brigade and the dragoons showed at the edge of the woods. Meanwhile Girard's and Gazan's divisions swept round through the olive groves, crossed both brooks and came up onto the heights beyond the allied right. Loy's Spanish horse gave way before their cavalry.

Beresford asked Blake to throw a line back across the heights. Blake sent only four battalions of Zayas's division and one battery. Girard came on in a column as wide as Zayas's line and four times as deep, 8,400 men in all. At about 60 yards the two sides opened fire.

Stewart, told only to support the Spaniards, sent Colborne's brigade straight in, each battalion as it arrived. Their fire, with four guns of Cleeves's battery, struck the French flank, and the French began to give way. Then a squall of rain and hail blew in from the north. In it, about 800 horsemen, Colonel Konopka's 1st Vistula Lancers and the 2nd Hussars, came round the open flank. Oman says three battalions were nearly destroyed within five minutes. Only the 2/31st formed square. Some lancers rode on into Beresford's staff; Beresford parried a lance and threw the man from his horse.

Zayas held. Hoghton's and Abercrombie's brigades took the Spaniards' place, and Gazan's division relieved Girard's. For about half an hour Hoghton's three battalions stood two deep against the 5th Corps at short range. Hoghton was killed. Colonel Inglis of the 57th, hit by grapeshot, lay in front of his men and called on them to "die hard". Neither commander sent in his reserve.

Cole then advanced without orders. Harvey's Portuguese beat off Latour-Maubourg's dragoons with volleys. Myers's three fusilier battalions met Werlé's 5,600 men in deep columns, and for perhaps 20 minutes they fired at 30 or 40 paces until Werlé's men broke. Myers was killed. Abercrombie's brigade wheeled into the flank of the 5th Corps, and the whole French mass went back over the Chicapierna, covered by its cavalry.

Beresford would not storm Soult's guns on the hill between the brooks, and another downpour ended the fighting. Both armies stood on 17 May. Soult left before dawn on 18 May, leaving several hundred badly wounded in a chapel in the wood.

### Where the accounts disagree
- **The armies.** Oman puts the allies at about 35,000 (10,449 British and Germans, 10,000 Portuguese, 14,000 Spaniards) and Soult at about 24,000. British Battles gives 32,000 and 23,000.
- **The losses.** Oman counts 4,159 British, 389 Portuguese and 1,368 Spanish killed, wounded and missing. For the French, Soult reported 2,800, a return of 6 July 5,936, and Belmas and Lapène 7,000; Oman, from the officers hit, reckons about 7,900.
- **Colborne's brigade.** Oman counts 1,248 lost of 1,648 in the three battalions ridden down, and 1,400 of about 2,000 for the brigade. He gives the lancers' loss as 130 of 580; Lieutenant Wojciechowski of the lancers recalled about 200 men and 16 officers.
- **The wounded.** Oman believes British accounts that the lancers speared men on the ground, such as Major Brooke of the 2/48th after he surrendered. The Polish memoirs do not mention it.
- **The colours.** Oman says five of the brigade's six colours were lost. Sorando, using French records, counts six taken, though part of the Buffs' cloth was saved.
- **Stewart's attack.** Beresford said he meant the whole division to form behind Zayas first, and that Stewart refused Colborne's request to put a wing in square. Colborne's own letter says only that the order was not his.
- **Who decided the battle.** Napier credits Hardinge with sending Cole forward, and writes that "the guidance of a commanding mind was nowhere seen". Oman calls Napier's account fine writing but full of errors born of his dislike of Beresford.
- **The times.** British Battles has Godinot's attack at about 08:00. Oman's two battle plans are marked about 10:00 and about 11:30.
