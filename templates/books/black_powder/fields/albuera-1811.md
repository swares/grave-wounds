### The campaign
In the spring of 1811 Marshal Beresford, with about 23,000 British and Portuguese detached by Wellington, laid siege to Badajoz, the French-held fortress on the Guadiana. Marshal Soult, commanding in Andalusia, gathered just under 25,000 men to relieve it. He took most of the 5th Corps, Latour-Maubourg's cavalry and two large borrowed brigades under Godinot and Werlé, and stripped his garrisons to do it. His column left Seville at midnight on 9-10 May. Spanish patriots in the city passed the news on, and it reached Beresford by the afternoon of 12 May.

On 13 May the French columns joined at Fuente Cantos and their cavalry reached Los Santos. Beresford took the 2nd Division and Hamilton's Portuguese to Valverde, where he could cover all three roads to Badajoz, and met General Blake, whose Spanish army was coming up from the south-west. On 15 May, sure that Soult was on the main road, he moved to La Albuera, where the road from Seville crosses a low ridge. The siege stores went back to Elvas.

That afternoon the allied cavalry under General Long fell back across the Albuera stream faster than Beresford wanted, giving up the far bank and its woods. Beresford gave the cavalry to General Lumley that night. Blake's divisions did not arrive until about 23:00 and camped in the dark. Cole's 4th Division left the lines at Badajoz at 02:00 and reached the field at 06:30. Kemmis's brigade was cut off by a rising river and had to go round by Jerumenha. Soult's infantry camped at Santa Marta, marched before dawn, and was still closing up at 07:00 or 08:00. He believed Blake had not yet joined.

### The places
- **Badajoz**: the French-held fortress on the Guadiana; the siege was raised on 15 May and resumed on 19 May.
- **Elvas**: the Portuguese fortress over the border, the allied base, where the siege stores were sent.
- **Valverde**: where Beresford waited on 13-14 May and met Blake.
- **Almendral**: the village about 5 miles off from which Blake's army marched up late on 15 May.
- **Santa Marta**: where Long's cavalry was pushed back on 15 May and Soult's infantry camped that night.
- **La Albuera**: a large village with a tall church tower on a knoll in front of the ridge, held by Alten's two German battalions.
- **The Albuera stream**: formed just south of the village where the Nogales and Chicapierna brooks meet. In May it was a trickle, fordable almost anywhere.
- **The olive woods**: on the French bank. With a low wooded hill between the brooks, they hid Soult's march round the allied right.
- **The heights**: bare, rolling ground on the allied side, between 60 and 150 feet above the water. The main fighting was in a dip about a mile south of the village.
- **Jerumenha**: the river crossing Kemmis's brigade had to use, about 30 miles round. It arrived the day after the battle.

### The leaders
- **William Carr Beresford** (Allies, commander): a British general and marshal of the Portuguese army, which he had reformed. He held the high ground, with his troops hidden behind the crest.
- **Joaquín Blake** (Allies, Spanish commander): about 14,000 men. He was slow to turn his line to face the flank attack.
- **José de Zayas** (Allies, Spanish division): his four battalions turned to face the French on the heights and held for over an hour.
- **William Stewart** (Allies, 2nd Division): sent Colborne's brigade into the attack before the rest of the division was up.
- **John Colborne** (Allies, 1st Brigade, 2nd Division): his brigade was ridden down by the lancers.
- **Daniel Hoghton** (Allies, 2nd Brigade, 2nd Division): killed as his brigade stood in line against the 5th Corps.
- **Lowry Cole** (Allies, 4th Division): advanced on his own decision, urged by Colonel Henry Hardinge of the Portuguese staff.
- **Nicolas Soult** (French, commander): planned a feint at the village and a march round the allied right.
- **Jean-Baptiste Girard** (French, 5th Corps): led the two divisions of the turning column in one deep mass.
- **Victor de Latour-Maubourg** (French, cavalry): sent in the lancers and hussars against Colborne's flank.
- **Jan Konopka** (French, 1st Vistula Lancers): colonel of the Polish regiment that made the charge.

### The battle
Soult's plan went off at first as he meant. About 08:00, by one modern account, Godinot's brigade, 3,500 men, attacked the village with two batteries in support. Werlé's brigade and two brigades of dragoons showed themselves at the edge of the woods as if to attack Blake. Meanwhile Girard's and Gazan's divisions made a wide sweep through the olive groves, crossed both brooks and came up onto the heights beyond the allied right. Four cavalry regiments led them, and Loy's Spanish horse gave way.

Beresford rode to Blake and asked him to throw a line back across the heights. Blake sent only one brigade of Zayas's division, four battalions, with his one battery. Zayas formed on the near side of the dip. Girard came on in a column about as wide as Zayas's line and four times as deep, 8,400 men in all. About 60 yards off, the French stopped and the two lines fired into each other.

Colborne's brigade led the 2nd Division up. Stewart, told only to support the Spaniards, sent it straight in, each battalion as it arrived: the Buffs on the right, then the 2/48th and 2/66th through Zayas's right, with the 2/31st not yet formed. Their fire, and four guns of Cleeves's battery, hit the French flank, and the French began to give way. Then a squall of rain and hail blew in from the north. In it, about 800 horsemen, the 1st Vistula Lancers and the 2nd Hussars, came round the open flank. Oman says three battalions were nearly destroyed within five minutes. Only the 2/31st had time to form square. Some lancers rode on through the rear of Zayas's line and into Beresford and his staff; Beresford parried a lance and threw the man from his horse.

Zayas held. Hoghton's and Abercrombie's brigades came up behind the Spaniards and took their place, while Gazan's division pushed up to relieve Girard's. For about half an hour Hoghton's three battalions, the 29th, 1/48th and 1/57th, with what was left of the 2/31st, stood in line two deep against the 5th Corps at short range. Hoghton was killed. Colonel Inglis of the 57th, hit by grapeshot, lay in front of his men and called on them to "die hard". Soult did not send in his reserve, and Beresford held back the 4th Division.

Cole advanced without orders from Beresford. Harvey's Portuguese brigade, on the right, beat off a charge by Latour-Maubourg's dragoons with volleys. On the left Myers's fusilier brigade, three battalions, met Werlé's 5,600 men in three deep columns. For perhaps 20 minutes they fired into each other at 30 or 40 paces. Then Werlé's columns broke. Myers was killed. At the same time Abercrombie's brigade wheeled into the flank of the 5th Corps, and the whole French mass went back over the Chicapierna. Latour-Maubourg's cavalry covered the retreat.

Beresford brought up the Portuguese but would not storm Soult's guns on the hill between the brooks. Another downpour ended the fighting. Both armies stood on 17 May. Soult left before dawn on 18 May, leaving several hundred badly wounded in a chapel in the Albuera wood. The allies were back before Badajoz on 19 May.

### Where the accounts disagree
- **The armies.** Oman puts the allies at about 35,000: 10,449 British and Germans, about 10,000 Portuguese, 14,000 Spaniards. He gives Soult about 24,000. The British Battles site gives 32,000 allies and 23,000 French.
- **The allied losses.** Oman counts 4,159 British, 389 Portuguese and 1,368 Spanish killed, wounded and missing.
- **The French losses.** Soult reported 2,800 killed and wounded. A return of 6 July gives 5,936. Belmas and Lapène give 7,000. Oman, counting the officers hit by name, arrives at about 7,900.
- **Colborne's brigade.** Oman gives two counts: 58 of 80 officers and 1,190 of 1,568 men in the three battalions ridden down, and 1,400 of about 2,000 for the brigade with the 2/31st. He gives the lancers' loss as 130 of 580. Lieutenant Wojciechowski of the lancers recalled 5 officers killed, 11 wounded and about 200 men down.
- **The wounded.** Oman believes British accounts that the lancers speared men on the ground, and names Major Brooke of the 2/48th, cut down after he had surrendered. The Polish memoirs edited by Jonathan North do not mention it.
- **The colours.** Oman says five of the brigade's six colours were lost. Sorando, using French records, counts six taken, though part of the Buffs' cloth was saved.
- **Who made Stewart attack.** Beresford said he meant the whole division to form behind Zayas first. Beresford's writings have Colborne asking to put a wing in square and Stewart refusing; Colborne's own letter says only that the order was not his.
- **Who decided the battle.** Napier credits Hardinge with sending Cole forward, and writes that "the guidance of a commanding mind was nowhere seen". Oman calls Napier's account fine writing but full of errors driven by his dislike of Beresford.
- **The times.** The British Battles site has Godinot's attack at about 08:00. Oman's two battle plans are marked about 10:00 and about 11:30. Wojciechowski says his regiment crossed the river at sunrise.

    Lieutenant Matthew Latham of the Buffs was near the King's Colour when the lancers came out of
