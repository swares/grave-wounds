!!! story "Deborah Sampson, 1782-1783"
    On 20 May 1782 Deborah Sampson, aged 21, enlisted at Uxbridge, Massachusetts, as "Robert
    Shurtleff", in the light infantry company of the 4th Massachusetts Regiment. In a skirmish
    near Tarrytown, New York, that summer she took a cut to the forehead and a musket ball in
    the thigh. She let a surgeon dress her head and left before he could see to the leg. Her
    own later accounts say she dug one ball out with a penknife and a needle, and that a second
    stayed in her for life; no one else saw it. In the summer of 1783 she fell ill with fever
    in Philadelphia, and the doctor who treated her found her out. She was honourably
    discharged at West Point in October 1783 (the 23rd or the 25th: sources differ).
    Massachusetts paid her withheld wages in 1792, and from 1805 Congress paid her an invalid's
    pension of $4 a month. Her later claim to have fought at Yorktown is false: she enlisted after it.
