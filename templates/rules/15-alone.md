# Playing alone {#alone}

Grave Wounds can be played by one person. The pages take the GM's work that needs no judgement:
the enemy's moves on the field map, its blows on the roller, the march, the weather, sickness and
the camp. What is left is the judgement, and for that you ask the dice. A session can follow a
campaign on the travel map (below), or a whole war a season at a time ([A war, season by
season](#alone-war)).

## How a session runs {#alone-session}

1. **Make a team** in the [team builder](team.html) and send it to the roller, as in
   [Making a team](#team). Pick a battlefield from a period book, or a campaign map on the
   [travel map](travel.html).
2. **Open the [campaign page](campaign.html).** It follows the pages through muster, march, fight,
   aftermath and downtime, and keeps the game's log and save.
3. **March** on the travel map. Give the enemy's side to the commander (**Moved by**, on the
   travel map or under Run the march: [The commander on the march](#alone-march)). Its clock stops
   when your force meets the enemy. Ask the dice anything the map does not say: is the farm
   empty, will the villagers sell bread.
4. **Fight.** A skirmish goes to the roller: turn on **Run the fight**, play your own men, and let
   the GM's script play the other side; **Play to my turn** runs it until it is yours. A battle goes
   to the field map: give the other side to the [enemy commander](#commander), attacking or holding
   as the battlefield says, and move your own units. Your team fights among them as heroes.
   If you use [camp roles](#roles), you hold all four: the council still stops each evening to make
   you look at the rations, the sick and the road, and who rolls is still your choice to live with.
5. **After the fight**, bind the wounded, carry them to camp and roll their recovery, as in
   [Recovery](#recovery). On the roller, **Send the result to the travel map** takes the fight's
   dead, wounded and prisoners back to the forces, and the beaten one falls back; then rest in camp
   (**Rest until tomorrow** on the campaign page) while the wounds mend. This is where a solo
   campaign is won or lost.
6. **Compare** your battle with the one fought, and with the enemy commander. On the field map a
   battlefield laid out with its armies as drawn up shows **Beat history**: how the battle went,
   how often the commander wins each side playing against itself, and, when the battle has ended
   by itself, your result against both. You beat the commander with a better outcome than its
   median run, or the same outcome at a better rate of their men lost for each of yours. Each
   battlefield's section in its period book tells how it really went, and what it cost.

!!! gm "Playing it straight"
    Alone, you are the GM as well as the players, so be a fair one. Ask the dice before you look
    at what the answer would cost you, and take the answer the dice give. Let the commander have
    the enemy's choices, not you.

## The commander on the march {#alone-march}

On the travel map, and under **Run the march** on the campaign page, **Moved by** gives a side to
the commander, attacking or holding. At each press of the clock it orders each of that side's
columns before the clock moves, from what the side has seen: where it saw each enemy force last,
and how big it looked. It never knows where an enemy it has not seen is. For each column, the
first of these that applies:

1. **Its rations are gone:** it marches back to its side's nearest depot.
2. **Exhausted or spent:** it makes a {{cmd_camp}} and rests until morning; exhausted, at most
   {{cmd_rest}} mornings running, then it goes on; spent, until it can march.
3. **Beaten** and fallen back within {{cmd_beaten}} days, or **outnumbered**: what it sees within
   {{cmd_near}} hexes is {{cmd_out}} times its strength or more. It falls back to its nearest depot
   or camp away from them.
4. **Attacking:** it marches on the nearest enemy force it has seen, if it is the stronger;
   otherwise on the nearest objective it has not yet reached, looking for them.
5. **Holding:** it marches to its nearest objective, or stays where it is, and makes a
   {{cmd_camp}} there.

Its baggage marches with its biggest column. When its columns come within reach of an enemy force
and are the stronger, an attacking commander sends the fight itself, as **Fights on offer** would:
the page opens on it. A fight sent, by it or by you, is not sent again until its result is
taken back into the travel map. Holding, or the weaker, it does not attack; a fight is still yours to set up
against it. The sizes are ours.

## A war, season by season {#alone-war}

A soldier's war was mostly marching, quarters, sickness and small fights, with a few named
battles. The [team builder](team.html)'s **A season with the battalion** plays it a season (three
months) at a time. Eight wars are there, from Henry V's in France to Italy in 1943-45. Italy (8
seasons) or the Western Front (18) fit an evening; the Peninsula (24) or Henry V's war (29) a long
one, and the Roses with Edward of March (47) a campaign.

1. **Set the lethality** at the top of any page: Gritty, Standard or Heroic. Under Standard each
   man has one fate point. Spent, it comes back only when he has served a term (four years, about
   sixteen seasons), so a man who spends it early in a war carries on without it. For a war where
   your men can die, choose Gritty.
2. **Make the team.** Pick the era, the region and a team (*company*, *retinue*, *section*...),
   then **Roll the team**. For Henry V's war, take *A retinue indented for France*: it has the
   indentures' men-at-arms (three archers to one), where the lord's retinue fits the Roses.
   **Send to the roller**, and there **Add the team**, so the roller knows your men when their
   wounds come to it.
3. **Open A season with the battalion** on the team builder, and pick the **War** and its first
   **Season**.
4. **Each season:**

    - **The team's posture**, for the season (the table below).
    - **Who serves it**, and where each man stands at its actions: in reserve, in the line, or
      in the thick of it.
    - **Serve the season.** Its log tells the season: sickness week by week, each named action
      and what it did to each man, any deed, the small fight if one came, the followers.
    - **A named action** with its own battlefield (Albuera, Badajoz, Towton...) can be played on
      the field map instead of rolled; one with scenes at its edge (the forlorn hope at Badajoz,
      the raid and the crater before the Somme) offers them first. Men who volunteer and come
      through may be known to their officers, or promised promotion: the forlorn hope roll for
      the rank above at the season's end.
    - **A small fight:** **Play it on the roller**, with **Run the fight** on and your own men to
      move, or **Roll it**. Played, the roller's **After the fight** offers each man's deeds
      (**Mark deeds**), and **Followers back** takes the followers' fates home; rolled, each man
      unhit may still do a deed.
    - **Wounds** wait on the roller: **Roll** each man's wound and apply it. Then the hours
      after: a wound that kills within rounds kills him, one within minutes or hours he may live
      through. A fate point, if he has one, is spent for him to live.
    - **End of season** rolls his ticked skills, ages him, pays him, opens the battalion's
      vacancies and rolls each man's promotion. A deed waives his years in rank and is worth +20;
      a knighting comes only by a deed.

5. **The journal**, under the panel, keeps every season; **Save the journal** keeps the war as a
   text file, the last season included.

The postures:

| Posture | Small fights | Hit at actions | Skill ticks | Promotion | Deeds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Keep their heads down | ×0.5 | ×0.75 | one fewer | −10 | ×0.5 |
| Do their duty | ×1 | ×1 | as ever | as ever | ×1 |
| Volunteer | ×1.5 | ×1.25 | one more | +10, and known to his officers | ×1.5 |

!!! gm "Choosing a posture"
    Nothing decides it for you, and that is the season's choice. A rule some players use: volunteer
    while every man is fit and someone wants rank; do their duty after a man has been sick; keep
    their heads down after one was badly hurt, until he mends. On the Western Front a man is hit
    in most seasons by the line itself, and that rule keeps the team down for most of the war.

## Ask the dice {#ask}

When a question has no rule and no page to answer it, put it as a yes-or-no question, decide how
likely a yes is, and roll d100 as for any test:

{{ask_odds}}

- **At or under:** yes. A **critical** ({{chk_crit}}% of the chance or under) is yes, and more
  than you asked for.
- **Over:** no. A **fumble** ({{chk_fumble}} or more) is no, and worse than you feared.
- **Within {{ask_within}} either side of the number:** the answer comes with a **complication**. Roll d10:

{{ask_complications}}

Ask only what matters, and let the answer stand. Two questions about the same thing are one
question asked twice.

!!! example "The retinue: the barn"
    On the road to Towton the retinue needs shelter for a wet night. Is the barn ahead empty? It is
    Lancastrian country, so Unlikely: 30. The roll is 33, within 5 of the number: no, with a
    complication. The d10 gives 1: men are sleeping in the barn, and one of them has seen the
    retinue's fire.
