# Playing alone {#alone}

Grave Wounds can be played by one person. The pages take the GM's work that needs no judgement:
the enemy's moves on the field map, its blows on the roller, the march, the weather, sickness and
the camp. What is left is the judgement, and for that you ask the dice.

## How a session runs {#alone-session}

1. **Make a team** in the [team builder](team.html) and send it to the roller, as in
   [Making a team](#team). Pick a battlefield from a period book, or a campaign map on the
   [travel map](travel.html).
2. **Open the [campaign page](campaign.html).** It follows the pages through muster, march, fight,
   aftermath and downtime, and keeps the game's log and save.
3. **March** on the travel map. Its clock stops when your force meets the enemy. Ask the dice
   anything the map does not say: is the farm empty, will the villagers sell bread.
4. **Fight.** A skirmish goes to the roller: turn on **Run the fight**, play your own men, and let
   the GM's script play the other side; **Play to my turn** runs it until it is yours. A battle goes
   to the field map: give the other side to the [enemy commander](#commander), attacking or holding
   as the battlefield says, and move your own units. Your team fights among them as heroes.
5. **After the fight**, bind the wounded, carry them to camp and roll their recovery, as in
   [Recovery](#recovery). On the roller, **Send the result to the travel map** takes the fight's
   dead, wounded and prisoners back to the forces, and the beaten one falls back; then rest in camp
   (**Rest until tomorrow** on the campaign page) while the wounds mend. This is where a solo
   campaign is won or lost.
6. **Compare** your battle with the one fought, and with the enemy commander. On the field map a
   battlefield laid out with its armies as drawn up shows **Beat history**: how the battle went,
   how often the commander wins each side playing against itself, and, when the battle has ended
   by itself, your result against both. You beat the commander with a better outcome than its
   median run, or the same outcome at a better rate of their men lost for each of yours. Each
   battlefield's section in its period book tells how it really went, and what it cost.

!!! gm "Playing it straight"
    Alone, you are the GM as well as the players, so be a fair one. Ask the dice before you look
    at what the answer would cost you, and take the answer the dice give. Let the commander have
    the enemy's choices, not you.

## Ask the dice {#ask}

When a question has no rule and no page to answer it, put it as a yes-or-no question, decide how
likely a yes is, and roll d100 as for any test:

| How likely | Roll at or under |
| --- | --- |
| Almost certain | 90 |
| Likely | 70 |
| Even | 50 |
| Unlikely | 30 |
| Hardly possible | 10 |

- **At or under:** yes. A **critical** ({{chk_crit}}% of the chance or under) is yes, and more
  than you asked for.
- **Over:** no. A **fumble** ({{chk_fumble}} or more) is no, and worse than you feared.
- **Within 5 either side of the number:** the answer comes with a **complication**. Roll d10:

| d10 | Complication |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Someone saw: an enemy, a villager or a rival now knows where you are |
| 2 | It costs time: half a day, or an exchange on the field |
| 3 | It costs something: food, ammunition, a horse, a day's pay |
| 4 | A man is hurt doing it: a fall, a kick, a cut (a light wound on the roller) |
| 5 | Someone wants something in return |
| 6 | The weather turns: roll the day's weather again, worse |
| 7 | It splits the team: one man is somewhere else for a day |
| 8 | An old trouble comes back: a debt, a grudge, a wound that opens |
| 9 | A stranger joins you, not entirely welcome |
| 10 | The enemy is nearer than you thought |

Ask only what matters, and let the answer stand. Two questions about the same thing are one
question asked twice.

!!! example "The retinue: the barn"
    On the road to Towton the retinue needs shelter for a wet night. Is the barn ahead empty? It is
    Lancastrian country, so Unlikely: 30. The roll is 33, within 5 of the number: no, with a
    complication. The d10 gives 1: men are sleeping in the barn, and one of them has seen the
    retinue's fire.
