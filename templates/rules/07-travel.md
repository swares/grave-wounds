# The march and the camp {#travel}

Between battles the team marches, camps and sickens. The [travel map](travel.html) moves
forces across a map of hexes, usually 0.6 mile (1 km) across, on one clock for everyone.

## Marching {#marching}

A force marches at its kind's road speed:

{{table:speeds}}

Other ground takes longer; a step from one hex to the next costs the average of the two:

{{table:terrain}}

The march starts {{t_start}} hour after sunrise and stops {{t_stop}} hour before sunset, up to
{{t_hours}} hours a day: a short winter day is a short march. Mud and snow slow it (next
chapter), and a flooded ford cannot be crossed. These speeds are for a small, fit band; a
large army's column made less ground a day.

**The clock.** Every force on a map keeps the same time, and it moves on only when the GM
moves it: an hour, to nightfall, or until something happens. **Orders** say whether a force
marches on its route or halts. When forces of different sides come within {{t_within}} hex of
each other, the clock stops and both halt: the fight can be set up, on the roller's battle
map or the field map, or they can be given new orders.

## On the road {#events}

An army loses men without a battle. Each night every fit man takes his era's chance of
**deserting**, of **falling out** and being lost to the force, and of being **injured** (a fall,
a kick, an accident: he joins the sick and wounded), and every man of a mounted force or a
wagon train of losing his **horse**. The chances depend on what the force did that day: in camp
they are small (Valley Forge lost 3 men in 10,000 a day to desertion); on the march larger; and
**withdrawing** far larger (the retreat to Corunna lost 1 in 100 a day, the British retreat from
Mons nearly 2). A big force simply loses more men. Then there is a chance of an **incident**: a
d100 on the era's table (foragers set on by peasants, a bridge down, a convoy ambushed, a mine
in the road, a checkpoint), some for small parties or wagons only, some costing men or a step
of fatigue, each with a hook for the GM. The rates come from the records where they exist and
are estimates where they do not; the incidents are estimates. At the GM's choice, named
characters take the injury chance too.

A force **short of food** deserts about fifteen times as fast (the starving camp at Nuremberg in
1632) and straggles five times; one whose **pay is in arrears** deserts three times as fast.
The larger applies, not both. A mounted force keeps its count of **horses**: lost horses, and
the horses its deserters ride off on, come off it, and under half a horse a man it marches at
foot pace. A wagon train short of teams slows to three quarters, and under half its teams to
half. The GM adds remounts when the force finds them.

## Loads {#loads}

What a man carries depends on his **order**: fighting order (weapon, ammunition, water), marching
order (with his pack) or full kit (with extra rations and the rest). Each era has its own weights,
from the records where they exist: about 12 kg in fighting order for a Napoleonic redcoat and 27 kg
marching, 27 kg on the Somme in 1916, 43 kg on an approach march in Afghanistan. Armour is added
where the era's orders leave it out (a medieval man-at-arms' harness, 12 to 22 kg). His **load**
as a share of his body weight (his era's average man's; less for a woman), against his Strength
(×0.8 under 30 up to ×1.3 at 75), falls in a band:

{{table:loads}}

**On the march** a force carries its era's marching order unless the GM changes it. The roads'
paces are already those of men in marching order, so a heavier order slows the force (the
band's march against the usual band's) and tires it more, and a lighter order is no faster. Each
named man who marches takes his band's chance of blistered feet (a minor wound on his track): the
full chance for 20 km or more, less for a shorter day, a quarter once his feet have hardened after
a week on the road, and none while they are still sore.

**In a fight** a man's band costs his Dodge (his defence is the better of Parry and Dodge less
that), and Overloaded or worse he cannot run. A green man (one term) fights a band heavier under
fire. Each era fights in its usual order (the British of the Napoleonic wars with their packs on;
most others in fighting order). A side may **drop its packs** before the fight; if it loses the
field, the packs are lost with it, and that night its force has no blankets or rations.

## Camps {#camps}

At the end of the march a force makes camp:

{{table:camps}}

Digging takes the work of the camp's ditches, banks, palisades and stakes, laid out as the
battle map would lay them, divided among the men ({{t_share}} of them work at once; the rest
guard and cook). Camp work can use what is left of the marching day and then {{t_evening}}
evening hours; beyond that it runs on into the next day.

**Surprise.** When a fight is set up from the travel map, the defenders may be caught
unawares: {{s_base}}%, plus the camp's figure above ({{s_none}} more with no camp at all),
+{{s_dark}} in the dark, +{{s_fog}} in fog, plus the attackers' best Stealth ÷ {{s_per}}, less the
defenders' best Watch ÷ {{s_per}}, and never under {{s_lo}}% or over {{s_hi}}%. Surprised men
cannot attack or move in the first round.

## Finding work {#work}

Between fights a man may hire himself out. Where the team is (a town, city, village, camp, the
road, the frontier or at sea) decides which jobs are on offer; he may take any of them that his
career allows and his skill reaches (the least skill to be hired). Pay is in Days for each day
worked, or a sum for the whole contract, a horse or a body.

A job is worked in **terms**: a day, a week ({{w_week}} days), a month ({{w_month}} days) or a
contract of so many days. Each term he makes **one check** on the job's skill. At or under it,
he is paid in full; over it, {{w_fail}} of the pay; {{w_fumble}} or more and over, a fumble,
and nothing. A job with no skill pays in full.

A job with a **risk** takes one d100 a term as well: at or under the risk, trouble. The GM uses
the job's hook, or rolls a d10 on the era's trouble table. Jobs marked † are against the law,
and their trouble is the law's.

**Keep.** A soldier with his army is fed by it. A man who is not (off on his own, on leave, or
working for someone else) pays his **keep** from his purse each day: a day's food
({{keep_food}} for an English archer), and a bed ({{keep_bed}}) where there is one to be had:
in a town, city or village, or at an inn on the road (on the travel map, a night in a village or
town out of camp). A job marked (fed) feeds him while he works:
his army's own service, or wages paid with board. A day he cannot pay for his food he goes
hungry: it counts as a day without fresh food, and tires him a step. A night he cannot pay for
a bed he sleeps rough. The medieval jobs:

## Rank, pay and loot {#rank}

**Rank.** Each era has its ladder of ranks; a man starts at the bottom unless his career puts him
higher (a sergeant, an officer). The medieval ladder, from the Calais muster of 1346 (in truth a
man's place was his contract, and rises like an archer's to man-at-arms were rare):

{{table:ranks}}

**Promotion.** A rank falls **vacant** when a man of it is lost, or when the GM opens one for the
wider company's losses. At the end of a season each vacancy is offered to the team's men one
rank below who have served its years in that rank (or did a **deed** in battle, which waives the
time and adds +{{deed_bonus}}) and, where the army demanded it, can read and write (Letters
{{literate}} or more: British sergeants in the Napoleonic wars, officers in the Civil War). The most
senior rolls Command first, at the rank's difficulty; the first to succeed is promoted, and his
old rank falls vacant in turn. A man can be **reduced** to a lower rank at the GM's word. The roller suggests a deed for a man
who put down two or more enemies, or their leader, in a fight; the GM confirms it.

**Pay.** Each day his force is fed by its army, a man earns his rank's pay, less what the army
stopped for his rations and necessaries, paid into his purse at the end of the season. Days
when his force's pay is **in arrears** are owed instead, until the GM says the arrears are paid.
Horsemen and surgeons may draw more; a sutler draws nothing.

**Loot.** After a won fight (the roller sees when one side alone is still standing, or the GM
names the winner, or the field map says who held the field), the field: each man rolls d100 (a stormed town's sack adds
{{sack_bonus}}, though only a 100 is a windfall):

{{table:finds}}

His era takes its share (the medieval captain a third, the Swedish hospitals a tenth). Where
plunder is forbidden (the Crimea's provost, the Lieber Code of 1863, the Hague rules of 1907),
or past what a man may keep as a trophy, the **provost** rolls his Watch against the man's
Stealth: if the provost wins, the loot is taken from him, and on the provost's critical or the
man's fumble the era's punishment follows (hanging, flogging, a court-martial). In the eras of
prize money each man draws his rank's shares (at Waterloo, a private one share of about 51
days' pay, a captain 35). A medieval or Thirty Years' War prisoner may be ransomed (with leave).

**Honours.** The Plains nations had no ranks and no pay. A man's honours come from coups
counted (the first to strike counts most: the roller counts it when his blow lands in close
combat) and horses taken from an enemy camp; his horses are
his wealth, and giving them away turns wealth into standing.

## Sickness {#sickness}

Before antibiotics, disease emptied armies faster than battle. Once a week every well man
may catch what the camp breeds, at a chance set by its **hygiene** (a bivouac is poor,
quarters good, other camps fair), by staying four weeks or more in one place, by the
ground (marsh, a town) and by the week's weather:

{{table:diseases}}

A case climbs to its peak after it shows, then gets better or worse each night on the
track of [Recovery](#recovery), with rest, a camp and shelter helping, and marching, cold
wet nights and filth hurting. Survivors of typhus and plague cannot catch it again; ague comes
back in marsh country.

- **What it costs.** Men at **{{d_unfit}}** or worse cannot dig, fight or march on their own;
  at **{{d_carried}}** or worse they are carried on litters. A force carrying litters marches at
  {{d_march}}%, or {{d_short}}% with fewer than {{d_bearers}} fit men a litter.
- **Scurvy.** After {{sc_weeks}} weeks without fresh food (fruit, greens, fresh meat, onions),
  each week every well man may fall ill: {{sc_chance}}% the first week, {{sc_per}} more each week
  after, against his Endurance. Fresh food heals it {{sc_heal}} steps a week. A scurvied man's
  old wounds open again.
- **Frostbite.** On a night of {{fb_below_f}} °F ({{fb_below}} °C) or colder, men without
  enough shelter may be frostbitten, a sickness on the same track.

!!! example "The retinue: on the road"
    The retinue marches north on foot: {{t_foot_day}} a day on the road when the days are long,
    less in March, when Towton was fought, and less again in the snow that fell that day. It
    makes a watched camp each night, with a sentry roster and fires; no tools are needed. In
    a fair camp, moving on every day, the flux is the only real danger; were it to sit
    in a filthy siege camp for a month, typhus would come for it.

!!! gm "For the GM"
    The travel map counts all of this for whole forces, and runs each named character's own
    case with his own Endurance when you put him **With** a force from the roller. Your job
    is the choices: how hard to march, where to camp, whether to wait for the sick.
