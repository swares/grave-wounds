# Black-powder guns {#mod-black-powder}

For the wars from the Thirty Years' War to the 1870s: guns that fire round shot, canister and
shell, muzzle-loaders that fail in the wet, and infantry that carry works with the bayonet.

## Guns of the black-powder wars {#guns}

A **battery** is a unit in battery order, {{g_per_hex}} men to a hex, its crews {{g_crew}} to a gun:
as crews fall, fewer guns are served. Each served gun fires a round an exchange at the nearest
enemy in sight, out to {{g_range}} ({{g_rifled}} rifled): **canister** within {{g_canister}},
**round shot** beyond (which ploughs through a deep column and bounds on over firm ground), and
**shell** from howitzers, over anything in the way. Each gun carries {{g_ammo}} rounds; limbering
or unlimbering takes an exchange; a battery that breaks loses its guns. Charged in front, it
fires one round of canister before the melee. The shot in full is in
[Batteries in full](#guns-full).

## Misfires {#misfire}

A muzzle-loader can **misfire**, losing the shot, and more often in the wet. The chance
depends on how it fires its charge: a slow match, a wheel-lock, a flint, or a percussion cap.
Even dry, a flintlock failed about one shot in six or seven in the British trials of 1834, and
a percussion cap about one in 165. A breech-loader's cartridge does not misfire.

## Storming works {#storming}

Attacking, infantry with muskets, rifle-muskets or breech-loaders do not stop to trade fire with
an enemy behind works (a parapet, trench or bank), dug in, or in buildings or rubble. They close
without seeking cover, firing as they go, and charge it, with the same odds. Flintlock riflemen,
archers and machine guns still fight by fire. A wall or palisade that blocks hand-to-hand
fighting stops them at its foot, unless they carry ladders: then they go up it (see
[works](#works)). Breaching it is the GM's order.
