# Quick start {#quick}

Fifteen minutes, one small fight, and you have rolled your first wounds. On the start page press
**Play in 15 minutes**: the pages make the retinue this book follows in its examples and set
them on the road to Towton, 1461, against three Lancastrian billmen. The pages play the
billmen, and a coach above the map says what to press next. Everything below is in the rest of
this book; this page is enough to start.

## The six men {#quick-men}

{{ex.qs_men}}

**Attack** is what a man rolls to hit, **Defence** what he rolls to parry or dodge, and
**Blood** what he can lose before he drops.

## The one roll {#quick-roll}

Roll d100. **Equal to or under** the number succeeds; how far under is the **margin**. In a
fight the margin is how bad the wound is.

## A turn {#quick-turn}

1. **Initiative** decides who acts first. On his turn a man **moves** (a few hexes, and still
   strikes) or **stays**.
2. He **strikes**: d100 against his Attack. The defender rolls against his Defence; equal to or
   under it, the blow is turned.
3. A hit lands on a **place on the body**, rolled on the war's own table of where real wounds
   fell, and the margin makes it **light**, **serious** or **critical**. Armour on that place
   lowers it.
4. The wound **bleeds** and **hurts**. Each round a bleeding man loses Blood; pain and blood
   lost make his rolls worse. At low Blood he is **down**.

## First aid {#quick-aid}

A friend can **Bind** a wound (less bleeding), put a **Tourniquet** on an arm or a leg (the
bleeding stops), or **Cauterise** it (it stops, and hurts more). The side's best Leechcraft
rolls for it. A man bleeding fast and left alone may die; one bound and carried to camp has a
chance.

## When it is over {#quick-over}

A side breaks when enough of its men are down, and the fight ends. Look at each man's card:
where he was hit, how much Blood is left, how long his worst wound could take to kill him.
That is the game: what happens next, in camp, is in [Recovery](#recovery).
