# The campaign in full {#campaign-full}

These rules matter in a long campaign, or when their case comes up in play. The main chapters say
each in brief and link here.

## Tongues in full {#tongues-full}

For [Tongues](#tongues).

**Related tongues.** A tongue he lacks he knows at a share of a related one he has (half of German from Low German,
three quarters of English from Scots). Pidgins rise no higher than {{lg_pidgin}} (Bamboo English
in Korea, GI Vietnamese), the Plains sign language no higher than {{lg_sign}}, and a learned tongue
no higher than {{lg_max}}.

**Phrase books.** From the Crimea on, armies issued phrase books: across tongues, a yes or no, a
number or a pointed direction is then Easy at no worse than {{lg_book}}.

**Passing as a native.** His figure against the listener's Watch (both roll): Ordinary in his
own tongue, Hard fluent without the idiom (76+), Very hard a good speaker (51-75), and below that
he cannot pass. In 1944 Skorzeny found ten men with perfect English and idiom for Operation
Greif, thirty or forty good speakers, and over a hundred moderate ones; those caught in American
uniform were shot.

**Taken and escaping.** A man who asks for quarter in his captors' tongue is a little safer (the
capture roll is 10 better: a surrender had to be accepted to protect him). Escaping, his Fieldcraft
to get home is +10 if he converses in their tongue and +20 if it is his own.

**On the travel map** the people where a force stands speak a tongue, or in mixed country several:
the nearest town, village or landmark within {{lg_place_km}} decides (its own, if it names them),
and everywhere else has the map's. A force with no named man who gets by in any of them forages Hard, its talk is in the best
of them he has, and a month among them ticks their main tongue. The map shows a place's tongues
when you point at it.

**Learning.** A tongue used well is ticked and rolls to rise at End of season like a skill (one at 0
always rises). A month among its speakers on the travel map, or a season held prisoner, ticks it;
so does a week with a language master, a teammate who speaks it well, or (from 1943) an army
course. The army's courses took three months for the better students to order a meal or rent a
room; the Foreign Legion's recruits answered in French within ten days.

**Interpreters.** The team may hire an interpreter for a season: a man of the other army who speaks
the team's tongue and his own at {{int_lo}} to {{int_hi}} (rolled). He costs {{int_wage}} Day a day
({{int_wage_modern}} for the modern wars, where Iraqi interpreters began at $600 a month in 2005). A
man who talks through him uses the interpreter's figure in that tongue, not his own. Of those
hired {{int_unrel}}% are unreliable (a whistleblower said 28% of one firm's Afghan hires failed the
language standard): his roll of {{int_err}} or more is a dangerous error. Each End of season he may
be killed ({{int_hazard}}%, {{int_hazard_modern}}% in the modern wars: in Iraq some fifteen times a US
soldier's rate), and his season is up. On the travel map, where the people speak his tongue and
the team's named men march with the force, he forages and talks for it: the force does not forage
Hard for want of the tongue, and its billeting and talk go through him at his figure.

## Age and wear in full {#age-full}

For [Age and wear](#age).

**Enlisting.** A man enlists at his era's starting age, raised to his army's legal least (15 to
60 under the Statute of Winchester; 17 to 25 for Victorian recruits; 18 to 37 in the American
draft of 1942). Then d100: a few are **boys under age** (about a tenth of the Union army was under
18), enlisted at up to {{en_under}} years below the least, and a few **older recruits**, from
{{en_older}} up to {{en_over}} years past the most (over it, he lied). A man under or over age is
found out at {{en_found}}% each End of season and sent home, until he is of age (a boy) or has
served out his first term (an older man). Most characters are made after their terms, so a boy's
lie matters only in the eras of one-year terms. A recruit who enlisted at {{en_older}} or more
takes {{en_wear}} times the Wear for his service: Union men enlisting at about 39 had 1.8 times the
odds of dying of disease of men of 20 in their first year, and 2.4 after.

A man ages with his terms. For every 4 years of his life past the start of his service (at the
making of a character, the years of his terms; in play, each term served) he rolls his age band's
decline, taken from his characteristics as a lasting hurt is (his skills that rest on them fall
with them), and, from 20, gains a step of maturity: +1 Presence and +1 Nerve until 40, +1 Wits
until 45 (at most +5 each). Each promotion adds +{{pres_rank}} Presence, to +{{pres_cap}} in all (a
design estimate: a man who leads grows into it). Endurance goes first, then Agility, then Strength; Wits slows from
50:

{{table:ageing}}

**Wear.** Service wears a man: +1 Wear for each 4 years, and +1 for each hard season (a month of
hard nights on the travel map, hungry, sick or wounded, or colder and wetter than his shelter; or
the GM's word for a siege, a famine or a tropical station). Each 4 Wear makes him 2 years older
for his decline, and from 6 Wear his Endurance rolls twice and takes the worse.

**Worn out.** For each term he serves from 35, or with 8 Wear or more, he rolls d100: at or under
3 × Wear + 2 × (his age − 35) − (Endurance − 50) ÷ 2, he is worn out (a 1-year term rolls a
quarter of that). From 40, Endurance under 30, or Strength, Agility or Endurance under 25, wears
him out at once. The GM may send him home (with a pension where his army gave one) or keep him
on. British soldiers discharged "worn out" were mostly 41 to 44, after 21 to 25 years.

## Training in full {#training-full}

For [Training](#experience).

**Training.** A skill can also be ticked by a week's teaching: from a master for hire where the
team is (a riding master, a master of defence, a schoolmaster, a surgeon, a craftsman; his skill
counts as 60, and he charges a fee by the week), or from a teammate at least 20 better (who gives
up the week too). The teacher rolls his skill; a success ticks the pupil's skill, a failure wastes
the week. The pupil pays the fee and his keep; a teammate who teaches loses the week from his
next job term. An army drills its men: a week in which a force
its army feeds spent four days or more not marching, the best Command with it (or the army's own
sergeants, 50) rolls, and on a success each named man's weapon skill is ticked. Riding lessons
in London in 1879 cost about six days of a private's pay each; the other fees are estimates.

**Courses.** Some training takes him away for weeks or a year, for set gains: a year as an
apprentice or in a knight's household, two weeks at a Civil War camp of instruction, eighteen
months at Sandhurst, four and a half at a 1916 officer cadet battalion, seventeen weeks at an
American Officer Candidate School (22 in Korea, 23 in Vietnam, 12 today), Ranger School, the
Special Forces course. An army's course counts as service, and an officer's course makes him
eligible for its rank at the next vacancy, whatever his rank and years:

## Strain in full {#strain-full}

For [Strain](#strain).

A light day in the line, a day in the line, a fight or an extreme day is changed by how he
stands with his comrades, summed:

{{table:strain_mods}}

{{st_toll}}% of every point also goes into his **Toll**, which only going home lowers. Each
time Strain and Toll together pass {{st_every}}, 20, 30 and on, he rolls d100: at or under his
Nerve + {{st_base}} − that total, he holds; a man who has broken down before rolls 10 less, and a roll of
{{st_fail_from}} or more always fails. If he fails, he rolls d10, adding 1 for each 10 he failed by: 1-4 **shaken** (−10 to attack and Nerve
until a day's rest), 5-7 **strained** (−20 until treated), 8-9 **broken down** (out of the fight
until treated), 10 or more **severely** (out, and to be sent back). Each era has its own words for
these: nostalgia in the Napoleonic and Civil wars, shell shock in 1914-18, combat exhaustion in
1944.

A day out of the line, camped, takes {{st_rest}} Strain off. A man who breaks down is treated
where he is, near the front or in the rear; the nearer and the sooner, the likelier he goes back
to duty, and treatment takes Strain off him (sent home, all of it, and half his Toll):

{{table:treatment}}

Except treatment with his unit, he is away from the force for those days: the travel map counts
them off, and he comes back to it when they are done. If the team's leader is killed, each of his
men takes the Strain of it ({{st_leader}}), twice that if he was a trusted leader (Command
{{st_trusted}} or more): a design estimate, since no study measured it.

Korea, Vietnam and the later wars rotated men home at the end of a tour: Strain gone, the Toll
cut to a quarter. For the Plains nations, a homecoming that welcomes and honours a man halves it.
A man who ends a campaign with his Toll at or above his Nerve, or who broke down, may carry a
lasting effect: the GM notes it as part of who he is (sleeping badly, starting at noises, drinking,
grief), never as a penalty.

## On the road in full {#events-full}

For [On the road](#events).

An army loses men without a battle. Each night every fit man takes his era's chance of
**deserting**, of **falling out** and being lost to the force, and of being **injured** (a fall,
a kick, an accident: he joins the sick and wounded), and every man of a mounted force or a
wagon train of losing his **horse**. The chances depend on what the force did that day: in camp
they are small (Valley Forge lost 3 men in 10,000 a day to desertion); on the march larger; and
**withdrawing** far larger (the retreat to Corunna lost 1 in 100 a day, the British retreat from
Mons nearly 2). A big force simply loses more men. Then there is a chance of an **incident**: a
d100 on the era's table (foragers set on by peasants, a bridge down, a convoy ambushed, a mine
in the road, a checkpoint), some for small parties or wagons only, some costing men or a step
of fatigue, each with a hook for the GM. The rates come from the records where they exist and
are estimates where they do not; the incidents are estimates. At the GM's choice, named
characters take the injury chance too.

A force **short of food** deserts about fifteen times as fast (the starving camp at Nuremberg in
1632) and straggles five times; one whose **pay is in arrears** deserts three times as fast.
The larger applies, not both. A mounted force keeps its count of **horses**: lost horses, and
the horses its deserters ride off on, come off it, and under half a horse a man it marches at
foot pace. A wagon train short of teams slows to three quarters, and under half its teams to
half. The GM adds remounts when the force finds them.

## Boots and clothing in full {#kit-full}

For [Boots and clothing](#kit).

Each named man's **boots** wear with every day he marches, faster on wet or muddy ground, and his
**clothing and blankets** with every day, twice as fast on a hard night. Lives in days:

{{era:kit_life}}

Worn boots raise his chance of blistered feet (half again; worn out, three times); worn clothing
makes his nights a step colder and wetter (worn out, two), for his recovery and for frostbite.
On a day his force does not march, its best Craft mends the most worn boots or clothing among its
named men (Mend kit: a success takes off half a life's wear, a fumble ruins them). He may buy new from
the price list (boots, shoes, a coat, a blanket), and a force its army feeds is issued new at its
depot. In October 1862 the 48th North Carolina returned 51 of its 619 men barefoot and 194 more
nearly so; at Valley Forge 2,898 men were unfit for duty, "barefoot and otherwise naked".

## Supplies in full {#supplies-full}

For [Supplies](#supplies).

A force carries what it needs between fights: **ammunition** (a share of its full loads),
**rations** (days a man carries), **water** (litres a man) and **medical stores**:

{{era:supplies}}

**Rations.** A day its army does not feed it, and it has not foraged or hunted its food, it eats a
day's rations; with none left it goes hungry (desertion and straggling run higher, and its named
men have a hard night). A Union quartermaster reckoned that of eight days issued, hardly five could
be counted on. **Water.** By a river, lake, ford, marsh, bridge, village or town, or in rain, it
fills up; elsewhere each man needs {{sp_cool}} L on a cool day, {{sp_warm}} L on a warm one, {{sp_hot}} L in
heat and {{sp_vhot}} L in great heat; short of it, the force is thirsty: a step more fatigue and a
hard night. **Medical stores** serve {{sp_cases}} wounded or sick for each hundred men; each new
wounded man draws one and each sick man a seventh a day; with none left its sick and wounded are
cared for a step worse in camp. **Ammunition** goes into a battle on the field map as its share of a
load, and what is left comes back.

It refills everything at its army's **depot** (where it started on the map, or a place marked a
depot of its side, flagged ⚑ on the map: Harfleur and Calais for Henry V, Balaklava and Kamiesh
before Sevastopol, Hagaru-ri and Koto-ri at Chosin), draws from its **baggage train** in the same hex (the train carries
{{sp_train_rations}} days' rations, {{sp_train_ammo}} loads of ammunition and {{sp_train_med}} fills of medical
stores for {{sp_serves}} men, and a larger force takes more of it), and where its army does not feed it
its named men may **buy** a day's rations for every man in a village or town at their army's keep.

## Sickness in full {#sickness-full}

For [Sickness](#sickness).

Before antibiotics, disease emptied armies faster than battle. Once a week every well man
may catch what the camp breeds, at a chance set by its **hygiene** (a bivouac is poor,
quarters good, other camps fair), by staying four weeks or more in one place, by the
ground (marsh, a town) and by the week's weather:

{{table:diseases}}

A case climbs to its peak after it shows, then gets better or worse each night on the
track of [Recovery](#recovery), with rest, a camp and shelter helping, and marching, cold
wet nights and filth hurting. Survivors of typhus and plague cannot catch it again; ague comes
back in marsh country.

- **What it costs.** Men at **{{d_unfit}}** or worse cannot dig, fight or march on their own;
  at **{{d_carried}}** or worse they are carried on litters. A force carrying litters marches at
  {{d_march}}%, or {{d_short}}% with fewer than {{d_bearers}} fit men a litter.
- **Scurvy.** After {{sc_weeks}} weeks without fresh food (fruit, greens, fresh meat, onions),
  each week every well man may fall ill: {{sc_chance}}% the first week, {{sc_per}} more each week
  after, against his Endurance. Fresh food heals it {{sc_heal}} steps a week. A scurvied man's
  old wounds open again.
- **Frostbite.** On a night of {{fb_below_f}} °F ({{fb_below}} °C) or colder, men without
  enough shelter may be frostbitten, a sickness on the same track.
