# Weather, light and works {#weather}

## The day's weather {#days-weather}

Each day on the travel map rolls its weather from the climate's figures for the month: its
highs and lows, its days of rain and fog, its winds. Wet and dry spells last, and a warm or
cold spell carries over. Rain falls as snow when the day's high is {{w_snow_f}} °F
({{w_snow}} °C) or less. The GM can roll again or set any of it by hand. A **sandstorm** is
never rolled: a battlefield names it (73 Easting, 1991) or the GM sets it.

**Mud and snow** build up and wear off day by day. On the travel map they slow the going;
on the battle map, mud or snow at level {{w_deep}} or more costs one more hex of movement
every hex. Heavy rain or a storm floods the fords for {{w_flood}} days, counting that day.

## Light {#light}

In the dark no one can see to aim, or to call fire, past the light's reach, and aimed fire
is harder. A battlefield can change the light as its battle goes on, at sunset or dawn. A flare or an illumination round lights its hexes as by day for a few exchanges,
and night-vision devices of the later wars see further:

{{table:light}}

## Works {#works}

Camps and battlefields have **works**: along a hex's sides (ditches, banks, palisades,
gates, walls) or in a hex (stakes, felled trees, wagons, trenches, bunkers):

{{table:works}}

A palisade, wall, barred gate or wagon must be **breached** to get through: men hack at it
instead of fighting, until it is down. Across a palisade, wall or barred gate only reach-2
weapons strike, from the front rank, and striking up a bank or ditch costs its height.
Horses cannot charge into stakes or felled trees.

On the field map, men on foot the GM marks with **ladders** can escalade a palisade, wall or
barred gate instead: a few men a hex fight from the ladders at {{f_esc_pen}}, the defenders
strike back at them, and once the far side is clear the unit can climb over at a cost of
{{f_esc_cross}} hexes of movement.

!!! gm "For the GM"
    Weather is the cheapest way to change a fight. Rain on the morning of a battle takes the
    archers' edge away and spoils matchlocks; fog lets the attackers close unseen; a gale at
    the archers' backs carries their arrows nearly a third further. Roll it, then let the players
    decide whether to fight in it.
