# Weather, light and works {#weather}

## The day's weather {#days-weather}

Each day on the travel map rolls its weather from the climate's figures for the month: its
highs and lows, its days of rain and fog, its winds. Wet and dry spells last, and a warm or
cold spell carries over. Rain falls as snow when the day's high is {{w_snow_f}} °F
({{w_snow}} °C) or less. The GM can roll again or set any of it by hand. A **sandstorm** is
never rolled: a battlefield names it (73 Easting, 1991) or the GM sets it.

**Mud and snow** build up and wear off day by day. On the travel map they slow the going;
on the battle map, mud or snow at level {{w_deep}} or more costs one more hex of movement
every hex. Heavy rain or a storm floods the fords for {{w_flood}} days, counting that day.

Shooting **with** the wind lengthens the range bands of bows, crossbows, slings and thrown
weapons; shooting **into** it shortens them, and rain or snow in a shooter's face halves how
far he can see to aim. Into a strong wind they also lose {{w_into_strong}}%, into a gale
{{w_into_gale}}%. A muzzle-loader can **misfire**, losing the shot, and more often in the
wet ([Misfires](#misfire), in Black-powder guns).

**Cold.** In {{w_hard_name}} ({{w_hard_f}} °F, {{w_hard}} °C, or colder) machine guns, submachine
guns and assault rifles misfire {{w_hard_jam}}% more, as oil thickens and actions freeze; in
{{w_bitter_name}} ({{w_bitter_f}} °F, {{w_bitter}} °C) {{w_bitter_jam}}% more, and every hex costs
{{w_bitter_move}} more to cross. A battle sent from the travel map takes its cold from the day's low.

## Light and the dark {#light}

In the dark no one can see to aim, or to call fire, past the light's reach, and aimed fire is
harder: on a moonlit night {{n_moon}}, on a dark night {{n_dark}}. The weather's own limit still
applies, and whichever is shorter counts. A battlefield can change the light as its battle goes on,
at sunset or dawn. Night-vision devices of the later wars see further
([Night vision](#night-vision), in Air and night devices):

{{table:light}}

<span id="night-air"></span><span id="dark"></span>The dark also slows a march and turns men round:

{{table:dark}}

- **Losing direction.** Each unit that moved in the dark may lose its way when its side's
  move ends. Its chance is the light's, adjusted by its quality: {{n_lost}}. A lost unit
  ends a point off its line, a hex astray that way (unless that is blocked or touches the
  enemy), and cannot move next exchange, though it can turn.
- **Muzzle flashes.** A unit that fires when the light limits sight to less than
  {{n_flash_under}} shows where it is. Through the next exchange, the hexes it fired from can
  be aimed at, and fire called on, from up to {{n_flash}} away.
- **Flares.** A flare lights the ground within {{n_flare}} for {{n_lasts}} exchanges, and
  illumination rounds from mortars and guns light far more. A lit hex is seen as by day.
- **The enemy commander** knows only what its units see, so at night it waits for the
  enemy to come close or to fire.

!!! gm "For the GM"
    Night evens the odds against a stronger side, and its fire support and aircraft
    count for less in the dark. A defender with fixed lines, flares and a plan for
    illumination can still hold.

## Works {#works}

Camps and battlefields have **works**: along a hex's sides (ditches, banks, palisades,
gates, walls) or in a hex (stakes, felled trees, wagons, trenches, bunkers):

{{table:works}}

A palisade, barred gate or wagon must be **breached** to get through: men hack at it instead
of fighting, until it is down. No axe opens a stone **wall**. On the field map, once the
armies carry demolition charges (from 1914), men ordered to breach blow a gap in it, and a
shell or bomb bursting beside it opens it at the firing kind's knock-out chance, as it knocks
out a bunker (heavy howitzers, tanks and aircraft; not mortars or field guns). Before that,
men go over it only with ladders. Across a palisade, wall or barred gate only reach-2 weapons
strike, from the front rank, and striking up a bank or ditch costs its height. Mounted men
cannot enter stakes, felled trees, wagons, wire, trenches, bunkers or wrecks.

On the field map, men on foot the GM marks with **ladders** can escalade a palisade, wall or
barred gate instead: a few men a hex fight from the ladders at {{f_esc_pen}}, the defenders
strike back at them, and once the far side is clear the unit can climb over at a cost of
{{f_esc_cross}} hexes of movement.

!!! gm "For the GM"
    Weather is the cheapest way to change a fight. Rain on the morning of a battle takes the
    archers' edge away and spoils matchlocks; fog lets the attackers close unseen; a gale at
    the archers' backs carries their arrows nearly a third further. Roll it, then let the players
    decide whether to fight in it.
